Heavy-ion collision papers

Phenomenology

Transport of hard probes in the glasmastrong

Dana Avramescu. One author. arXiv:2610.20291

Summary. This doctoral-thesis submission studies jet and heavy-quark propagation through the pre-equilibrium glasma using real-time lattice classical Yang-Mills fields, classical transport, and a light-front quantum treatment. It finds larger and anisotropic transport coefficients than in the later QGP, a potentially measurable D-Dbar azimuthal decorrelation, and moderate R_AA modifications once nuclear PDFs are included. The gauge-invariance analysis distinguishing kinetic from canonical momentum is a particularly useful methodological component.

Why it matters. Many phenomenological extractions effectively start transport after the glasma. This work supplies a systematic estimate of what that omission can do to heavy-flavor and jet observables and identifies a concrete correlation observable for future tests.

Critical assessment. Strong as a synthesis and calculation program, with explicit control of gauge and classical-versus-quantum issues. The experimental discriminating power is still prospective, and the thesis format plus model choices mean the numerical shifts should be propagated through full event-level comparisons before being used in precision extractions.

Strangeness suppression, the phi meson and Lambda-antLambda spin correlations from exact strangeness conservation at the quark levelstrong

P. Castorina and H. Satz. Two authors. arXiv:2610.20449

Summary. The paper imposes exact strangeness conservation on strange quarks in a causally connected cluster before hadronization, while separating same-pair combinations from other strange-quark pairings. With one multiplicity-dependent cluster parameter it describes kaon, phi, Xi, and Omega production in pp and p-Pb, and links phi suppression to the dilution of Lambda-antLambda spin correlations.

Why it matters. It addresses a real weakness of hadron-level canonical suppression: an unsuppressed phi is difficult to reconcile with data. The unified treatment also makes a nontrivial cross-observable prediction rather than fitting each strange species independently.

Critical assessment. Promising and unusually economical, with a stated five-sigma rejection of two limiting phi-formation pictures within the model comparison. The result is still conditional on the cluster ansatz and the selected data treatment; independent comparisons to modern event generators and differential spin-correlation data are needed before treating the mechanism as established.

Evolution of multihadron fragmentation functions with nonperturbative behaviorstrong

K. Makino and T. C. Rogers. Two authors. arXiv:2610.20448

Summary. This formal QCD paper derives evolution for collinear multihadron fragmentation functions when the hard scale coexists with additional invariant-mass scales associated with internal motion in the multihadron system. It separates the extra-scale dependence from ordinary single-hadron evolution and gives a common treatment of perturbative and genuinely nonperturbative contributions.

Why it matters. Multihadron correlations and hadrochemistry are increasingly used in pp and nuclear collisions, but interpreting them requires keeping several scales distinct. A consistent evolution framework can reduce ambiguities in connecting measured pair observables to partonic structure.

Critical assessment. Technically substantial and directly relevant to the factorization side of heavy-ion observables, but it is not yet a phenomenological prediction for a collision system. Transfer value is high for future analyses; validation against data and concrete nuclear-modified fragmentation examples remain open.

AI/ML use cases in physics

Thermal properties of the rho meson from a machine-learning-improved holographic QCD modelmixed

Jia-Jie Jiang, Han Tang, Danning Li, and Lang Yu. Four authors. arXiv:2610.20348

Method and result. A neural-network-assisted inverse procedure calibrates a soft-wall AdS/QCD dilaton background to lattice-QCD screening-mass data. The calibrated model is then used to compute vector spectral functions and quasi-normal modes, yielding a dissociation estimate near 168 MeV, a growing thermal width, and a transition toward a transport peak.

Likely HEP/NP use case. The transferable idea is physics-integrated calibration: use ML to fit an effective model to lattice constraints, then propagate the calibrated model into spectral or transport observables. Similar workflows could support Bayesian surrogate calibration of heavy-ion response models, provided emulator error and model discrepancy are treated explicitly.

Critical assessment. This is substantive ML use rather than a decorative classifier, and the downstream observables are physically interpretable. However, the inferred dissociation temperature is dominated by the holographic ansatz and by the chosen criterion for vanishing real quasi-normal-mode frequency; the paper does not establish a model-independent QCD result.

Weak or overclaimed papers worth caution

No additional paper from the narrow same-day scan was strong enough to include as a separate caution item. The main caution is internal to the ML/holographic study above: its workflow is interesting, but the numerical temperature should not be read as a direct lattice-QCD extraction.
