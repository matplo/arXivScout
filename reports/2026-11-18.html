AI-Assisted Analysis of CMS Open Data: Prompt J/psi Cross-Section Measurements in pp and Peripheral PbPb Collisions at sqrt(s) = 7 TeV and sqrt(sNN) = 2.76 TeV

Zhipeng Cui, Ziyu Wang, Gerry Bauer, Kai Yi · 2611.22723 · Heavy-ion AI/ML

A supervised coding agent helps reproduce published CMS prompt J/psi cross sections using data selection, two-dimensional fits, custom detector simulation and uncertainty studies. Reanalysis-to-publication ratios average 1.000 with RMS 0.215 in 39 pp bins and 1.158 with RMS 0.225 in six peripheral PbPb intervals.

Critical assessment: A substantive end-to-end benchmark with published references and explicit review checkpoints, directly relevant to collider analysis. The six PbPb intervals are not all independent; comparison uses known results and human input, with incomplete calibrations. No controlled manual-analysis benchmark establishes a speed or accuracy gain, and the retrospective 64-error sample is not an error rate.

Abstract synopsis: The authors investigate human-supervised AI-assisted analysis of CMS Open Data, including event selection, fits, detector simulation and systematic uncertainties. They compare prompt J/psi cross sections in pp and peripheral PbPb collisions with published CMS measurements and document agent failure modes.

Strangeness-to-entropy ratio across the QCD crossover

Artemiy Lysenko, Mark I. Gorenstein, Marek Gazdzicki, Roman Poberezhniuk, Volodymyr Vovchenko · 2611.22986 · Theory

An interacting hadron-gas-to-QGP equation of state relates absolute open-strangeness density to the lattice-accessible net-strangeness susceptibility. The authors infer a lattice-based strangeness-to-entropy maximum of about 0.063 at 220 MeV; their equation-of-state calculation peaks at about 0.067 at 260 MeV.

Critical assessment: A well-motivated bridge between strangeness abundances and lattice thermodynamics, with a useful susceptibility comparison. Absolute strangeness is not directly a partition-function derivative; the near-unity fluctuation factor and inferred peak are model-dependent. Equilibrium at zero chemical potentials does not directly predict final-state yields, which require hadronization, feed-down and acceptance.

Abstract synopsis: At zero conserved-charge chemical potentials, the authors use an equation of state interpolating between an interacting hadron resonance gas and a quark-gluon plasma to study absolute open-strangeness density and entropy. They compare strangeness susceptibility with lattice QCD and estimate the density-to-entropy ratio from lattice susceptibility using a model-motivated fluctuation factor.

strangeness, QCD crossover, lattice QCD, equation of state, susceptibility
