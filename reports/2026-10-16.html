Theory · 2610.20072

Constraining three-nucleon forces in nuclear lattice effective field theory

Serdar Elhatisari, Ulf-G. Meißner, Zhengxue Ren, Shuang Zhang (4 authors)

Summary. This is adjacent rather than directly heavy-ion: it addresses a key nuclear-theory input that underpins reaction and structure calculations. The fit uses a broad calibration set, including finite-volume neutron-alpha information, to reduce the three-nucleon-force parameterization to a minimal form.

Why it matters. Better-controlled three-nucleon interactions can improve the nuclear baseline and initial-state modeling used in lower-energy collision and reaction phenomenology.

Critical assessment. Strong technical contribution if the calibration and regulator/systematic checks hold up in the full paper. Its impact on heavy-ion observables is indirect, and the abstract does not establish that the reduced force improves reaction predictions yet.

Abstract. Short-range three-nucleon-force terms in nuclear lattice effective field theory are decomposed into locally and non-locally smeared components and supplemented by pair contacts coupled to the density of a third nucleon. Coefficients are constrained using binding energies for nuclei with A=3–40 and neutron-alpha finite-volume energies, yielding a minimal three-nucleon force for future precision studies.

Phenomenology · 2610.19392

Strange-quark-number-dependent response to partonic dynamics in baryon-rich Au+Au collisions

Di Zhao, Xun Zhu, Gao-Chan Yong (3 authors)

Summary. This is the clearest heavy-ion paper in the screen. It uses a controlled model comparison to isolate what changes when explicit partonic dynamics are switched on in a baryon-rich transport calculation, and it identifies a strangeness-ordered response that becomes sizeable above roughly 6 GeV. Importantly, the authors do not equate their model-defined response with a direct observable; they translate it into experimentally accessible ratios and flow observables.

Why it matters. It offers a concrete Beam Energy Scan strategy for separating partonic dynamics from a hadron-string baseline, with the strongest leverage in multi-strange hadron production.

Critical assessment. Strong and appropriately cautious at abstract level. The central result is model-dependent and needs scrutiny of parameter variations, hadronization and detector-level feasibility, but the comparison is well posed and the proposed observables are testable.

Abstract. We identify a strange-quark-number-dependent hierarchy in the response to explicit partonic dynamics by comparing PHSD5.2 with its hadron--string limit in baryon-rich Au+Au collisions at √sNN=3.0–7.2 GeV. The hierarchy is strongest in fractional yields at the upper end of the scan, while abundant-hadron flow changes little at lower energies. The proposed discriminants are matched Ξ−/Λ and Ω−/Λ excitation functions together with high-mT proton elliptic flow.

Au+Au · PHSD/HSD · strangeness · BES energies

Phenomenology · 2610.19632

Collective-flow signatures of bubble nuclei in intermediate-energy projectile fragmentation reactions

Ge Ren, Chun-Wang Ma, Xi-Guang Cao, Kai-Xuan Cheng, Jie Pu (5 authors)

Summary. The paper turns a static bubble-nucleus signature into a dynamical proposal: compare nucleon and preformed-alpha flow in projectile fragmentation. The predicted sign change in proton/neutron directed flow and the different response of alpha clusters are interesting handles for future measurements.

Why it matters. It connects exotic density structure to measurable reaction observables rather than relying only on ground-state density plots.

Critical assessment. Mixed-to-promising. The observable design is useful, but the conclusion is tied to a specially initialized ten-alpha configuration and one EQMD framework; sensitivity to initialization, cluster recognition, interaction choices and secondary decay is decisive.

Abstract. The paper studies collective flow in 40Ca+9Be at 140 MeV/u with an extended quantum molecular dynamics model. The projectile is initialized as a relaxed ten-alpha cluster configuration with a bubble-like density, and the isoscalar monopole excitation strength is varied. Fragment yields, directed flow, cluster flow and rapidity slopes change markedly near ε≈0.5.

bubble nuclei · EQMD · collective flow · fragmentation

Phenomenology · 2610.19415

Constraints on pion parton distributions at small x from leading-neutron electroproduction data

C. Yang, P. C. Barry, W. Melnitchouk, N. Sato (4 authors)

Summary. This is not a heavy-ion paper, but it is relevant adjacent QCD phenomenology. The controlled fit variations directly address whether small-x pion-PDF uncertainties and the zero-gluon solution are artifacts of data selection or parameterization.

Why it matters. Pion PDFs and their uncertainties matter for hadronic baselines and nuclear-collision phenomenology, especially where small-x parton structure enters.

Critical assessment. Strong and methodologically useful. The conclusions depend on the treatment of leading-neutron factorization and splitting functions; transfer to heavy-ion observables is indirect.

Abstract. The authors revisit pion PDFs with a global QCD analysis of pion-nucleus Drell-Yan and leading-neutron electroproduction data, varying data coverage, parameterization and positivity constraints while fitting pion-nucleon splitting functions. The enlarged H1+ZEUS leading-neutron data set reduces parameterization dependence and strongly disfavors a vanishing pion gluon PDF.

pion PDFs · small x · global QCD fit · leading neutrons

AI/ML use

AI/ML use · 2610.18880

Do Perturbation Predictors Learn Gene Interactions? Diagnosing Extrapolation in Agentic Screening

Ziyuan Gao (1 author)

Summary. The scientific domain is biology, but the evaluation lesson is directly relevant to HEP and nuclear ML: in compositional OOD settings, a simple physics-motivated baseline can be more informative than model confidence, and disagreement should be calibrated against held-out truth. The paper’s agentic-screening use case is secondary to this diagnostic result.

Why it matters. The same pattern appears in event-level extrapolation, detector conditions, reaction systems and model-to-data transfer: novelty must be defined relative to known components, and uncertainty signals need empirical validation.

Critical assessment. Transferable evaluation idea, not a physics result. The evidence is credible within three biological data sets, but no HEP/NP benchmark is provided, so this should guide validation design rather than be treated as a ready-made method.

Abstract. The paper studies unseen gene pairs for which both individual genes appeared during training. It compares neural predictors with an additive baseline and defines additive disagreement as a signal of interaction extrapolation risk. Across eight methods and three Perturb-seq data sets, the specialized predictors do not consistently beat the additive baseline, while larger disagreement tracks larger error.

OOD evaluation · uncertainty · compositional extrapolation · active screening
