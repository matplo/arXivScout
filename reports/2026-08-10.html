Heavy-Ion Collision Papers

Theory

Laboratory-frame T-matrix and heavy quark drag in the quark-gluon plasma

strong

Authors: Anurag Tiwari, Min He. Total authors: 2. arXiv: 2608.06731.

Summary: This is the most directly useful heavy-ion theory paper in the screened window. It addresses a concrete systematic in nonperturbative heavy-quark transport by solving the in-medium T-matrix in the medium rest frame rather than relying on a two-particle center-of-mass construction.

Why it matters: Heavy-flavor diffusion is a main extraction channel for QGP transport properties, and a 25-40% correction to low-momentum drag is large enough to matter for charm/bottom phenomenology and Bayesian inference.

Critical assessment: Strong. The problem is sharply posed, the symmetry reduction is practical, and the observable-level impact is quantified. The next test is propagation into full heavy-flavor RAA and vn calculations against data.

Abstract evidence: The paper develops a laboratory-frame in-medium two-body T-matrix framework and finds large corrections relative to center-of-mass-frame approximations.

Quantum simulation of bottomonium dynamics in the quark-gluon plasma via the Lindblad equation

mixed

Authors: Linyuan Wei, Jin Hu, Anping Huang, Yunpeng Liu, Baoyi Chen. Total authors: 5. arXiv: 2608.06754.

Summary: The paper maps an isotropic next-to-leading-order Lindblad equation for bottomonium in the QGP onto qubits and simulates Upsilon(1S) survival probability. It also reports a single-ancilla circuit optimization.

Why it matters: Bottomonium survival is a precision hot-medium probe, and real-time nonunitary dynamics are hard enough that quantum algorithms are worth monitoring.

Critical assessment: Mixed but worthwhile. The algorithmic contribution is interesting, but the physics is demonstrated in a reduced setup and does not yet replace phenomenological transport comparisons to LHC data.

Phenomenology

Reconstructability and directed flow of short-lived resonances in Au+Au collisions at 19.6 and 200 GeV

mixed

Authors: Junyi Han, Xialei Jiang, Hongcan Li, Yaping Wang. Total authors: 4. arXiv: 2608.06444.

Summary: Using UrQMD, the paper studies rho0, K*0, and Lambda(1520) reconstructability and directed flow in Au+Au at 19.6 and 200 GeV. Reconstructability is mainly controlled by resonance lifetime, while mid-central resonance flow can differ from stable-hadron flow.

Why it matters: Short-lived resonances probe rescattering and regeneration in the hadronic stage between hydrodynamics and final observables.

Critical assessment: Mixed. The observable question is well motivated, but the result is model-specific and needs comparison to STAR/BES resonance measurements before it becomes a robust constraint.

q qbar scattering phase shift in the pi0 channel and pi0 meson spectral function under external magnetic field and finite meson momentum

mixed

Authors: Min Zhou, Zhiyang Liu, Yvming Tian, Chonglong Xie, Guoyun Shao, Shijun Mao. Total authors: 6. arXiv: 2608.06788.

Summary: This NJL-model study computes pi0-channel phase shifts and spectral functions under external magnetic field and finite meson momentum, identifying bound, resonant, non-Breit-Wigner, and anisotropic structures.

Why it matters: Magnetic-field-modified spectral functions can inform discussions of chiral restoration, dileptons, and anomalous transport if modeling assumptions remain visible.

Critical assessment: Mixed. The calculation is detailed, but model dependence and the lack of direct observable connection limit immediate phenomenological weight.

AI/ML use

Extraction of Pion Unpolarized Quark and Gluon Generalized Parton Distributions using Deep Neural-Networks

mixed

Authors: Satyajit Puhan, Shubham Sharma, Narinder Kumar. Total authors: 3. arXiv: 2608.07085.

Summary: A physics-informed neural-network parameterization extracts pion quark and gluon GPDs from PDFs, electromagnetic form-factor data, gravitational form-factor constraints, and lattice-QCD results, with PDF replica ensembles used for uncertainty bands.

Likely use case: The workflow transfers naturally to nuclear PDFs, fragmentation, and heavy-ion initial-state inference where flexible parameterizations must obey physics constraints.

Critical assessment: Mixed-positive. The ingredients are sensible, but the abstract does not make clear how strongly the neural component is benchmarked against conventional fits or how robust the regularization choices are.

Millisecond-Scale Neural Operator Surrogates for Double-Null Free-Boundary Grad-Shafranov Equilibria

strong

Author: Plamen G. Krastev. Total authors: 1. arXiv: 2608.05555.

Summary: A geometrically conditioned Fourier Neural Operator trained on FreeGS double-null equilibria reaches 0.05% mean relative L2 error, X-point recovery within 0.2 cm, FreeGS-level residual diagnostics, and millisecond inference.

Likely use case: This is a credible template for HEP/nuclear fast constrained surrogates: hydrodynamic emulators, transport-model scans, detector field maps, and control-oriented simulation loops.

Critical assessment: Strong but domain-limited. The fixed machine geometry and prescribed topology are real constraints; transfer requires equally disciplined domain restriction and residual checks.

Unsupervised Adaptation of PDE Foundation Models

mixed

Authors: Ziye Song, Zhao Wei, Xin Yu, Ivor Tsang, Yueming Lyu. Total authors: 5. arXiv: 2608.07053.

Summary: The method pretrains a neighborhood-attention Transformer on diverse PDEs and adapts to unseen PDEs without ground-truth solutions using residuals, boundary conditions, LoRA, and a Newton-Schulz orthogonalized variant.

Likely use case: Heavy-ion hydrodynamics and transport often face expensive simulator labels; residual-based adaptation could help if the governing equations and closures are reliable.

Critical assessment: Mixed. Transfer to event-by-event heavy-ion pipelines is nontrivial because stochastic initial conditions, uncertain closures, and imperfect residual definitions are central rather than peripheral.

Alternating Levenberg-Marquardt Training of Physics-Informed Neural Networks with Fourier-Enhanced Features

mixed

Authors: Yulun Wu, Matthieu Barreau, Miguel Aguiar, Karl H. Johansson. Total authors: 4. arXiv: 2608.05892.

Summary: FALM-PINN decouples Fourier-enhanced representation learning from coefficient fitting via a Levenberg-Marquardt nonlinear least-squares solve, reporting up to two orders of magnitude lower relative L2 error on high-frequency and nonlinear PDE benchmarks.

Likely use case: Potential transfer targets include hydrodynamic residual solvers, reduced-order transport equations, and detector-field PDE problems.

Critical assessment: Mixed. The benchmarks look encouraging, but noisy data, discontinuities, shocks, and uncertain physics remain the stress tests that matter for HEP use.

AI/ML Use Cases in Physics

The most relevant AI/ML papers are arXiv:2608.05555, arXiv:2608.07085, arXiv:2608.07053, and arXiv:2608.05892. Their credible transfer value lies in constrained surrogate modeling, uncertainty-aware nonparametric inference, residual-based PDE adaptation, and more stable PINN optimization. None should be treated as plug-and-play for heavy-ion workflows without domain-specific closure, uncertainty, and out-of-distribution validation.

Weak or Overclaimed Papers Worth Caution

Probing dark matter through charged Higgs pair production at future multi-TeV muon colliders: A machine-learning analysis

caution

Authors: Khiem Hong Phan, Quang Hoang-Minh Pham. Total authors: 2. arXiv: 2608.06957.

This is a plausible collider-phenomenology exercise, but the abstract presents ML mainly as a sensitivity enhancer over cut-based selections without enough visible information about robustness, systematic uncertainties, or transferability. It should not be treated as a strong physics-AI contribution from the abstract alone.

Quantum Computers will constrain the Equation of State of Neutron Stars

caution

Authors: Adrian Castano-Garcia, Nahia J. Dios-Bilbao, J. J. Galvez-Viruet, Felipe J. Llanes-Estrada, Mario Logrosan-Alvarez, Nicolas M. Arenaza, Maria Gomez-Rocha. Total authors: 7. arXiv: 2608.06515.

The long-term motivation is legitimate, but the title overstates present capability. A three-to-four-particle finite-density QCD demonstration is far from constraining neutron-star observables, so this should be read as algorithmic groundwork rather than a near-term EoS result.
