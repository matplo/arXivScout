Heavy-Ion Collision Papers

Theory

Color Coherence and the Soft Structure of QCD Jets in Vacuum and the QGP

strong

Paul Caucal, Yacine Mehtar-Tani (2 authors) | arXiv:2608.25984 | Theory

A substantial theory paper developing a factorized all-order language for soft jet evolution in dense QCD matter. It formulates near-threshold jet observables with Wilson-line correlators obeying BMS evolution, with medium-induced energy loss and decoherence entering as boundary data rather than changing the ultraviolet evolution.

Why it matters: If this survives detailed phenomenological implementation, it could sharpen high-pT jet and jet-substructure calculations at RHIC and the LHC by putting color coherence, vacuum radiation, and medium response in a common perturbative structure.

Critical assessment: Scientifically strong and conceptually important, with careful scale-separation logic. The main limitation is that realistic medium profiles, finite-Nc effects, and uncertainty propagation remain future work.

Chiral soliton lattice in inhomogeneous magnetic fields

mixed

Tomas Brauner, Ramkumar Radhakrishnan (2 authors) | arXiv:2608.27319 | Theory

The authors ask whether QCD chiral soliton lattice states persist when magnetic fields are spatially nonuniform, as motivated by heavy-ion collisions and neutron stars. They obtain analytic control in the massless-pion limit and numerical examples away from it, finding that field bending reduces but does not necessarily remove CSL-like ordering.

Why it matters: Heavy-ion magnetic fields are short-lived and structured, so this is a necessary step beyond uniform-field toy models.

Critical assessment: Solid within low-energy EFT, but transfer to real heavy-ion collisions is still tentative because time dependence, conductivity, backreaction, and non-equilibrium evolution are not central.

Phenomenology

Jet Quenching Meets Gluon Saturation

strong

Paul Caucal, Kevin Eisenberg, Yacine Mehtar-Tani (3 authors) | arXiv:2608.25983 | Phenomenology

A compact companion letter translating the longer formal development into a phenomenological bridge between jet quenching and saturation physics. The jet function obeys BMS evolution, while medium-induced energy loss and color decoherence are encoded in the initial condition; a proof-of-principle jet nuclear modification factor illustrates the interplay.

Why it matters: This is the more immediately readable entry point for heavy-ion phenomenologists interested in connecting color coherence, saturation concepts, and jet suppression observables.

Critical assessment: Strong as a conceptual and calculational letter, but not yet a precision phenomenology paper. Its quantitative payoff depends on full evolution with realistic boundary conditions.

Particle production in p-O collisions at LHC energy

mixed

Chuan Li, Georg Wolschin (2 authors) | arXiv:2608.25501 | Phenomenology

This short update compares earlier charged-hadron production predictions for p-O at sqrt(sNN)=9.618 TeV with preliminary ALICE Run 3 data and retunes a three-source relativistic diffusion model. It predicts a centrality-dependent inversion of the larger-pseudorapidity production amplitude.

Why it matters: p-O provides important control data for nuclear initial conditions and small-system collectivity claims at the LHC.

Critical assessment: Useful as a baseline/model-comparison contribution, but parameter retuning limits the standalone scientific force until finalized ALICE measurements and broader generator comparisons arrive.

AI/ML Use Cases in Physics

Learning Transverse Momentum Distributions from Raw Scattering Events via Conditional Diffusion

strong

Jitao Xu, Christopher Cocuzza, Kevin Braga, Daniel Lersch, Nobuo Sato, Yaohang Li (6 authors) | arXiv:2608.27077 | AI/ML use

A conditional diffusion model maps raw simulated SIDIS event kinematics to TMD PDFs, avoiding fixed functional forms. On CLAS12-like simulated data, uncertainty bands narrow with event statistics and remain informative even around 1,000 conditioning events.

Likely HEP/heavy-ion use case: A template for simulation-conditioned posterior generation from variable-size event sets, especially sparse kinematic bins, unfolding, and emulator-assisted inference in HEP/nuclear workflows.

Critical assessment: Strong and transferable, with the right caveat: validation is currently simulated, leading-order, and detector-simplified. Real data, NLO theory, polarized observables, and detector effects are decisive next tests.

Cross-simulator transfer with foundation model summaries: Towards robust SKA-era reionization inference

strong

Yannic Pietschke, Caroline Heneka, Ayodele Ore, Romain Meriot (4 authors) | arXiv:2608.26354 | AI/ML use

Although astrophysics-focused, this paper attacks a shared problem: simulation-trained summaries often fail under model mismatch. A self-supervised ViT/JEPA-style encoder is pretrained on inexpensive simulations, frozen, and used for inference on a different radiative-transfer simulator, with reported calibration gains under simulator shift and realistic SKA noise.

Likely HEP/heavy-ion use case: A benchmark pattern for cross-generator SBI: pretrain summaries on cheap approximate simulations, freeze them, and test calibration on expensive or higher-fidelity simulators.

Critical assessment: Strong transfer value because the evaluation directly stresses simulator shift and calibration. Domain distance means it is a workflow template rather than a plug-in collider solution.

Evaluating Quantum Kernel Methods for Track-Based Classification in High-Energy Physics

mixed

Emmanuel Billias, Nikos Chrisochoides (2 authors) | arXiv:2608.27333 | AI/ML use

The paper scales an exact quantum-kernel SVM workflow to 100k training and 400k test track-classification events using CLAS12 drift-chamber features and HPC kernel evaluation. Ideal simulation performs well, while a calibrated noise model substantially reduces AUC and significance improvement.

Likely HEP/heavy-ion use case: Use mainly as a benchmark design and cautionary scaling/noise study for QML in reconstruction, not as evidence that quantum kernels are ready for online HEP use.

Critical assessment: Mixed but worth reading. The noisy study is much smaller and simulated; hardware execution, fake-rate control, latency, and stronger classical deep baselines remain unresolved.
