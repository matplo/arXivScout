Measurements of inclusive J/ψ production in Au+Au collisions at √sNN = 14.6, 17.3, 19.6, and 27 GeV with the STAR experiment

Experiment · The STAR Collaboration (individual author count not verified) · arXiv:2611.25487

STAR measures inclusive midrapidity J/ψ in Au+Au at four energies. RAA is about 0.5 in 0–20% central collisions, with hints of centrality and transverse-momentum dependence; central RAA shows no significant energy variation up to 200 GeV within uncertainties.

Abstract summary: STAR measures inclusive J/ψ production at midrapidity in Au+Au collisions at 14.6, 17.3, 19.6 and 27 GeV, using RAA relative to interpolated pp references and comparing trends with transport models containing hot and cold nuclear matter effects.

Assessment: Strong and directly relevant experimental measurement with systematic variations and an RCP cross-check. The pp baseline is interpolated, total yield systematic uncertainties vary from 6.5% to 20.6%, and correlated global uncertainties matter. The trends are not a unique identification of QGP suppression; transport models show some deviations.

Inclusive and diffractive D meson photoproduction in pPb collisions at the LHC using Pythia8 framework

Phenomenology · Victor P. Goncalves, Luana Santana, Wolfgang Schäfer · arXiv:2611.25933

Pythia8 calculations of inclusive and diffractive charm photoproduction are compared with H1 ep measurements and extended to ultraperipheral pPb. The predicted diffractive fraction exceeds 15% in selected low-pT and forward-rapidity regions.

Abstract summary: The authors compare predictions using different proton and Pomeron PDFs to H1 inclusive and diffractive D* data, then predict inclusive and diffractive D0 rapidity and transverse-momentum distributions in pPb collisions.

Assessment: A useful, testable UPC feasibility study with an external HERA comparison. The pPb predictions are model- and PDF-dependent, and a detectable diffractive component or PDF constraint is not established without detector-level background and uncertainty studies. Who Moved My Z Boson? Revealing Resonance Features Inside a Neural Network

AI/ML in physics · Kevin Alberto Urquía Calderón, Midori Kato, Inar Timiryasov, Oleg Ruchayskiy · arXiv:2611.26291

The authors probe a flow-matching model trained on ATLAS open data, constructing gradient-based steering directions that alter the Z peak and reveal resonance-related features. Ablation broadens the Z peak while much of the remaining invariant-mass spectrum stays approximately unchanged.

Abstract summary: The paper identifies features associated with Z, J/ψ and Υ resonances in an unlabeled collider event generator, and uses a gradient-derived steering direction to shift the generated Z mass between approximately 80 and 105 GeV.

Assessment: A substantive intervention on real-data-trained models and physically measurable observables, with additional network tests. It is an interpretability proof of concept, not evidence that steered events reproduce a physically consistent alternative theory or calibrated systematic variations; heavy-ion transfer needs high-multiplicity and correlated-observable validation.
