Heavy-Ion Collision Papers

Experiment

First measurement of the ratio of psi(2S)-to-J/psi inclusive production in pAr and pp collisions at sqrt(sNN)=113 GeV with SMOG2

strong

LHCb collaboration: R. Aaij, M. Abdelfatah, A.S.W. Abdelmotteleb, C. Abellan Beteta, F. Abudinen, et al. (1191 authors) | arXiv:2608.30534

Summary: LHCb reports a fixed-target measurement of the psi(2S)-to-J/psi production cross-section ratio in pAr and pp at sqrt(sNN)=113 GeV using SMOG2 gas targets. The double ratio pAr/pp is 0.90 +/- 0.04 +/- 0.02 over -2.3 < y* < 0.0 and 0 < pT < 8 GeV/c, indicating measurable nuclear effects in the pAr system.

Why it matters: The measurement directly supports interpretation of quarkonium suppression and nuclear-matter effects in future LHCb fixed-target and larger-system data.

Critical assessment: Strong because it is a first measurement in a constrained kinematic regime with a clean pp reference from the same fixed-target program. The abstract-level caveat is that detailed systematics, feed-down treatment, and model comparison need inspection before over-interpreting the mechanism.

Abstract: A measurement of the psi(2S)-to-J/psi production cross-section ratio is performed in proton-argon and proton-proton collisions in fixed-target mode at sqrt(sNN)=113 GeV. The ratio is measured versus transverse momentum and rapidity, and the pAr-over-pp double ratio indicates the emergence of nuclear effects.

Phenomenology

Small-x TMD distributions: JIMWLK evolution and the Gaussian truncation

strong

Florian Cougoulic, Piotr Korcyl | arXiv:2608.30687

Summary: The paper evolves small-x TMD distributions with JIMWLK dynamics from McLerran-Venugopalan initial conditions and compares the result with Gaussian truncation of the Balitsky hierarchy. It finds that Gaussian truncation, though good at the initial condition, fails for many evolved distributions.

Why it matters: Heavy-ion and EIC phenomenology often leans on controlled approximations in the CGC/saturation regime; this work identifies where those approximations may break down for phenomenologically relevant observables.

Critical assessment: Strong technical contribution. Its value is methodological rather than immediately data-facing, but the observable-by-observable failure analysis is exactly the kind of approximation audit the field needs.

Abstract: The authors study energy evolution for three quark-gluon and seven gluon-gluon small-x TMD distributions using JIMWLK evolution at fixed Nc=3, compare to Gaussian truncation, and show that the truncation often fails after evolution.

Medium effect on spin alignment of strange and charm vector mesons

mixed

Ruixiang Chen, Hiwa A. Ahmed, Yidian Chen, Mei Huang | arXiv:2608.30930

Summary: This study uses a four-flavor soft-wall holographic model at finite temperature, baryon chemical potential, and angular velocity to compute spin-resolved spectral functions for K*, phi, D*, Ds*, and J/psi. It predicts flavor-separated behavior in rho00, with charm vector mesons above 1/3 at low pT and strange vector mesons below 1/3.

Why it matters: Vector-meson spin alignment is a persistent heavy-ion puzzle, and the paper gives an equilibrium in-medium baseline across flavor sectors.

Critical assessment: Interesting and relevant, but mixed: holographic spectral-function modeling plus instantaneous freeze-out can clarify mechanisms, yet it is not a full dynamical description. Treat the qualitative trends as baseline physics, not a settled explanation of the data.

Abstract: The authors determine spin alignment from medium-induced splitting of spin-resolved vector-current spectral functions and report qualitative agreement with low- and intermediate-pT trends, while finding weak sensitivity to baryon chemical potential and angular velocity.

Production of the phi and Omega via recombination of jet parton showers in relativistic heavy ion collisions

mixed

Kyong Chol Han, Sungtae Cho, Su Houng Lee | arXiv:2608.30891

Summary: The paper studies phi(1020) and Omega(1672) production at sqrt(sNN)=5.02 TeV using both a coalescence model and a hybrid shower-thermal recombination plus remnant string-fragmentation framework. It uses blast-wave thermal partons and Q-PYTHIA/HIJING shower inputs, reporting good agreement with measurements.

Why it matters: Strange hadron production is a sensitive hadronization probe, especially in the intermediate-pT region where thermal recombination and jet fragments can overlap.

Critical assessment: Worth reading but mixed. Agreement with data is encouraging, yet the framework combines several phenomenological components, so the discriminating power depends on whether parameter choices and uncertainties are constrained tightly.

Abstract: Two complementary approaches are used for phi and Omega production in heavy-ion collisions, including a hybrid recombination/string-fragmentation setup; both are said to reproduce experimental measurements and illuminate strangeness components.

Quantum Simulation of Markovian and Non-Markovian Open Quantum Dynamics in Heavy-Ion Collisions

mixed

Doojin Kim, Balbeer Singh | arXiv:2608.31173

Summary: The authors propose a quantum-computing framework for Markovian and non-Markovian open quantum systems relevant to heavy-ion probes. The non-Markovian case is represented through an auxiliary two-level pseudomode carrying memory and reproducing the target kernel after tracing out that mode.

Why it matters: Open-system dynamics are central for heavy quarks, quarkonia, and jet-medium interactions, and efficient quantum simulation could eventually help where classical methods are expensive.

Critical assessment: Conceptually useful, but currently mixed: it establishes a framework and feasibility argument rather than a validated heavy-ion calculation. The practical value depends on scaling, noise tolerance, and whether realistic kernels and observables can be handled.

Abstract: The paper introduces a pseudomode-based quantum circuit construction for non-Markovian open-system evolution and shows convergence to the Markovian limit under relevant time-scale hierarchy assumptions.

High energy thermal photons from chirally imbalanced QGP

mixed

Sourav Duari, Nilanjan Chaudhuri, Pradip Roy, Sourav Sarkar | arXiv:2608.30264

Summary: This paper computes thermal photon emission from a chirally asymmetric QGP, combining a new hard contribution with prior soft HTL/Braaten-Pisarski treatment and demonstrating cutoff independence. It finds that chiral imbalance enhances the high-energy thermal photon rate.

Why it matters: Thermal photons are among the few penetrating probes of early QGP conditions, and chiral imbalance links electromagnetic emission to anomalous transport physics.

Critical assessment: Relevant but incremental from the abstract. The calculation may be useful as a rate ingredient, but phenomenological strength will depend on embedding in realistic space-time evolution and confronting photon spectra/flow constraints.

Abstract: The authors compute hard thermal photon production from chiral quark scattering and combine it with a previous soft contribution, finding an overall enhancement when chiral imbalance is present.

AI/ML Use Cases In Physics

Bias-Corrected Machine-Learning Estimation of Chiral Condensate Cumulants: A Retrospective Lattice QCD Case Study

strong

Benjamin J. Choi, Hiroshi Ohno, Akio Tomiya | arXiv:2608.30416

Method and result: The paper tests supervised ML estimators for traces of inverse Dirac operators on a fixed lattice-QCD dataset, with explicit bias correction and downstream checks on chiral-condensate cumulants and multi-ensemble reweighting. In the studied setup, bias-corrected estimates track the full-data reference while uncorrected estimates can drift after nonlinear cumulant/reweighting steps; nominal solve-count accounting suggests reducing inversion cost to about 25.75% of the conventional calculation.

Likely use case for HEP/nuclear physics: Cost-controlled surrogate estimation for expensive lattice observables, with the same bias-correction logic transferable to emulator-assisted heavy-ion parameter inference and expensive detector/calibration estimators.

Critical assessment: Strong by physics-AI standards because it targets a concrete computational bottleneck and explicitly audits bias propagation. The authors are appropriately cautious that the cost number is a projection, not an end-to-end benchmark including training and analysis overhead.

Abstract: The work studies bias-corrected ML estimates of Tr M^-n for n=1-4, evaluates derived chiral-condensate cumulants and reweighting applications, and emphasizes the risks of uncorrected estimates in nonlinear downstream analyses.
