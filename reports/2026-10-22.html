Daily arXiv Physics Scout

22 October 2026

Heavy-ion collision physics and transferable AI/ML methods, selected for scientific strength and practical relevance.

Executive takeaways

  • The strongest heavy-ion paper predicts a large, experimentally accessible femtoscopic discriminator of oxygen-versus-neon nuclear geometry, supported by two ab initio structure inputs and a hybrid dynamical calculation.
  • The strongest AI/ML paper performs a controlled distillation study and finds that extremely small Lorentz-equivariant jet taggers can preserve high benchmark performance at sharply reduced inference cost.
  • The D0 response-scaling analysis is a useful cross-system empirical benchmark, but its extracted charm attenuation factors lack the statistical machinery needed for a quantitative transport inference.
  • The nuclear photon-TMD proposal is mathematically neat and unusually candid about its assumptions; realistic rates, detector effects, and factorization tests are still required.
  • A baryon-stopping analysis is worth flagging for caution: the advertised sharp Reynolds-number crossover is highly dependent on its phenomenological construction and assigned uncertainties.

Heavy-ion collision papers

Theory

Theorystrong

Nuclear Geometry-Driven Femtoscopic Correlations in Light-Ion Collisions

Dániel Kincses, Bithika Karmakar, Iurii Karpenko, Boris Tomášik

Summary. The authors propose azimuthally sensitive pion femtoscopy as a coordinate-space discriminator of nuclear geometry in the new O+O and Ne+Ne systems. The predicted deformation signal is much larger than the corresponding elliptic-flow ratio and survives a meaningful early-time-dynamics variation.

Why it matters. It gives the LHC light-ion program a concrete, experimentally accessible observable that can cross-check nuclear-structure information inferred from flow.

Critical assessment. Strong. The calculation is high-statistics, uses two independent ab initio configuration sets and a spherical control, and tests one important model variation. Remaining risk is model dependence beyond the tested free-streaming switch, including bulk-parameter calibration and final-state interaction treatment.

Abstract (condensed)

A hybrid calculation combining ab initio oxygen and neon configurations, TRENTo initial conditions, viscous hydrodynamics, and UrQMD predicts substantially enhanced azimuthal femtoscopic eccentricity ratios for deformed nuclei. The Ne+Ne/O+O freeze-out-eccentricity ratio reaches about 1.5–1.8 in central events and remains stable when pre-equilibrium free streaming is removed.

Experimentmixed

Heavy-Quark Dynamics from D0-Meson Anisotropy-Response Scaling Violations

Roy A. Lacey

Summary. This is a cross-system reanalysis rather than a new measurement. Its useful idea is a null test against a light-flavor scaling curve, intended to isolate charm-specific attenuation and a transition momentum between collective and quenching regimes.

Why it matters. A compact scaling benchmark could be useful for testing heavy-quark transport models across harmonics and collision systems.

Critical assessment. Mixed. The cross-system synthesis is interesting, but the paper does not present a conventional global fit, goodness-of-fit measure, or transparent propagated uncertainty on the extracted scaling parameters; some light- and charm-hadron inputs also use nonidentical centrality intervals. Treat the numerical k_c values as an empirical parametrization, not a transport-coefficient determination.

Abstract (condensed)

Published D0-meson v2 and v3 measurements in Pb+Pb, p+Pb, and Au+Au are transformed relative to a light-meson response reference. A charm-specific attenuation factor is introduced to restore the common scaling, with reported system- and centrality-dependent values.

Phenomenologymixed

Direct Access to Nuclear Photon TMDs with In Situ Probes in Ultraperipheral Heavy-Ion Collisions

Cong Li

Summary. The central result is an elegant local inversion formula connecting radiative e+e−γ production to nuclear photon distributions. The paper carefully derives acceptance, binning, Sudakov, and migration conditions under which the ratio retains that interpretation.

Why it matters. If viable, it would provide a new way to probe coherent nuclear electromagnetic fields in momentum space.

Critical assessment. Interesting but not yet demonstrated. The extraction depends on scalar factorization, neglects finite-size Coulomb corrections at the present order, and supplies no realistic rate or detector study. The author states these limitations; experimental usefulness remains an open feasibility question.

Abstract (condensed)

The paper proposes extracting a nuclear photon occupation number from a ratio of radiative lepton-pair cross sections in ultraperipheral collisions. Under scalar statistical factorization, the occupation factor multiplies a complete tree-level reference, while finite bins and detector migration turn the inversion into a weighted average.

AI/ML use cases in physics

AI/ML usestrong

How Far Can Foundation Models Be Distilled for Efficient Jet Tagging?

Jay Agarwal, Siddharth Khare, Dhruv Kumar

Summary. This is an unusually systematic collider-ML efficiency study: teacher strength, student architecture and size, predictive agreement, inherited errors, operations, GPU throughput, memory, and CPU latency are all examined. The main empirical message is that strong teachers help broadly, while foundation-model pretraining is not itself the decisive ingredient once teacher performance is controlled.

Why it matters. Compact high-quality taggers are directly relevant to trigger, reconstruction, and large-scale analysis constraints in HEP and could transfer to heavy-ion jet classification after domain-specific retraining.

Critical assessment. Strong within its benchmark. The controlled teacher–student matrix, three-run uncertainties, inference measurements, and released artifacts are real strengths. External validity is still limited by a single simulated binary top-tagging dataset and one distillation setup; detector/domain-shift robustness is not established.

Abstract (condensed)

Six teachers spanning architectures, symmetries, and pretraining are distilled into five compact students on a top-tagging benchmark. A 20k-parameter L-GATr-slim student exceeds published from-scratch taggers in the authors’ comparison, while a 2k-parameter model matches ParT; code, weights, and hardware-oriented cost measurements are released.

AI/ML usemixed

Lorentz-Equivariant Geometric Algebra Transformers for Top-Quark Event Reconstruction

E. Abasov, L. Dudko, A. Markina, M. Perfilov, P. Volkov, G. Vorotnikov, A. Zaborenko

Summary. The paper tests one symmetry-aware representation across three genuinely different collider tasks rather than presenting only a single classification benchmark. The universal reconstruction setup jointly handles regression, identity classification, decay assignment, and derived observables.

Why it matters. The representation could transfer to heavy-ion event and jet reconstruction where four-vectors, object types, missing information, and symmetry constraints coexist.

Critical assessment. Promising but mixed. Breadth and released implementation are positives, and the authors report three-seed variation for the FCNC comparison. Gains are task-dependent, some comparisons use different information content, and the study does not yet establish robustness to detector mismodeling or real data.

Abstract (condensed)

Lorentz-equivariant geometric-algebra transformers are evaluated on tW/ttbar discrimination, FCNC classification, and universal event reconstruction. They outperform or match transformer baselines on parts of the program, but a handcrafted high-level MLP remains best for the FCNC task.

Weak or overclaimed papers worth caution

Cautioncaution

Multi-Energy Analysis of Baryon Stopping: Extracting Non-Equilibrium Dynamics via Stochastic Rapidity Diffusion

Dhananjaya Thakur

Summary. The multi-energy compilation is relevant, but the transport interpretation is much less direct than the language suggests.

Why it matters. Baryon stopping is central to high-baryon-density programs, so an apparently sharp transport excitation function is easy to overinterpret.

Critical assessment. Caution. Several downstream uncertainties are assigned as fixed percentages, the momentum-diffusion input carries an assumed 20% error, and the derived Reynolds number depends on the chosen blast-wave and linear-drift construction. Claims that a diffusion-dominated regime is 'completely reached' and that higher-energy data would only inflate uncertainty are substantially stronger than the demonstrated evidence.

Abstract (condensed)

A blast-wave interpolation of net-proton spectra is combined with an Ornstein–Uhlenbeck-style rapidity diffusion model to infer friction, diffusion, relaxation, and a longitudinal Reynolds number over a wide beam-energy range. The paper reports a roughly 163-fold fall in that Reynolds number.
