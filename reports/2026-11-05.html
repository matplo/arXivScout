Quarkonium spin transport, small-x EFT, and symmetry-aware physics ML

The selected literature centers on spin-dependent open-system transport, saturation-scale effective theory, lattice-constrained quasiparticles, light-nucleus coalescence, Lorentz-equivariant inference, shortcut-resistant evaluation, and dimensionless equation discovery.

Executive takeaways

  • Heavy-quarkonium spin transport is the day’s strongest heavy-ion advance. The pNRQCD open-system derivation is substantial; its bottomonium and charmonium numerics are explicitly exploratory and should not be read as precision predictions.
  • The proposed Goldstone extension of the CGC is conceptually important but untested. It introduces a condensate-depletion timescale and “color superfluid” regime, yet no quantitative small-x or nuclear-collision observable is calculated.
  • The lattice-to-quasiparticle map is consistent but largely reconstructive. It supplies a compact pure-glue equation-of-state representation, without propagated lattice uncertainties or direct full-QCD applicability.
  • EIC antinuclei offer a useful universality test for coalescence. PYTHIA-plus-afterburner predictions are experimentally targeted, though short-proceedings scope and unquantified model systematics limit confidence.
  • The AI/ML standout is the Lorentz-equivariant spin/CP analysis. It beats established hadronic-top analyzers, calibrates its probabilistic output, and explicitly separates scalar and pseudoscalar information.
  • Evaluation discipline is the transferable ML theme. Group-constrained splitting and shortcut controls from astronomy are immediately relevant to detector and event-image studies; dimensionless learning is promising but has not yet earned its broad “law discovery” language.

Heavy-ion collision papers

Theory

Heavy quarkonium spin transport in a hot medium

strong

Nora Brambilla, Shuo Fang, Tom Magorsch, Panayiotis Panayiotou · 4 authors · arXiv:2611.06707

Summary. The paper builds a systematic pNRQCD transport framework connecting in-medium quarkonium spin dynamics to dilepton angular distributions. Relative quarkonium-medium motion supplies the leading anisotropy at zero fluid-gradient order, with chromomagnetic and spin-orbit terms entering the first nonzero alignment kernel.

Why it matters. This is a substantive step toward putting quarkonium spin alignment on the same controlled open-system footing as suppression and regeneration, and it identifies which recoil and spin interactions survive at leading nonzero order.

Critical assessment. The formal development is the strongest heavy-ion paper today: long, explicit, and clear about scale assumptions. The phenomenology is not yet precision-ready: it uses leading-order correlators, simplified temperature and freeze-out treatments, fixed relative velocity, no realistic production-spectrum weight, and a naive charmonium rescaling. Treat the ~10^-5 bottomonium alignment as an illustrative scale, not a prediction for direct comparison.

Abstract

An open-quantum-system treatment based on potential nonrelativistic QCD derives a spin-dependent quantum master equation and corresponding semiclassical Boltzmann equations for heavy quarkonium, including dissociation, regeneration, recoil corrections, spin-chromomagnetic interactions, and spin-orbit effects. An exploratory rate-equation estimate finds a very small medium-induced Υ(1S) polarization, while a rescaled charmonium calculation is only qualitatively descriptive.

Goldstone theory of gluon saturation in high energy QCD: From color glass to color superfluid condensate

mixed

V. P. Nair, Raju Venugopalan · 2 authors · arXiv:2611.05498

Summary. A conceptually ambitious EFT extension of the CGC introduces colored Goldstone modes and a new dynamical timescale associated with condensate depletion into collinear gluons.

Why it matters. If the construction is robust, it changes the usual static-shockwave picture at low frequency and could affect small-x DIS and initial-state dynamics in ultrarelativistic nuclear collisions.

Critical assessment. The symmetry and EFT construction is serious and potentially important, but the phenomenological status is early. No quantitative observable or comparison to existing small-x data is supplied, and the physical meaning of global-color symmetry breaking and gauge-invariant colored excitations will draw close scrutiny. Worth reading as a theory proposal, not yet as established saturation phenomenology.

Abstract

The authors generalize the Color Glass Condensate by treating spontaneous breaking of a global color subgroup and construct a colored-Goldstone effective theory with decay constant f_G = sqrt(N) Q_S. Coupling these modes to collinear gluons depletes the condensate and motivates a longer-time 'color superfluid' description, with low-frequency probes proposed as sensitive to the new dynamics.

Phenomenology

From lattice QCD thermodynamics to an effective quasiparticle description of gluonic matter at finite temperature

mixed

Cheng-Gang Guo, La-Yuan Peng, Zhen-Yan Lu, Marco Ruggieri, Shu-Peng Wang, Zhi-Jun Ma · 6 authors · arXiv:2611.06128

Summary. The work maps lattice pure-glue thermodynamics onto an effective quasigluon spectrum and a consistency-restoring background contribution, reproducing several equation-of-state observables once the entropy-derived mass is fixed.

Why it matters. Such parametrizations can be useful ingredients for transport or kinetic modeling when a compact quasiparticle representation of the lattice equation of state is needed.

Critical assessment. Technically coherent and admirably explicit that the fitted quantity is not a physical gluon pole mass. The scientific gain is nevertheless modest: the mass is inferred from the same lattice thermodynamics later reconstructed, no uncertainty propagation is presented, and pure Yang-Mills thermodynamics is not quantitatively the QCD medium produced in heavy-ion collisions.

Abstract

A thermodynamically consistent quasiparticle model for pure SU(3) Yang-Mills matter extracts a temperature-dependent effective gluon mass from lattice entropy density over 0.70 ≤ T/Tc ≤ 5. With one pressure normalization, it reconstructs pressure, energy density, interaction measure, sound speed, and a background term, emphasizing strong nonconformality near Tc.

Light (anti)nuclei production via coalescence in electron-proton collisions: from HERA to EIC

mixed

Niccolò Ciavarelli, Francesco Noferini, Francesca Bellini · 3 authors · arXiv:2611.06752

Summary. The proceedings contribution transports a wave-function-overlap coalescence model from pp and HERA ep validation to concrete EIC deuteron and antideuteron predictions.

Why it matters. ep provides a cleaner environment for testing how universal coalescence modeling is across collision systems, with direct relevance to a central heavy-ion hadronization question.

Critical assessment. Useful and timely, with cross-system data checks and an experimentally targeted prediction. Confidence should remain moderate: this is a short proceedings paper, the source and deuteron radii are tune parameters, model and generator systematics are not quantified, and the claimed proton-structure sensitivity is prospective rather than demonstrated.

Abstract

A coalescence afterburner coupled to PYTHIA 8 is tuned and compared with ALICE pp and ZEUS ep measurements, then used to predict deuteron and antideuteron yields in the ePIC acceptance. The calculation finds sizable yields and a matter-antimatter asymmetry toward the proton-going direction.

AI/ML use cases in physics

Top Spin and CP from Equivariant Transformers

strong

Henning Bahl, Marco Menen, Antoine Petitjean, Tilman Plehn · 4 authors · arXiv:2611.05463

Method and result. The architecture encodes Lorentz and CP structure instead of treating symmetry as an afterthought, producing a calibrated hadronic-top analyzer and learned scalar/pseudoscalar observables.

Likely HEP/NP use case. Directly useful for precision LHC spin and CP analyses. For heavy-ion or nuclear applications, the transferable idea is symmetry-typed network outputs for parity-odd, polarization, and angular-correlation observables rather than the specific top tagger.

Critical assessment. The strongest AI/ML paper in this batch. It reports calibrated outputs, meaningful analytic baselines, detector-level simulation for top pairs, and an interpretable connection to likelihood-ratio observables. Remaining gaps are the usual simulation-to-data and systematic-uncertainty tests; the WBF demonstration is only parton level.

Abstract

A Lorentz-equivariant transformer is used to identify the down-type quark in hadronic top decays and to construct interpretable CP-sensitive observables. The reported detector-level spin-analyzing power is 0.721, rising to 0.935 for the most confident 20% of events; a pseudoscalar channel enables CP-odd optimal observables in weak-boson-fusion Higgs production and top-pair production.

Object-independent evaluation of small-data astronomical image classifiers: architecture, representation, and acquisition shortcuts

strong

Eraldo Pereira Marinho · 1 author · arXiv:2611.06484

Method and result. The paper shows that respectable held-out accuracy can still be driven by acquisition settings, catalogue context, or repeated-object leakage rather than the target morphology.

Likely HEP/NP use case. Highly transferable to HEP/NP imaging and detector ML: split by physical event, run acquisition/run-period and masked-region controls, use cluster bootstrap intervals, and avoid attributing performance to architecture when pretraining differs.

Critical assessment. Methodologically strong despite the small domain dataset. It uses grouped splitting, three seeds, multiple controls, multiple-comparison correction, and cluster bootstrap uncertainty. Transfer is about evaluation design, not the astronomy classifier itself; the limited sample size leaves broad intervals and does not isolate architecture from pretraining.

Abstract

A 370-object astronomical image dataset is split by physical object and used to compare a custom CNN-Transformer, pretrained ResNet-50, and pretrained ViT-B/16 across field-of-view and masking controls. Architecture differences are not statistically significant after Holm correction, while metadata-only and central-masked controls retain substantial classification power.

Discovering like a scientist: Literature-informed dimensionless learning for physical closure of data-driven equations

mixed

Hao Xu, Tianhao Wu, Dongxiao Zhang · 3 authors · arXiv:2611.05780

Method and result. The method combines a literature-derived prior over dimensionless groups with exact unit constraints and equation discovery to model condition-dependent closure coefficients.

Likely HEP/NP use case. Potentially useful for discovering compact scaling variables in hydrodynamic response, jet-medium coupling, detector calibration, or reduced transport models where data span beam energies, system sizes, and operating conditions.

Critical assessment. Promising because the inductive bias is physical, outputs are interpretable, and testing extends to held-out experimental conditions. The headline language is broader than the evidence: seven curated problems do not establish general law discovery, corpus leakage and comparison to exhaustive dimensional-analysis searches deserve close inspection, and no HEP/NP transfer is demonstrated.

Abstract

The π-former learns variable associations and exponents from 1,288 established dimensionless groups under exact dimensional constraints, then optimizes combinations against observations. Across seven physical problems it recovers predictive dimensionless coordinates; an evaporation-driven wetting experiment shows held-out generalization across liquids and initial volumes.

Weak or overclaimed papers worth caution

A Duality Model in Which Dual QCD Becomes Hydrodynamics

caution

Noriaki Aibara, So Katagiri, Yoshiki Matsuoka, Kazuya Mitsutani, Akio Sugamoto · 5 authors · arXiv:2611.06503

Why caution is warranted. The paper derives a formal model but supplies no comparison to lattice observables, heavy-ion data, viscosities, saturation evolution, or controlled QCD limits. Several displayed relations and coefficients are explicitly schematic, new octet scalars are introduced without phenomenological constraints, and classical zero viscosity is not an explanation of measured near-perfect fluidity. Treat it as speculative model-building, not evidence for a third quantitative method for QCD.

Abstract (condensed)

An old duality construction rewrites a QCD-like action using adjoint two-form fields interpreted as a fluid, adds two color-octet scalars and vortex strings, and proposes a strong-coupling expansion. The paper suggests this framework could explain perfect-fluid QGP behavior, gluon saturation, confinement, and supplement perturbative and lattice QCD.
