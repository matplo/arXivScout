Sampling nucleon short-range correlations in coordinate and momentum space for Au+Au collision simulations

Theory · Yu-Jing Huang, Qian-Ru Lin, Long-Gang Pang · arXiv:2611.29946

At 2.4 GeV, a correlated nuclear initialization changes charged-pion and kaon yields. Controlled variations indicate that the high-momentum single-nucleon distribution drives the pion change, while a refitted mean field can counteract it.

Assessment: Strong diagnostic with controlled ingredient tests and data-guided recalibration. The compensation is model- and calibration-dependent, not an isolated measurement of short-range correlations; the initial submission predates the strict 24-hour window.

Abstract summary

A correlated initialization combining spatial and momentum structure, proton-neutron pair assignments and high-momentum components is implemented in SMASH. Tests separate contributions to pion production and investigate compensation by the mean field and kaon production.

Revealing Nuclear Shape through Identified-Hadron Flow Decorrelations

Phenomenology · Hadi Mehrabpour, Abhisek Saha, Li Yan · arXiv:2611.30429

Hydrodynamics plus hadronic transport predicts that crossing points of pion-kaon and pion-proton flow-factorization ratios respond differently to quadrupole deformation and triaxiality in U+U and Ne+Ne.

Assessment: A specific, testable observable with comparisons across species and systems, but it is a model prediction, not a nuclear-shape measurement. Detector acceptance, statistical reach, nonflow, reference selection, and sensitivity to evolution assumptions require validation.

Abstract summary

The authors propose identified-hadron flow decorrelations and their crossing coordinates as complementary probes of nuclear shape, studying deformation and collision-energy dependence using event-by-event hydrodynamics and hadronic transport.

Lorentz symmetry in self-supervised representation learning

AI/ML use · Andreas Hermansen, Tobias Golling, Slava Voloshynovskiy · arXiv:2611.31011

LeJEPA self-supervised pretraining on 100 million simulated JetClass jets is combined with a Lorentz-equivariant transformer and compared with several representation-learning baselines. The authors report stronger probe and downstream performance.

Assessment: Substantive baselines, ablations and downstream tasks make this a credible HEP method study. Heavy-ion jet transfer is speculative: underlying-event contamination, detector response and domain shifts require dedicated validation; the paper was submitted about 25 hours before the run.

Abstract summary

The study combines symmetry-aware architectures or physics-motivated augmentations with self-supervised jet representation learning, comparing pretrained models against other objectives and training from scratch on jet benchmarks.
