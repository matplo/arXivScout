Daily arXiv Physics Scout · 11 November 2026

Heavy-ion physics and transferable AI/ML

Heavy-flavor flow fluctuations, resistive QGP magnetohydrodynamics, quarkonium–underlying-event correlations, hit-level collider reconstruction, and adjoint-guided inverse modeling define the selected literature.

Executive takeaways

  • M-SHIVA is the day's strongest heavy-ion infrastructure paper: it combines serious numerical validation with a clear negative conclusion that Joule heating is negligible in the tested smooth, inviscid collision setups.
  • D-meson v3 and v4 look more discriminating than RAA for event-by-event QGP fluctuations; the v3 comparison to CMS data and v4 prediction make the paper immediately testable.
  • The ATLAS Upsilon–underlying-event measurement supplies a useful small-system hard-soft correlation, but the state-dependent multiplicity trend is not uniquely diagnostic of a QGP.
  • AlphaHits is the most directly relevant ML paper, scaling hit-level tracking to 150,000-hit events and reusing representations across reconstruction tasks; real-detector robustness and latency remain open.
  • AdjointFlow is strong scientific ML with a credible transfer route to physics inverse problems, but no HEP or nuclear application has yet been demonstrated.

Heavy-ion collision papers

Theory

M-SHIVA 1.0: A Relativistic Resistive MHD Code for Heavy-Ion Collisions, with Applications to Momentum Anisotropy and Joule Heating in QGP

strong

Krishanu Sengupta, Victor Roy · 2 authors · arXiv:2611.13899

resistive MHDelectromagnetic fieldsJoule heatingQGP

Summary. A serious numerical foundation for finite-conductivity heavy-ion MHD, with a quantitatively negative result for Joule heating in the tested systems.

Abstract. M-SHIVA is a 2+1-dimensional relativistic resistive-MHD solver in Cartesian and boost-invariant Milne coordinates, using IMEX time integration, complementary primitive recovery, and GLM divergence cleaning. It passes standard shock, blast-wave, and rotor benchmarks. In optical-Glauber Au+Au and Pb+Pb applications, Joule heating rises sharply toward lower collision energy but remains negligible relative to longitudinal expansion cooling, with ratios from roughly 10^-14-10^-11 at 2.76 TeV to 10^-7-10^-4 at 62.4 GeV.

Why it matters. Electromagnetic-field effects are often discussed qualitatively; a benchmarked open computational framework can turn those claims into controlled calculations.

Critical assessment. The numerical validation and transparent scale comparison make this one of the day's strongest papers. Its physical conclusion is narrower than the title may suggest because the present version is inviscid, 2+1D, boost invariant, and uses smooth optical-Glauber profiles and prescribed spectator fields.

Experiment

Correlations between Upsilon(nS) production and the underlying event in pp collisions with the ATLAS detector

mixed

Baidyanath Sahoo on behalf of the ATLAS Collaboration · 1 author · arXiv:2611.13643

small systemsUpsilonunderlying eventATLAS

Summary. A useful hard-soft correlation measurement for small-system baselines, with a state-dependent multiplicity pattern.

Abstract. Using 139 fb^-1 of 13 TeV pp data, ATLAS measures charged-particle activity associated with Upsilon(1S), Upsilon(2S), and Upsilon(3S), differential in quarkonium transverse momentum. At low pT, the associated multiplicity is systematically lower for the excited states than for Upsilon(1S), and the difference decreases with increasing pT.

Why it matters. The measurement constrains how hard quarkonium production correlates with soft event activity, which matters when interpreting QGP-like signatures in small systems.

Critical assessment. Experimentally grounded and relevant, but this is a short conference proceeding rather than a full analysis paper. The observed correlation does not by itself establish final-state QGP dynamics and can receive production, selection, and underlying-event contributions.

Phenomenology

Triangular and fourth-order flow of D mesons in Pb-Pb collisions with event-by-event fluctuations

strong

Yiyun Tang, Jiamin Liu, Yunpeng Liu, Baoyi Chen · 4 authors · arXiv:2611.13891

D-meson flowevent-by-event fluctuationsheavy-flavor transportiEBE-VISHNU

Summary. Higher heavy-flavor harmonics emerge as cleaner probes of fluctuating QGP geometry than inclusive suppression.

Abstract. Charm transport in 5.02 TeV Pb-Pb collisions is coupled to event-by-event iEBE-VISHNU hydrodynamics through a Langevin model and instantaneous coalescence. The fluctuations barely change the azimuthally averaged D-meson RAA, but become increasingly important for higher flow harmonics: v3 is entirely fluctuation generated, and the calculation reproduces the main features of available CMS data while predicting D-meson v4.

Why it matters. The separation between a fluctuation-insensitive RAA and fluctuation-driven v3/v4 gives experiments a sharper way to test how initial geometry propagates through charm diffusion.

Critical assessment. Strong and directly testable phenomenology, especially because v3 is compared with data and v4 is predicted. The inference remains model conditional: one Langevin implementation, instantaneous coalescence, and no broad transport-parameter uncertainty analysis are shown.

AI/ML use

No new paper applied AI/ML directly to heavy-ion observables in this listing. AlphaHits, below, has a credible detector-reconstruction transfer path but was developed for generic LHC events rather than heavy-ion data.

AI/ML use cases in physics

AlphaHits: Full-Event Tracking and Reconstruction Towards a Hit-Level Foundation Model

strong

Sascha Caron, Nadezhda Dobreva, Polina Moskvitina, et al. · 7 authors · arXiv:2611.13222

hit-level transformerfull-event trackingmulti-task learningHL-LHC

Summary. The most HEP-native ML result today: inclusive hit-level tracking and reusable multi-task representations at realistic pileup scale.

Abstract. AlphaHits processes detector hits directly, reconstructing complete TrackML events with up to 150,000 hits and charged particles down to about 150 MeV. Its tracking pipeline reports a TrackML score of 0.959; pretrained hit representations are then reused for tracks, jets, flavor, missing momentum, and event classification. Higgs classification reaches AUC 0.944, comparable to a Particle Transformer using reconstructed inputs.

Why it matters. Heavy-ion detectors face dense occupancy and expensive reconstruction; transferable low-level representations could eventually unify tracking and downstream inference, though domain adaptation would be essential.

Critical assessment. Strong architecture work with unusually broad task coverage and detailed ablations. Important caveats are that downstream studies use simulation without pileup, the detector is generic rather than a real experiment, and tracking takes about three seconds per event on an H100; the authors explicitly leave latency optimization for future work.

AdjointFlow: Accelerating Scientific Inverse Problems with Physics-Based Transport

strong

Ethan Hanold, Nicholas M. Boffi, Qin Li, Leonardo Zepeda-Nunez · 4 authors · arXiv:2611.13389

inverse problemsstochastic interpolantsadjoint physicsgenerative transport

Summary. Physics is inserted into generative transport through the adjoint rather than applied only as an iterative correction.

Abstract. AdjointFlow uses a data-dependent adjoint initialization and a regularized stochastic-interpolant velocity to shorten the learned transport from measurements to concentrated posterior targets. Across nonlinear and linear inverse scattering, seismic imaging, and black-hole imaging, it reports up to about 70% lower reconstruction error and one-to-five-order inference speedups relative to selected diffusion and plug-and-play baselines, with systematic ablations.

Why it matters. The transferable idea is credible for HEP and nuclear inverse problems with differentiable simulators: detector unfolding, tomography, and possibly Bayesian parameter-field reconstruction could benefit from amortized physics-aware initialization.

Critical assessment. Methodologically substantial, with multiple PDE domains, iso-compute comparisons, and ablations. Transfer to collider or heavy-ion inference is not demonstrated, and the approach is most natural when a useful adjoint or efficient linearized inverse is available.
