Heavy-Ion Collision Papers

Theory

Gradient Flow for the Jet Transport Coefficient: A Quenched Lattice Benchmark

strong

Hai-Tao Shu; Cheng Zhang. arXiv:2609.25089

Summary: The authors determine the leading-twist, nonperturbative medium contribution to the jet transport coefficient using gradient-flow energy-momentum tensors in quenched SU(3) lattice QCD over 0.76 Tc to 2.25 Tc. With three lattice spacings, controlled continuum and small-flow-time extrapolations, and one-loop short-distance matching, they obtain qhat/T^3 approximately 1.1 in the deconfined range and consistency with an earlier quenched calculation.

Why it matters: It provides a systematically renormalized first-principles anchor for the nonperturbative contribution entering jet-quenching phenomenology.

Critical assessment: Strong methodology: three lattice spacings, continuum and flow-time extrapolations, two field-strength discretizations, and agreement with a distinct renormalization procedure. The central limitation is physical scope: pure gauge, leading twist, one-loop matching, and the asymptotically energetic limit are not yet full-QCD phenomenology.

Abstract: The authors determine the leading-twist, nonperturbative medium contribution to the jet transport coefficient using gradient-flow energy-momentum tensors in quenched SU(3) lattice QCD over 0.76 Tc to 2.25 Tc. With three lattice spacings, controlled continuum and small-flow-time extrapolations, and one-loop short-distance matching, they obtain qhat/T^3 approximately 1.1 in the deconfined range and consistency with an earlier quenched calculation.

Physical interpretation and limits of photon--dilepton radial-flow tomography

strong

Lipei Du. arXiv:2609.25691

Summary: A controlled expanding-fireball calculation shows why dilepton and flow-free photon inverse slopes track the same thermal scale, and that the photon shift probes an emission-weighted velocity component rather than a bulk radial-flow average. Prethermal source changes can create apparent flow, while multiple photon and dilepton windows help separate source composition from flow.

Why it matters: It turns a proposed electromagnetic observable into a better-defined diagnostic by identifying both what it measures and how early-source mismatch biases it.

Critical assessment: Strong conceptual diagnostic with explicit closure tests and source-mismatch studies. It deliberately uses a reduced boost-invariant fireball and phenomenological prethermal fugacity rather than full event-by-event hydrodynamics, so the quantitative response matrix is not yet experiment-ready.

Phenomenology

System-size dependence of bottomonium suppression from Pb-Pb to light-ion collisions

strong

Nora Brambilla; Tom Magorsch; Antonio Vairo. arXiv:2609.25182

Summary: Using the same NLO pNRQCD Lindblad evolution and microscopic transport coefficients previously constrained in Pb-Pb, the paper predicts integrated Upsilon double ratios in O-O and Ne-Ne hydrodynamic backgrounds. It reproduces the O-O sequential hierarchy within current uncertainties; Ne-Ne favors somewhat more suppression, but its uncertainty is still large.

Why it matters: Light-ion bottomonium is a sharp stress test of whether a common microscopic QGP response survives a large change in system size.

Critical assessment: Strong because the light-ion comparison is genuinely out of the Pb-Pb calibration domain and the transport coefficients are not retuned. Current errors are broad, Ne-Ne is not perfectly described, cold-nuclear-matter effects are omitted, and the conclusion is safest for double ratios where state-independent effects cancel.

Abstract: Using the same NLO pNRQCD Lindblad evolution and microscopic transport coefficients previously constrained in Pb-Pb, the paper predicts integrated Upsilon double ratios in O-O and Ne-Ne hydrodynamic backgrounds. It reproduces the O-O sequential hierarchy within current uncertainties; Ne-Ne favors somewhat more suppression, but its uncertainty is still large.

Effects of in-medium NN inelastic cross sections and the high-momentum tail of nucleon momentum distributions on pion production in heavy-ion collisions

mixed

Pengcheng Li; A. B. Larionov; Yongjia Wang; Gaochan Yong; Qingfeng Li. arXiv:2609.25818

Summary: The authors add relativistic-BUU in-medium NN inelastic cross sections and a short-range-correlation high-momentum tail to UrQMD for intermediate-energy Au+Au collisions. The former suppresses NDelta production, the latter partly restores pion yield, and their combination improves comparisons with HADES pion yields and the FOPI charged-pion ratio.

Why it matters: Near-threshold pion observables are used to constrain dense nuclear matter, so disentangling initial high-momentum tails from in-medium reaction rates is essential.

Critical assessment: Useful, multi-observable transport study, but mixed for inference: adding two ingredients improves data agreement without a global uncertainty analysis, and the in-medium cross section lacks explicit isospin dependence beyond threshold and Clebsch-Gordan structure.

Abstract: The authors add relativistic-BUU in-medium NN inelastic cross sections and a short-range-correlation high-momentum tail to UrQMD for intermediate-energy Au+Au collisions. The former suppresses NDelta production, the latter partly restores pion yield, and their combination improves comparisons with HADES pion yields and the FOPI charged-pion ratio.

AI/ML Use

Machine-learning modeling of nuclear collective observables and low-lying spectra

mixed

Dan Shi; Zu-Xing Yang; Xiao-Hua Fan; Zhi-Pan Li. arXiv:2609.25888

Summary: The Nuclear Collective Generator predicts a collective potential and six inertial functions on the beta-gamma plane from nuclear and shell descriptors, then propagates them through a five-dimensional collective Hamiltonian. Across 568 even-even nuclei it reports a 0.58 MeV mean RMS potential error and reasonable reproduction of deformation and low-lying spectroscopy.

Why it matters: Fast global surrogates for deformation-dependent collective inputs could make systematic nuclear-structure surveys and uncertainty studies far cheaper.

Critical assessment: Substantive ML-to-physics propagation is a strength. The 5% random held-out split mainly tests interpolation, not regional extrapolation; ensemble spread is not presented as calibrated physics uncertainty, and improved agreement with experiment can reflect smoothing of the teacher model rather than new physics.

Abstract: The Nuclear Collective Generator predicts a collective potential and six inertial functions on the beta-gamma plane from nuclear and shell descriptors, then propagates them through a five-dimensional collective Hamiltonian. Across 568 even-even nuclei it reports a 0.58 MeV mean RMS potential error and reasonable reproduction of deformation and low-lying spectroscopy.

AI/ML Use Cases in Physics

Predictive Uncertainty for Neural CAE Surrogates

strong

Kaustubh Tangsali; Mohammad Amin Nabian; Kelvin Lee; Carmelo Gonzales; Sanjay Choudhry. arXiv:2609.25430

Method and result: The paper compares a Gaussian-process-based method, concrete Monte Carlo dropout, and deep ensembles for geometry-conditioned neural surrogates on three large aerodynamics and crash datasets. It evaluates error ranking, interval coverage, unfamiliar-input response, and derived quantities, finding that no method dominates across datasets or decision criteria.

Likely HEP/heavy-ion use case: HEP detector and heavy-ion emulators need uncertainty estimates that rank failures and remain calibrated across changing geometries and observables.

Critical assessment: Strong transfer candidate because it compares several UQ families on multiple large field-valued datasets and tests more than average error. Calibration relies on validation rescaling and method rankings change by dataset, so HEP use would require domain-specific shift tests.

It evaluates error ranking, interval coverage, unfamiliar-input response, and derived quantities, finding that no method dominates across datasets or decision criteria.

Variational objectives for amortized Bayesian inference in inverse problems: The role of posterior conditioning

strong

Abhishek Srivastava; Arijit Hazra; Rajesh Dubbaku. arXiv:2609.25145

Method and result: The authors analyze KL-, Jensen-Shannon-, and Jensen-Shannon-Wasserstein-regularized amortized inference with a full-covariance Gaussian encoder and physics surrogate. Analytic linear-Gaussian tests and nonlinear ODE/PDE inverse problems show that the preferred objective depends on posterior conditioning, with the Wasserstein variant improving strongly ill-conditioned cases.

Likely HEP/heavy-ion use case: Heavy-ion parameter extraction is often weakly identified; conditioning-aware objectives could improve amortized posterior inference for transport and initial-condition parameters.

Critical assessment: Strong and unusually careful: known-posterior benchmarks, generalized-Fisher analysis, and nonlinear ODE/PDE tests support the conditioning claim. Transfer to stochastic, simulator-based HEP posteriors remains unproven.

Abstract: The authors analyze KL-, Jensen-Shannon-, and Jensen-Shannon-Wasserstein-regularized amortized inference with a full-covariance Gaussian encoder and physics surrogate. Analytic linear-Gaussian tests and nonlinear ODE/PDE inverse problems show that the preferred objective depends on posterior conditioning, with the Wasserstein variant improving strongly ill-conditioned cases.

Weak or Overclaimed Papers Worth Caution

Longitudinal Lambda Polarization as a Quantitative Constraint on Geometric Relaxation in Pb-Pb Collisions at 5.36 TeV

caution

Yilong Xie; Simin Wu; Laszlo P. Csernai. arXiv:2609.26740

Summary: Across 48 initial states evolved with 3+1D ideal hydrodynamics and an isothermal local-equilibrium spin prescription, the authors fit a compact relation linking longitudinal Lambda polarization to freeze-out eccentricity and elliptic flow. A 12-state same-framework holdout retains roughly 86% explained variation, and a proof-of-principle inversion is applied to ALICE data.

Why it matters: Polarization may encode residual geometry, but a quantitative inversion would be highly valuable only if it survives realistic dissipative and fluctuating dynamics.

Critical assessment: Caution. The impressive 86-87% fit quality is obtained within one ideal-hydrodynamic model family, and the holdout changes inputs inside that same family. Reflection-symmetric Bjorken profiles, zero angular momentum, no viscosity, fixed isothermal decoupling, and one spin prescription make the ALICE inversion proof-of-principle rather than a robust extraction.
