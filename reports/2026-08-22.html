Heavy-Ion Collision Papers

Theory

Correspondence between hydrodynamic frames, transport coefficients, and hydrodynamic modes in relativistic fluids

strong

Md Hasanujjaman, Mahfuzur Rahaman. arXiv:2608.20324

Summary: The authors derive exact transformations between Eckart and Landau-Lifshitz hydrodynamic frames and show how thermal conductivity changes under frame choice. In a baryon-rich Boltzmann nucleon gas, the Landau-Lifshitz conductivity is suppressed relative to Eckart, while sound attenuation remains invariant.

Why it matters: Heavy-ion extractions of transport coefficients, especially at finite baryon density, can become confusing if frame conventions are compared naively. This paper is a useful cleanup of that bookkeeping problem.

Critical assessment: Strong as theory infrastructure. It does not by itself extract new QGP properties, but it reduces ambiguity in comparing hydrodynamic, kinetic-theory, and lattice-motivated inputs.

Lattice-data-driven specific heat and isentropic bulk modulus of SU(3) gluon matter at finite temperature

mixed/useful

Wei Shen, Zhen-Yan Lu, Muhammad Waqas, Xun Chen, Zhi-Jun Ma, Guang-Xiong Peng. arXiv:2608.20031

Summary: A temperature-dependent effective-gluon-mass model constrained by lattice-QCD pressure data is used to derive pressure, trace anomaly, gluon density, specific heat, and isentropic bulk modulus. The response functions peak or stiffen across the deconfinement region and approach conformal reference values at high temperature.

Why it matters: Response functions near T_c are relevant to equation-of-state modeling and hydrodynamic sensitivity studies, even though pure SU(3) gluodynamics is not full QCD.

Critical assessment: Useful but not transformational. The model is thermodynamically organized and accepted in EPJC, but phenomenological translation to heavy-ion matter with dynamical quarks requires care.

Negative diffusion in the Functional Renormalization Group flow for the Quark-Diquark Model

mixed/diagnostic

Johannes Poeplau, Ashutosh Dash, Dirk H. Rischke. arXiv:2608.20196

Summary: The paper traces low-temperature, high-chemical-potential instabilities in a quark-diquark FRG calculation to a negative diffusion coefficient in the flow of the derivative of the effective potential. It argues this is a model feature, not merely a numerical artifact, and proposes a hyperdiffusion regularization.

Why it matters: The high-density QCD phase diagram is a key target for FAIR/NICA/RHIC-BES-adjacent theory, and numerical artifacts in color-superconducting or inhomogeneous regions can be scientifically misleading.

Critical assessment: Important as a warning and repair attempt. The proposed regularization appears practical, but any phase-diagram conclusions from the regularized model should be treated as model-dependent until benchmarked against alternatives.

Experiment

Heavy-flavour production and correlations in pp collisions: precision tests of pQCD and hadronisation with ALICE

mixed/baseline

Deependra Sharma, for the ALICE Collaboration. arXiv:2608.19714

Summary: This conference proceeding reports preliminary prompt D-meson results, final B0 production down to low pT at midrapidity, rapidity comparisons to LHCb, and D0-J/psi associated production in pp collisions at 13.6 TeV.

Why it matters: Heavy-ion heavy-flavour interpretation depends on reliable pp baselines for production, hadronization, and single- versus double-parton scattering contributions.

Critical assessment: Useful but limited by proceeding format. The ALICE measurements are important, but the paper is a compact status report rather than a full heavy-ion analysis.

Phenomenology

Tracing Vacuum Hadronization with Conserved Currents

strong

Weiyao Ke, Hai Tao Li, Wanchen Li, Xiaohui Liu, Ding Yu Shao. arXiv:2608.19708

Summary: The authors propose flavor-resolved conserved-current moments in jets as probes of vacuum hadronization, relating charge, baryon number, and strangeness flows through symmetry constraints. The framework connects measurable jet quantum-number flow to diquark and strange-pair production probabilities.

Why it matters: Hadronization baselines and baryon/strangeness transport are central for interpreting heavy-ion modifications. A conserved-current approach could provide cleaner vacuum reference measurements.

Critical assessment: Strong conceptual phenomenology. The main risk is experimental reconstruction of net quantum-number flow with sufficient purity, but the proposal is concrete and symmetry anchored.

Production of quarkonium pairs via the fragmentation mechanism

mixed/relevant

Franco Barattini, Benjamin Guiot, Marat Siddikov. arXiv:2608.20329

Summary: The paper estimates single- and dihadron-fragmentation contributions to inclusive heavy-quarkonium-pair production, using a microscopic perturbative model for dihadron fragmentation and CGC amplitudes for heavy-quark production.

Why it matters: Quarkonium-pair baselines affect interpretations of double-parton scattering, heavy-quark production, and potentially pA/AA quarkonium reference modeling.

Critical assessment: Interesting but not yet a heavy-ion centerpiece. The sizeable dihadron-fragmentation claim is worth tracking, but its immediate QGP relevance is indirect.

AI/ML Use Cases in Physics

Distilling Normalizing Flows for Real-Time Anomaly Detection at the LHC

strong

Tara P. A. Tahseen, Noah Clarke Hall, Nikolaos Konstantinidis, Paula Martinez Suarez. arXiv:2608.19912

Method/result: A conditional normalizing flow is used as a high-quality likelihood teacher, then distilled into lightweight decision-tree and quantized neural-network students for FPGA-compatible trigger deployment. The setup explicitly handles missing input features.

Likely HEP/heavy-ion use case: Real-time anomaly detection at LHC triggers, online data quality monitoring, and potentially heavy-ion trigger streams where latency, bandwidth, and incomplete detector information matter.

Critical assessment: Strong and transferable. The value is in matching a principled density estimator to hardware constraints; the remaining test is full realistic trigger integration rather than offline performance alone.

Green BOA: Determining the environmental break-even point for ML-based data compression

caution

Caterina Doglioni, Akshat Gupta, Thomas Elliott, Hanzila Hussain, Sanjiban Sengupta. arXiv:2608.19994

Why caution: The environmental break-even framing is genuinely relevant to HEP data operations, especially if ML compression is proposed for large detector datasets.
