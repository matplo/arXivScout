Tuesday arXiv new-list scan, screened for heavy-ion collision physics and AI/ML methods with credible transfer value for HEP or nuclear physics. Candidate triage emphasized abstracts first and selected only papers with clear scientific leverage or a useful caution.

Executive Takeaways

  • The strongest heavy-ion entry is 2607.24153: a concrete 3+1D hydro plus hadronic afterburner improvement for longitudinal entropy deposition in d+Au, with validation also claimed across p+Au, He3+Au, and Au+Au.
  • 2607.23195 is a useful dense-matter baseline: a purely hadronic transport model reproduces proton flow data up to 4.3 GeV but fails at 4.5 GeV, giving a cautious handle on the possible onset of partonic degrees of freedom.

Heavy-Ion Collision Papers

Theory

Proton Collectivity in Au+Au Collisions at sqrt(sNN)=2.4-4.5 GeV from a Unified Purely Hadronic EOS without QCD Phase Transition

strong

Gao-Feng Wei, Shuang-Jie Liu, Yu-Liang Zhao, Qi-Jun Zhi, Zhigang Xiao. arXiv:2607.23195.

Summary: The authors use a purely hadronic transport model with a momentum-dependent mean field and K0=230 MeV to compute proton directed, sideward, and elliptic flows in Au+Au collisions at sqrt(sNN)=2.4-4.5 GeV. They report quantitative agreement with HADES, E895, and STAR flow data up to 4.3 GeV, followed by failure at 4.5 GeV.

Why it matters: A credible hadronic baseline is essential before interpreting BES/fixed-target flow deviations as evidence for partonic degrees of freedom or EOS softening at high baryon density.

Critical assessment: Strong within its stated scope because it confronts multiple flow observables and experiments in the dense-baryonic regime. The partonic-onset interpretation remains circumstantial; robustness against alternative mean fields, in-medium cross sections, and centrality/systematic uncertainties will decide how much weight to give the 4.5 GeV discrepancy.

Interaction fingerprints in temperature-fluctuation cumulants from the QCD crossover to nuclear liquid-gas criticality

mixed

Debasish Mallick. arXiv:2607.24616.

Summary: The paper calculates temperature-fluctuation cumulants c2, c3, and c4 in ideal HRG, excluded-volume HRG, and van der Waals HRG models. Along the freeze-out curve, the models separate strongly at lower beam energies, and the van der Waals model shows features associated with the liquid-gas Widom line.

Why it matters: The proposed connection to event-by-event mean-pT fluctuations could make thermodynamic response functions more directly observable in beam-energy-scan data.

Critical assessment: Interesting and physically motivated, but still model-level. It needs a careful mapping from temperature cumulants to measured mean-pT cumulants, including volume fluctuations, acceptance, hadronic afterburning, and noncritical correlations.

Experiment

Light hadron production measurements with Au+Au Collisions from sqrt(sNN)=3.2--4.5 GeV with STAR

mixed

Mathias C. Labonte, for the STAR Collaboration. arXiv:2607.22945.

Summary: This SQM proceedings contribution reports STAR fixed-target light-hadron spectra for pi, K, and p as functions of pT, rapidity, and centrality at sqrt(sNN)=3.2, 3.5, 3.9, and 4.5 GeV. Blast-wave fits are used to infer kinetic freeze-out temperature and surface velocity.

Why it matters: These low-energy BES-II measurements are direct inputs to dense-baryonic-matter modeling and EOS constraints, especially around high baryochemical potential.

Critical assessment: Important experimental input, but the arXiv item is a short proceedings summary rather than a full analysis paper. The science value is clear; quantitative assessment awaits the complete spectra, uncertainties, correlations, and model-comparison details.

Phenomenology

Study the Longitudinal Entropy Deposition using d+Au Collision

strong

Zhu Meng, Weiyao Ke, Long-Gang Pang. arXiv:2607.24153.

Summary: The authors improve initial-state modeling for asymmetric collisions by sampling deuteron configurations from an ab initio wavefunction and adding a longitudinal entropy deposition model with a transverse entropy coefficient beta and binary-collision-dependent rapidity loss. Using CLVisc plus SMASH, they reproduce d+Au pseudorapidity distributions across five centralities, plus pT spectra and vn, and claim validation in p+Au, He3+Au, and Au+Au.

Why it matters: Longitudinal structure is a weak point in many hydro descriptions, and asymmetric/light-ion systems are becoming important for constraining both initial conditions and nuclear structure.

Critical assessment: Strong and directly relevant. The breadth of validation is encouraging, but the universality claim should be tested against more differential longitudinal decorrelation and identified-particle observables, not only bulk one- and two-dimensional projections.

Probing collective behaviour of Heavy Quarks through pT-differential radial flow v0(pT)

mixed

Salvatore Plumari, Maria Lucia Sambataro, Santosh K. Das, Vincenzo Greco. arXiv:2607.23499.

Summary: This proceedings paper proposes heavy-flavor pT-differential radial flow v0(pT) as a probe of heavy-quark coupling to the expanding QGP. In an event-by-event Langevin plus relativistic Boltzmann transport setup, the observable is sensitive to the temperature dependence of Ds(T) at intermediate pT and to hadronization at low pT.

Why it matters: Heavy-flavor transport coefficients remain a major QGP target, and an observable sensitive to Ds(T) in a different way from v2 and RAA would be useful.

Critical assessment: Worth tracking, but still a compact proceedings-level proposal. The observable needs experimental feasibility studies, covariance with existing heavy-flavor observables, and robustness checks against hadronization and background modeling.

AI/ML Use Cases in Physics

Predict before you train: Scaling Laws for particle physics foundation models

strong

Jan-Lucas Uslu, Benjamin Nachman, Christopher Re. arXiv:2607.23377.

Method and result: The authors fit joint model-and-data scaling laws on small generic jet transformers spanning three orders of magnitude in compute, then predict the loss of models trained with more than 100 times more compute to within one percent. They also connect lower pretraining loss to improved downstream top-tagging and quark/gluon discrimination.

Likely HEP/heavy-ion use case: This is directly useful for planning collider foundation models and could transfer to heavy-ion jet, event, and detector pretraining where large-model training costs are nontrivial.

Critical assessment: Strong because it tests prediction beyond the fit range and releases models/recipe/code. The main limitation is model-family and task dependence: scaling forecasts need revalidation for detector-level inputs, pileup/heavy-ion backgrounds, and observables beyond standard jet tagging.

Neural Control Variates at LO and NLO

strong

Theo Heimel, Tilman Plehn, Rebecca Revelli, Sophia Vent, Ramon Winterhalder. arXiv:2607.23591.

Method and result: The paper uses signed neural control variates, built from two normalizing flows, to reduce event-weight spread and mitigate negative weights in LO and NLO phase-space integration and event generation. For NLO, the conditional control variate acts like a trainable complement to standard subtraction schemes.

Likely HEP/heavy-ion use case: Strong transfer to HEP event generation and potentially to expensive heavy-ion hard-probe calculations where weighted samples and integration cost are painful.

Critical assessment: Strong and methodologically well targeted. Key remaining questions are numerical stability, failure modes in high-multiplicity final states, and how easily the method can be integrated into production generator workflows with systematic uncertainty control.

Symbolic Extraction of Non-Perturbative Transverse-Momentum-Dependent Distributions from Drell-Yan Data

strong

Cole Granger, Alessandro Bacchetta, Valerio Bertone, Chiara Bissolotti, Matteo Cerutti, Marco Radici, Simone Rodini, Lorenzo Rossi, Cristiano Fanelli. arXiv:2607.24710.

Method and result: A factorized neural network is fit directly to Drell-Yan cross-section data at high resummation accuracy, then symbolic regression extracts compact analytic forms from the learned components. The selected formula uses nine free numerical constants and achieves chi2/ndf=1.040 on 482 points, retaining a nontrivial x-bT term.

Likely HEP/heavy-ion use case: Relevant to TMD phenomenology, EIC/LHC/RHIC analyses, and more broadly to turning flexible ML fits into interpretable parametrizations for QCD and nuclear PDFs/TMDs.

Critical assessment: Strong because it balances flexibility, interpretability, and a clear goodness-of-fit criterion. Watch for robustness under dataset variations, correlated systematics, and whether the symbolic form remains stable when new data are added.

Extending Fourier Neural Operators for Modeling Parameterized and Coupled PDEs

strong transfer

Cheng Jing, Uvini Balasuriya Mudiyanselage, Abhishek Verma, Kallol Bera, Shahid Rauf, Kookjin Lee. arXiv:2607.23466.

Method and result: The paper extends Fourier neural operators with hypernetwork-based parameter conditioning and design choices for coupled variables. Benchmarks include one-dimensional capacitively coupled plasma equations and Gray-Scott dynamics, with reported 55-72 percent lower errors than strong baselines.

Likely HEP/heavy-ion use case: Transfer value is credible for surrogate modeling of parameterized hydrodynamics, transport, detector response, and plasma-like PDE systems where coupled fields and changing parameters matter.

Critical assessment: Strong as scientific-ML infrastructure, especially with ICLR acceptance, but not HEP-specific. Heavy-ion usefulness depends on handling relativistic geometry, shocks/discontinuities, conservation constraints, and uncertainty quantification.

On the post-hoc Evaluation of PDE Discovery: A Multifaceted Challenge of Scientific Advancement

useful caution

Baptiste Mathevon, Farah Cherfaoui, Amaury Habrard, Marc Sebban. arXiv:2607.23753.

Method and result: This review/taxonomy argues that PDE discovery evaluation must jointly consider predictive accuracy, physical consistency, interpretability, and out-of-distribution generalization, and that existing metrics only partially address those goals.

Likely HEP/heavy-ion use case: Valuable for vetting symbolic-regression or physics-informed discovery claims in hydrodynamics, transport, detector calibration, and effective-model construction.

Critical assessment: Useful because it pushes against overinterpretation of discovered equations. It is not a new algorithm or physics result, but it is a good guardrail for evaluating future ML-for-physics claims.

Global Convergence of DGM and PINN Algorithms for Solving Nonlinear PDEs

mixed transfer

Justin Sirignano, Konstantinos Spiliopoulos, Samuel Cohen. arXiv:2607.24726.

Method and result: The authors prove global convergence for DGM/PINN training with gradient descent for a class of semi-linear PDEs nonlinear in the solution and first derivative.

Likely HEP/heavy-ion use case: Conceptual transfer to PINN-based solvers for transport and field equations, especially where guarantees are currently weaker than empirical performance claims.

Critical assessment: Mathematically valuable, but the transfer is bounded by the PDE class and assumptions. It should not be read as a blanket guarantee for realistic relativistic hydrodynamics, kinetic theory, or detector inverse problems.

Weak or Overclaimed Papers Worth Caution

Exploring the Effects of Color Reconnection on Net-Charge Fluctuations in pp Collisions at sqrt(s)=2.76 TeV using PYTHIA Monash

caution

Dibakar Dhar, Tumpa Biswas, Zubayer Ahammed, Prabir Kumar Haldar. arXiv:2607.24442.

The paper is useful as a PYTHIA sensitivity study: it checks color-reconnection configurations against ALICE net-charge fluctuation data and includes a fixed-multiplicity NoCR comparison. The caution is interpretive. A PYTHIA Monash color-reconnection scan in pp can diagnose model behavior, but it should not be oversold as direct evidence about heavy-ion critical fluctuations or QGP dynamics without stronger baselines, systematic retuning, and broader data comparisons.
