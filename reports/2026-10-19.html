Exploring QCD Matter through Energy Correlators of Soft Hadrons

Yiyang Peng, João Barata, Andrey V. Sadofyev, Huichao Song

arXiv: 2610.22477

Summary: Introduces soft-hadron energy-energy correlators and their azimuthal harmonics, decomposing them into mean-flow and connected-correlation pieces. VISH2+1/iEBE-VISHNU and PYTHIA comparisons show a clean system-size and angular-scale separation between collective flow and nonflow.

Why it matters: This creates an experimentally measurable bridge between field-theoretic energy-flow observables and the small-system collectivity problem, while retaining sensitivity to initial geometry and shear viscosity.

Critical assessment: Strong. The observable is analytically well motivated and tested across smooth and fluctuating hydrodynamics, a hybrid model, and PYTHIA. The main limitation is that discrimination is still model-level; detector effects and data closure tests remain to be demonstrated.

Abstract

We propose energy-energy correlators (EECs) of soft hadrons and their azimuthal harmonics as new soft observables to probe the collective flow and correlations beyond flow for large and small systems created in high energy nuclear collisions. We separate the soft EEC into a disconnected contribution determined by the mean energy flow and a connected contribution encoding correlations beyond it. Viscous hydrodynamic simulations demonstrate that the soft EEC of a fluid system is governed by the disconnected contribution, while its elliptic and triangular harmonics respond to the initial geometry and shear viscosity as traditional flow harmonics do. Comparisons between iEBE-VISHNU and PYTHIA for p+p, O+O, and Pb+Pb collisions show that the disconnected contribution dominates at large angles in all systems, whereas connected correlations beyond the mean energy flow grow toward small angles and small systems, most strongly for the harmonics.

Regularization of stochastic fluid dynamics and non-perturbative renormalization of the shear viscosity of incompressible fluids

Mattis Harhoff, Cedric Riedel, Johannes Roth, Sören Schlichting

arXiv: 2610.23442

Summary: Builds a symmetry-preserving UV-finite regulator for stochastic hydrodynamics and compares renormalization-group-improved viscosity calculations with direct lattice simulations.

Why it matters: Hydrodynamic fluctuations and transport-coefficient renormalization are foundational issues for precision QGP modeling; a regulator compatible with both continuum functional methods and lattice simulation is a useful technical advance.

Critical assessment: Strong methodology, but indirect heavy-ion relevance today. The explicit validation is for incompressible fluids, so extension to relativistic, expanding QCD matter is essential before phenomenological impact can be claimed.

Abstract

We propose a regularization of stochastic fluid dynamics that preserves all relevant symmetries. By considering an incompressible fluid, we demonstrate explicitly that the regularized theory is UV finite, and therefore admits a continuum limit. The regularized theory is amenable both to direct numerical simulations of the lattice discretized theory and to functional methods in the continuum. We use it for renormalization-group-improved perturbative calculations of shear-viscosity renormalization in two and three spatial dimensions and compare with direct lattice simulations.

Proton-Number Fluctuations in 238U+238U Collisions at sqrt(sNN)=2.1 GeV with the CEE Fast Simulation

Shuaike Lv, Fan Si, Xionghong He, Xu Sun, Yige Huang, et al. (9 authors total)

arXiv: 2610.23460

Summary: A realistic fast-simulation feasibility study for proton cumulants through fourth order at CEE, including reconstruction efficiency, acceptance gaps, centrality, and PID.

Why it matters: It quantifies the data volume and correction burden for fluctuation measurements in a high-baryon-density regime that is otherwise sparsely covered.

Critical assessment: Useful and credible preparation work, but mixed as physics evidence: all conclusions are simulation-based and depend on JAM2 plus a fast detector model. Closure under alternative generators and full simulation will be decisive.

Abstract

The CEE experiment at HIRFL offers an opportunity to map the high-baryon-density QCD phase diagram. Uranium-uranium collisions at 2.1 GeV are generated with JAM2 and processed through a CEE fast simulation including detector response, centrality determination, and particle identification. Proton-number cumulants through fourth order are compared with generator truth. Track-by-track efficiency and TPC azimuthal-gap corrections recover generator-level results within current statistical uncertainties, while acceptance compensation carries a substantial statistical cost.

Unexpectedly large violation of isospin symmetry in nucleus-nucleus collisions at CERN SPS energies

Andrzej Rybicki for the NA61/SHINE Collaboration

arXiv: 2610.23498

Summary: Highlights a charged-to-neutral kaon-yield excess in central Ar+Sc collisions and compiles suggestive evidence across lower-energy systems.

Why it matters: If confirmed with controlled correlated uncertainties, the effect would challenge standard assumptions about isospin symmetry in hadron production and influence yield-based phenomenology.

Critical assessment: Important anomaly, but evidence remains mixed. This is a workshop proceeding rather than a definitive measurement paper; the cross-experiment combination contains sizeable and heterogeneous uncertainties, and the paper itself gives a broad significance range and calls for independent precision checks.

Abstract

NA61/SHINE reports an excess of inclusive charged over neutral kaon production in 0–10% central Ar+Sc collisions at sqrt(sNN)=8.8 and 11.9 GeV that is not explained by mechanisms in present models. Comparisons to other experiments suggest a similar excess, with sizeable uncertainties, over 2.6–200 GeV; LHC results remain consistent with unity but are not precise. A similar discrepancy appears in minimum-bias pi−+C reactions.

Effects of kinematic cuts on v0(pT)

Tribhuban Parida, Rupam Samanta, Jean-Yves Ollitrault

arXiv: 2610.23427

Summary: Shows that a low-pT acceptance threshold induces an approximately constant negative offset in the radial-flow harmonic v0(pT), and derives a hydrodynamic correction that improves ALICE–ATLAS consistency.

Why it matters: This is a compact, directly actionable analysis correction for cross-experiment comparison of an emerging observable.

Critical assessment: Strong and practical. The logic is transparent and addresses a real discrepancy, although the correction inherits some model dependence from the hydrodynamic estimate and should be propagated through full experimental systematics.

Abstract

We show that the analysis of v0(pT) is biased when low-pT particles are not detected. The low-pT threshold shifts v0(pT) by a constant negative value, which depends on the threshold. We evaluate the shift using hydrodynamic simulations. Correcting for this bias significantly improves agreement between ALICE and ATLAS results for v0(pT) in Pb+Pb collisions.

Equiprobability Anomaly Detector Using Angular Distributions

Alex Whitten, John Cicero, Tae Min Hong

arXiv: 2610.23667

Summary: A physics-structured density-estimation anomaly detector built from angular bases and equiprobable bins rather than a black-box neural density model.

Likely HEP/NP use: Credible transfer to heavy-ion analyses that use angular correlations, event-plane structure, or multidimensional reference distributions. Its explicit basis and density contours could make validation and systematic stress tests easier than for opaque generative models.

Critical assessment: Promising and interpretable, but mixed overall. The normalizing-flow comparison at parton level is encouraging; the marked reconstruction degradation shows that combinatorics, detector effects, and nuisance robustness remain the real test.

Abstract

The angular distribution of semileptonic top-quark pairs is expanded in Wigner D functions, with coefficients obtained by orthogonal-series density estimation. Equiprobable surfaces of the estimated top-pair density define bins in which non-top events concentrate at low density. Separation is quantified using the Kullback–Leibler divergence. Parton-level performance is comparable to normalizing flows, but reconstruction-level performance is limited by jet assignments.

AI/ML use cases in physicsstrong

Reconstruction of Magnetic Fields with a Physics-Informed Neural Network

P. Adhikari, R. Bhattarai, J. Brewington, D. Buskirk, F. B. Hills, et al. (12 authors total)

arXiv: 2610.23691

Summary: Uses Maxwell-constrained PINNs to interpolate three-dimensional magnetic fields and gradients from sparse measurements, with conventional interpolation benchmarks and public data.

Likely HEP/NP use: Directly relevant to detector field mapping and systematic-control workflows in HEP and nuclear experiments, particularly when sensor placement is sparse or regions are inaccessible.

Critical assessment: Solid transferable instrumentation work. Physics constraints, conventional benchmarks, and released data are strengths. The abstract and demonstrations do not yet establish superiority under realistic sensor noise, calibration drift, or out-of-domain geometry, so operational advantage remains to be shown.

Abstract

A physics-informed neural network reconstructs three-dimensional magnetic fields and gradients from discrete measurement points while enforcing the source-free Maxwell constraints div B=0 and curl B=0. The method is applied to two distinct field profiles and benchmarked against spherical-harmonic and cubic-spline reconstructions; the associated datasets are public.

A comparative study of variational quantum classifiers and classical machine learning for tW–ttbar discrimination at the LHC

Rohan Hindocha, Priyanka

arXiv: 2610.22504

Summary: The VQC is benchmarked against standard classical classifiers, but all models achieve only moderate discrimination and are statistically similar.

Critical assessment: Worth cautioning about rather than selecting as a strong AI result. Reported AUCs are about 0.60–0.70, and the paper finds no statistically significant VQC advantage; some apparent differences are unstable or within bootstrap uncertainties. Demonstrating parity on a limited classical simulation is not evidence of quantum utility, scaling advantage, or deployment readiness.

Abstract

A variational quantum classifier is compared with boosted decision trees and a multilayer perceptron for simulated tW versus ttbar discrimination using CMS Open Data-derived samples across dilepton, boosted-hadronic, and resolved-hadronic regimes.
