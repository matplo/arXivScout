Heavy-Ion Collision Papers

Theory

Resonances at finite temperature from the lattice

strong

Jakob Hoffmann, Peter Lowdon, Owe Philipsen. arXiv:2607.22346.

Summary: The paper uses thermoparticles to define finite-temperature two-particle scattering, derives a thermal unitarity relation, and obtains a finite-temperature generalization of the finite-volume quantization condition. The payoff is a framework for constraining thermal scattering amplitudes and resonance properties from lattice finite-volume energy levels.

Why it matters: Thermal resonance properties are central to connecting lattice QCD, hadronic spectral functions, and QGP phenomenology. A controlled finite-volume formalism would be valuable for moving beyond qualitative statements about in-medium broadening.

Critical assessment: Strong formal work, but not yet a phenomenology result. Its impact will depend on numerical implementations and whether usable thermal spectra can be extracted with controlled systematics.

Abstract: The authors introduce a finite-temperature scattering and quantization framework where broadened thermoparticle peaks modify the finite-volume kinematic structure relative to vacuum analyses.

Lattice study of spin interactions between heavy quarks in the quark-gluon plasma

strong

Dibyendu Bala, Olaf Kaczmarek, Sayantan Sharma, Swagatam Tah. arXiv:2607.21729.

Summary: This work calculates the spin-dependent O(1/M^2) correction to the thermal heavy-quark potential. It first shows in hard-thermal-loop perturbation theory that the imaginary part differs for pseudoscalar and vector quarkonia, then extracts the imaginary part nonperturbatively in quenched lattice QCD at about 470 MeV.

Why it matters: Quarkonium suppression and spectral functions are important QGP probes, and spin-dependent thermal effects can alter vector versus pseudoscalar interpretations.

Critical assessment: Strong and novel because the target observable is directly tied to quarkonium phenomenology and the lattice extraction is nonperturbative. Caveats are substantial: quenched QCD, one high temperature, and the need to propagate this potential into realistic spectral and transport comparisons.

Abstract: The authors find a complex spin-dependent potential in the deconfined phase whose imaginary part can be significant for charmonium states.

Momentum anisotropy from Resistive Magnetohydrodynamics

mixed

Khwahish Kushwah, Gabriel S. Denicol. arXiv:2607.21879.

Summary: The authors derive second-order relativistic resistive MHD from the Boltzmann-Vlasov equation for a two-component ultrarelativistic plasma. They find that electric fields couple charge diffusion to shear stress and can generate momentum anisotropy even without a flow gradient.

Why it matters: Electromagnetic fields in heavy-ion collisions remain hard to connect quantitatively to flow observables. This paper clarifies one possible field-induced source of anisotropy.

Critical assessment: Worth tracking, but it is a short SQM contribution and mostly formal. The heavy-ion strength depends on whether realistic field lifetimes, conductivities, and expanding geometries leave an experimentally relevant signal.

Experiment

The future fixed-target program at the CERN SPS

mixed

Enrico Scomparin. arXiv:2607.22328.

Summary: This proceedings contribution reviews the CERN SPS heavy-ion fixed-target legacy and outlines continued finite-muB QGP studies through NA61 and the newly approved NA60+/DiCE program.

Why it matters: The SPS remains one of the key facilities for mapping QCD matter at high baryochemical potential, especially through dileptons and hadronic observables.

Critical assessment: Important program context rather than a new measurement. It is scientifically relevant, but the evidence content is in the experimental agenda and prior achievements, not in fresh data.

Phenomenology

Finite Formation Time Antenna Radiation: Color Spectators Break Single-Emitter BDMPS-Z

strong

Fabio Dominguez, Pablo Guerrero-Rodriguez, Carlos A. Salgado. arXiv:2607.22502.

Summary: The paper computes direct in-medium soft-gluon emission from a color-singlet quark-antiquark antenna while allowing medium interactions during the finite formation time of the antenna. It finds nontrivial correlations with the non-emitting spectator leg and a significant departure from the instantaneous-formation limit that reduces to BDMPS-Z.

Why it matters: This attacks a microscopic assumption behind jet-quenching calculations: when and how a color antenna becomes a set of effectively independent emitters in the medium.

Critical assessment: Strong theory/phenomenology. The calculation is specialized, but it addresses a real missing ingredient in medium-induced radiation. The next test is whether the correction can be organized into a practical jet-evolution implementation and compared against jet substructure data.

Abstract: The authors report spectator-induced complexity even at leading Nc and a breakdown of the usual single-emitter approximation for finite antenna formation time.

Non-Markovian heavy-quark equilibration and equilibrium correlation function in a thermal medium

strong

Monisha Nair, Juan Torres-Rincon, Santosh K. Das. arXiv:2607.22403.

Summary: The paper computes charm current-current correlations in Langevin and generalized Langevin frameworks, extracting the diffusion coefficient through Green-Kubo. Memory kernels alter transient correlation structure but leave the nonrelativistic diffusion coefficient unchanged; in relativistic correlated-noise cases, equilibration can become damped and oscillatory.

Why it matters: Heavy-flavor transport analyses often compress medium effects into diffusion and drag coefficients. This paper helps separate what memory effects can change dynamically from what they may leave invariant in transport extraction.

Critical assessment: Strong and careful, with useful ancillary numerical material. The caveat is that phenomenological impact requires embedding these memory effects in expanding, spatially inhomogeneous QGP backgrounds and comparing to heavy-flavor observables.

AI/ML Use Cases in Physics

Light Antinuclei Coalescence: Femtoscopic Constraints via Neural-Flow Surrogates

strong

M. Korwieser, L. Fabbietti, B. Hashemi, L. Heinrich, M. Mahlein, D. L. Mihaylov, C. S. Zeyn. arXiv:2607.21689.

Method and result: A differentiable normalizing-flow surrogate emulates the CECA femtoscopic source model, is fit to 49 ALICE proton-proton correlation functions, and is extrapolated to low-multiplicity cosmic-ray-relevant regimes. The authors report sub-percent emulation fidelity and large reductions in B2 and B3 coalescence-parameter uncertainties.

Likely HEP/heavy-ion use case: Directly relevant to ALICE femtoscopy, antinuclei coalescence, and surrogate-based propagation of experimental constraints into phenomenological production models.

Critical assessment: Strong because the ML component is not decorative: it accelerates and differentiates a physics model, then uses real ALICE correlation data. The main caution is extrapolation to low multiplicity and wavefunction systematics.

Statistical validation of template-based parameter extraction from toy thrust distributions for future FCC-ee studies

strong

Fatih ilgin. arXiv:2607.22282.

Method and result: The paper tests template-based parameter extraction on controlled toy thrust distributions using 10,000 pseudo-experiments, comparing Pearson chi2, multinomial likelihood, and interpolated likelihood strategies. It explicitly studies off-grid recovery, binning, template statistics, and model mismatch.

Likely HEP/heavy-ion use case: The validation pattern transfers well to heavy-ion template fits, emulator calibration, jet-substructure extractions, and any analysis where closure under the nominal model is easier than robustness under mismatch.

Critical assessment: Strong as methods hygiene, not as a physics extraction. The controlled design and code/ancillary outputs are positives; the next step is realistic shower, hadronization, detector, and nuisance studies.

An Introduction to Bayesian and Frequentist Simulation-Based Inference with Machine Learning

strong transfer

Maximilian Dax, Theo Heimel, Gilles Louppe. arXiv:2607.21702.

Method and result: This is a tutorial-style review of ML-based SBI, covering neural posterior estimation, neural likelihood estimation, Empirical Bayes, unfolding, validation, and limitations across Bayesian and frequentist inference.

Likely HEP/heavy-ion use case: Highly relevant to detector unfolding, parameter inference from simulators, global Bayesian model-to-data comparison, and emulator-based heavy-ion calibration.

Critical assessment: Strong as a practical orientation document rather than a new result. Its value is that it discusses validation and limitations explicitly, which is exactly where many physics-SBI applications are weakest.

Weak or Overclaimed Papers Worth Caution

SMEFT-Pheno-Agent: a natural-language-driven AI agent for machine-learning-assisted Standard Model Effective Field Theory phenomenology

caution

Yu-Chen Guo, Jie Wang, Ji-Chong Yang. arXiv:2607.22331.

The paper has some sensible workflow-safety ideas: locked physical configurations, phase manifests, and delegation of numerical work to MadGraph, Pythia, Delphes, and MLAnalysis. The caution is that the abstract emphasizes orchestration and auditability more than evidence that the agent improves physics reach, reduces errors in realistic analyses, or survives adversarial/edge-case natural-language requests. It is worth watching as infrastructure, but I would not count it as a strong scientific ML contribution yet.
