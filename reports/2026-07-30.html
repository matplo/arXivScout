Heavy-Ion Collision Papers

Theory

TheoryarXiv:2607.26851

Imaging non-hydrodynamic modes with jet wakes

Aleksi Kurkela, Ian Moult, Alexander Soloviev, Urs Achim Wiedemann (4 authors)

Summary: The paper argues that the angular structure of jet wakes can isolate non-hydrodynamic sectors of the QGP. In its long-wavelength analysis, hydrodynamics contributes mainly to low angular moments, while higher moments probe microscopic relaxation spectra through a kinetic-theory framework.

Why it matters: Direct observables for non-hydrodynamic modes are rare; this offers a concrete measurement target tied to jet wake imaging and QGP relaxation dynamics.

Critical assessment: Strong conceptually and authored by a technically credible group. The main caveat is experimental: measuring negative-wake higher moments precisely enough will be hard, and the observable must survive realistic backgrounds and jet reconstruction biases.

Abstract signal: The authors claim higher angular moments of the late-time jet-wake energy flux encode non-equilibrium relaxation spectra and motivate precision measurements of negative jet wakes.

Experiment

ExperimentarXiv:2607.26783

Measurement of the average transverse momentum of forward prompt charged particles in pp and pPb collisions at sqrt(sNN)=5.02 TeV

LHCb Collaboration: R. Aaij, M. Abdelfatah, A.S.W. Abdelmotteleb, C. Abellan Beteta, F. Abudinen, et al. (1129 authors)

Summary: LHCb reports first measurements of forward prompt charged-particle average transverse momentum versus multiplicity and pseudorapidity in pp and pPb at 5.02 TeV. The abstract states that average pT decreases with pseudorapidity, especially in high-multiplicity events, and that 3+1D hydrodynamic calculations reproduce the data while saturation models do not.

Why it matters: This is a forward-acceptance constraint on small-system collectivity and longitudinal structure, with direct relevance for pA initial-state versus final-state interpretations.

Critical assessment: Strong as an experimental result because it adds a distinctive forward observable and direct model comparisons. The physics interpretation should remain cautious until systematic details and model-parameter choices are inspected in the paper beyond the abstract.

Abstract signal: The measurement covers pp and pPb multiplicity and pseudorapidity dependence at LHCb and compares hydrodynamic and saturation-based descriptions.

Phenomenology

PhenomenologyarXiv:2607.26143

An Equilibrating Parton Shower for Jet Quenching and Medium Response

Ismail Soudi, Adam Takacs (2 authors)

Summary: The authors derive a parton shower from QCD effective kinetic theory that reproduces the full linearized Boltzmann equation while joining elastic/inelastic energy loss to equilibration near thermal scales. The framework connects jet evolution to hydrodynamic response, including wakes, Mach cones, and multiparticle correlations.

Why it matters: It addresses a real modeling gap: hard-parton showering and medium response are often treated with separate approximations, while this paper tries to keep them in one weak-coupling kinetic framework.

Critical assessment: Strong and methodologically substantive. It is still a compact theory paper, so practical impact depends on implementation in realistic event simulations and comparison to jet-substructure and wake-sensitive data.

Abstract signal: The shower recovers standard energy-loss/broadening at high energy and gives a consistent near-thermal description tied to hydrodynamic response.

PhenomenologyarXiv:2607.26912

Critical net-proton number fluctuations with hydrodynamics

Rui-zhe Zhao, Shi Yin, Shanjin Wu, Lipei Du, Xiaofeng Luo, Wei-jie Fu (6 authors)

Summary: This work computes net-proton cumulant ratios on hydrodynamic freeze-out hypersurfaces at nine beam energies from 7.7 to 200 GeV, using fRG-derived regular and critical fluctuations. It includes experimental pT/rapidity windows, isospin randomization, global baryon conservation, and a noncritical baseline.

Why it matters: Net-proton cumulants are central to the Beam Energy Scan critical-point program, and this paper tries to bring critical fluctuations into a more experiment-facing calculation.

Critical assessment: Worth highlighting, but mixed. The observable treatment is relevant and careful at abstract level, yet CEP signals are notoriously sensitive to mapping choices, dynamics, noncritical baselines, and conservation effects; the nonmonotonic C4/C2 result should not be read as standalone evidence.

Abstract signal: Critical effects remain small in low-order cumulants but grow for higher cumulants at lower collision energy, producing a nonmonotonic C4/C2 trend absent in the noncritical baseline.

PhenomenologyarXiv:2607.26758

Constraining alpha-cluster compactness in 16O and 20Ne at TeV energies using azimuthal anisotropy

Aswathy Menon Kavumpadikkal Radhakrishnan, Suraj Prasad, Neelkamal Mallick, Raghunath Sahoo, Gergely Gabor Barnafoldi (5 authors)

Summary: The paper uses IP-Glasma+MUSIC+iSS+UrQMD for O+O and Ne+Ne at 5.36 TeV, comparing Woods-Saxon and alpha-clustered densities with variable cluster size and separation. It focuses on v2{2} in ALICE, CMS, and ATLAS acceptances and compares to Run 3 measurements.

Why it matters: LHC light-ion data are now a serious probe of nuclear structure inputs, and this paper explores whether flow can constrain alpha-cluster compactness.

Critical assessment: Relevant and timely, with a strong model chain. The assessment is mixed-to-strong because extracting nuclear-structure parameters from v2 alone is vulnerable to initial-condition, viscosity, centrality, and nonflow systematics; the result is best viewed as a constraint study, not a final determination.

Abstract signal: OO v2 centrality trends and peak positions are reported to be especially sensitive to alpha-cluster geometry.

AI/ML Use

AI/ML usearXiv:2607.26131

Neural quantum states for non-Abelian lattice gauge theories with dynamical fermions

Gabriel Rouxinol, Julian Bender, Michele Grossi, Patrick Emonts, Jad C. Halimeh (5 authors)

Summary: The authors build a variational Monte Carlo framework for fully continuous SU(2) lattice gauge theory with dynamical staggered fermions, using a neural-network gauge wavefunction and a gauge-covariant Gaussian fermionic correction. They validate against strong-coupling perturbation theory and map a coarse phase diagram for L=4,6,8 systems.

Why it matters: Neural quantum states are a credible route for Hamiltonian lattice gauge theory beyond toy Abelian models; non-Abelian gauge fields with dynamical fermions are much closer to HEP-relevant problems.

Critical assessment: Strong transfer value for lattice and quantum-simulation workflows. It is not yet a QCD-scale solver, but the inclusion of dynamical fermions, gauge covariance, analytic fermion energy contributions, and explicit validation make it more than decorative ML.

Abstract signal: The method recovers expected strong-coupling behavior and identifies phase-transition structure via hysteresis analysis.

AI/ML Use Cases In Physics

Physics AI/MLarXiv:2607.26414

Origins and mitigation of double descent in reduced order modeling

Andrei A. Klishin, J. Nathan Kutz, Krithika Manohar (3 authors)

Method and result: The paper develops a Data-Noise Averaging theory for double descent in sparse sensing and reduced-order reconstruction, predicts risk curves cheaply, identifies unstable sensor combinations, and proposes regularization mechanisms. Demonstrations include sea-surface temperature reconstruction and a PDE reduced-order model.

Likely HEP/heavy-ion use case: Detector sparse readout, emulator design, and reduced-order hydrodynamic or transport surrogates where sensor placement and noisy partial observations can create brittle reconstructions.

Critical assessment: Strong transfer value because it diagnoses a concrete failure mode, not just a new architecture. Transfer still needs domain-specific tests on irregular detector geometries and event-by-event heavy-ion fields.

Physics AI/MLarXiv:2607.27062

PIKS: Universal Physics-Informed Kernel Methods

Joachim Bona-Pellissier, Giacomo Meanti, Matteo Santacesaria, Lorenzo Rosasco (4 authors)

Method and result: PIKS replaces PINN-style black-box optimization with physics-informed kernel estimators for linear differential constraints, proving universal consistency for universal kernels and deriving finite-sample bounds under source conditions. Numerical tests compare against PINNs and finite-element methods.

Likely HEP/heavy-ion use case: Small-data PDE-constrained inference, surrogate checks for hydrodynamics or detector-field reconstruction, and uncertainty-aware baselines where analytical tractability is more valuable than maximum flexibility.

Critical assessment: Strong as theory-facing ML, but transfer is conditional: linear constraints are easier than full nonlinear hydrodynamics or transport equations, so this is best viewed as a principled baseline and analysis tool.

Physics AI/MLarXiv:2607.26887

Conformalized Rate-Adaptive Sensing

Jiawei Yang, Yao Zhang (2 authors)

Method and result: CoRAS adaptively chooses acquisition/compression rates for image reconstruction while maintaining high-probability error control. It calibrates stopping-time estimates using images with similar early reconstruction behavior and reports target coverage with fewer measurements than fixed-rate rules.

Likely HEP/heavy-ion use case: Adaptive detector readout, online compression, and data-quality-aware acquisition where physics events differ sharply in reconstruction difficulty.

Critical assessment: Mixed-to-strong transfer value. The uncertainty-control framing is attractive, but the paper is image-dataset focused; HEP deployment would need non-image detector tests, distribution-shift studies, and trigger-latency constraints.
