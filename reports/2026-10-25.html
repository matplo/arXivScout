Executive takeaways

  • Best overall methodology: sequential SBI improves learned likelihood accuracy and calibration at fixed simulation budget, with both exact and reconstruction-level checks.
  • Best physics-constrained ML: the neutrino–nucleus study turns a known black-box generalization failure into a well-posed structure-function learning problem and tests oscillation-parameter bias.
  • Heavy-ion theory: massive-quasiparticle kinetic theory provides a useful nonconformal description of OO data, but manual tuning and its restricted EOS prevent precision claims.
  • Most actionable observable: the predicted sign flip of in-jet Λc+/D0 modifications could separate color reconnection from coalescence once a matched pp reference exists.
  • Foundation-model reality check: both TPC and neutrino-telescope results are promising, but neither yet demonstrates robust real-data or broad detector transfer.

Heavy-ion — Theory

Heavy-ion — Theorystrong

Nonconformal kinetic theory with transverse dynamics and event-by-event simulations using CoMBolt-RTA

Seyed Farid Taghavi

Summary. CoMBolt-RTA extends a boost-invariant transverse kinetic solver to massive quasiparticles, giving a nonconformal equation of state closer to lattice QCD than the massless version. The hybrid calculation describes several OO observables and predicts that central OO remains fluid-like while He–He systems may better expose the crossover toward particle-like dynamics.

Why it matters. This is a useful bridge between hydrodynamics and microscopic transport precisely where small-system collectivity makes either limiting picture questionable.

Critical assessment. Technically substantial and directly timely, but not yet a precision extraction: the quasiparticle mass is constant, the EOS is only approximate, shear and bulk sectors are linked by the RTA ansatz, and parameters are manually tuned rather than globally inferred.

Abstract-level screening note

A massive-quasiparticle relaxation-time Boltzmann solver is coupled to TRENTo and UrQMD, manually tuned to recent OO flow and multiplicity data, and used to study the transition from fluid-like to particle-like collective response.

Heavy-ion — Phenomenology

Heavy-ion — Phenomenologystrong

Charmed baryon-to-meson ratios inside jets as a discriminator of hadronization mechanisms: from color reconnection in p+p to coalescence in Pb+Pb

Zi-Xuan Xu, Wei Dai, Ben-Wei Zhang, Enke Wang

Summary. The proposed discriminator is the sign of the in-jet Λc+/D0 modification: enhancement from color reconnection in pp versus moderate suppression when PbPb coalescence products lose correlation with the jet. The accompanying longitudinal momentum fraction and radial profiles offer additional handles on non-fragmentation hadronization.

Why it matters. A sign-changing observable is considerably more useful experimentally than another model-dependent normalization shift, and it targets a central ambiguity in heavy-flavor hadronization.

Critical assessment. A sharp and testable phenomenology study. The main weakness is reference construction: no matched 5.02 TeV pp measurement exists, and some PbPb/pp ratios compare different hadronization schemes, so the clean discrimination awaits the proper experimental baseline.

Abstract-level screening note

The paper predicts opposite in-jet Λc+/D0 modifications for color reconnection in pp and coalescence in PbPb, supplemented by momentum-fraction and radial-profile observables.

Heavy-ion — Phenomenologymixed

Thermomagnetic enhancement of conserved-charge correlations in a lattice-constrained PNJL model

Bruno S. Lopes, Arthur E. B. Pasqualotto, Jandir J. Buttenbender Filho, Dyana C. Duarte, Ricardo L. S. Farias

Summary. The model uses lattice information from one chiral observable to constrain a temperature- and magnetic-field-dependent effective coupling. Without fitting charge correlations directly, it improves their magnetic response relative to fixed-coupling PNJL and predicts enhanced baryon–charge correlations.

Why it matters. It proposes experimentally relevant conserved-charge combinations as possible QCD magnetometers and tests whether lattice constraints propagate beyond the fitted observable.

Critical assessment. Interesting cross-observable validation, but conclusions remain model contingent. Agreement within an effective PNJL framework is not evidence that heavy-ion measurements can cleanly reconstruct the short-lived magnetic field; dynamical evolution and acceptance effects are outside the present study.

Abstract-level screening note

A Gaussian-process-inferred thermomagnetic coupling is fitted to lattice light-quark condensates and used in a three-flavor PNJL model to predict magnetic-field responses of charge correlations and finite-density Taylor coefficients.

Heavy-ion — AI/ML usemixed

Track finding and particle identification performance with particle physics foundation models

Shuhang Li, Haiwang Yu, Gabor Galgoczi, Yeonju Go, Yi Huang, et al. (11 authors total)

Summary. The pretrained model markedly improves low-pT track seeding over a cellular-automaton baseline and supports PID despite a pretraining objective centered on spatial pattern recognition. PID gains are modest: pion AUC improves from 0.774 to 0.792, while kaon performance is nearly tied.

Why it matters. Low-pT tracking and reusable detector representations are directly relevant to sPHENIX, EIC, and high-occupancy heavy-ion workflows.

Critical assessment. Promising evidence for representation reuse, not yet a general foundation-model result. Tests use one simulated detector subsystem; domain shift to data, cross-detector transfer, latency, and comparisons with stronger task-specific ML baselines remain open.

Abstract-level screening note

FM4NPP, pretrained on sPHENIX TPC spacepoints, is evaluated for track finding and particle identification using a frozen backbone and supervised adapters.

AI/ML use cases in physics

Physics AI/MLstrong

Machine Learning Neutrino-Nucleus Cross Sections II: More Leptons

Daniel C. Hackett, Joshua Isaacson, Shirley Weishi Li, Karla Tame-Narvaez, Michael L. Wagman

Summary. By learning Standard-Model structure functions rather than an unconstrained black box, the method recovers injected oscillation parameters without significant bias and approaches the constraints obtained with the exact cross section. The expanded analysis also exposes flux nuisance parameters as essential.

Why it matters. This is a good example of ML improving a hard nuclear-input problem by making physics structure part of the hypothesis class and propagating the result to end-to-end inference.

Critical assessment. Among the strongest papers today: it confronts a known generalization failure, uses physics constraints, and tests parameter bias. It remains a controlled synthetic study; robustness to realistic detector systematics and nuclear-model misspecification is the next decisive test.

Abstract-level screening note

A symmetry-structured learned cross-section model is extended to multiple lepton flavors and neutrino/antineutrino channels in a DUNE-inspired oscillation analysis.

Physics AI/MLstrong

Sequential SBI for Resonance Searches at the LHC

Henning Bahl, Ranit Das

Summary. The sequential procedure reallocates a fixed simulation budget toward the parameter region favored by observations. It yields more accurate and better-calibrated learned likelihoods than single-round training, with independent calibration and evaluation samples and both exact-likelihood and reconstruction-level tests.

Why it matters. Simulation cost is a binding constraint in collider inference; targeted generation can make likelihood-free mass and width scans materially more practical.

Critical assessment. Methodologically strong for an arXiv first version: exact-reference validation, realistic reconstruction examples, and explicit likelihood calibration. Remaining risks are coverage under multimodal or badly misspecified posteriors and the distance from full experimental systematics.

Abstract-level screening note

Sequential simulation-based inference concentrates event generation near data-supported mass and width regions and is tested against an exact parton-level likelihood plus two reconstruction-level resonance searches.

Physics AI/MLmixed

CURTAIN: Towards a Foundation Model for Neutrino Telescopes

Severin Magel, Felix J. Yu, Carlos A. Argüelles

Summary. CURTAIN improves monotonically with pretraining scale, reduces median angular and energy errors relative to training from scratch, and transfers to a new geometry. It also outperforms two matched-budget self-supervised baselines in the reported simulations.

Why it matters. Detector-to-detector transfer and label efficiency are exactly the properties a physics foundation model must demonstrate to justify its cost.

Critical assessment. The evaluation asks the right questions and shows unusually clear gains, but all results use simplified simulation, full fine-tuning, 100k labels, and single training runs. Real-data transfer, few-label behavior, and an ablation isolating temporal masking remain open.

Abstract-level screening note

A temporal-masking self-supervised objective is used to pretrain a neutrino-telescope encoder and is tested for direction and energy reconstruction, including transfer to an unseen detector geometry.

self-supervised learningneutrino telescopesdetector transferscaling
Physics AI/MLmixed

Action Learning for Scientific Simulations

Sarah Liaw, Yuanqi Du, Yilun Du

Summary. The method is tested on double-well dynamics, a 31-body orbital system, and alanine dipeptide trajectories, with multiple seeds and exact-action checks where available. It captures nonadditive path dependence and reuses one learned action across new objectives and conditions.

Why it matters. A reusable path objective could transfer to transport-event histories, rare-transition sampling, or constrained surrogate dynamics in nuclear and HEP simulations.

Critical assessment. Conceptually strong and carefully tested, but HEP transfer is speculative. Performance requires overlap between reference and observed path distributions; state-dependent diffusion and projection-induced memory complicate the formal guarantees.

Abstract-level screening note

LEAP learns a differentiable stochastic action over complete trajectories by estimating a correction to a tractable reference process, then reuses it for path ranking, optimization, constraints, and sampling.

UrQMD-Agent: An LLM-Assisted Framework for Microscopic Tracing of Proton Azimuthal Correlations

Fu-Peng Li, Yige Huang

Summary. The deterministic pair-kernel and event-history analysis may be scientifically useful, especially the localization of negative correlations to decay-fed proton pairs. However, the paper does not isolate what the LLM improves relative to a scripted workflow or an expert using the same tools.

Critical assessment. Worth watching as workflow engineering, but the AI claim is currently under-validated. Reproducibility across models/prompts, error rates in planning and interpretation, and blinded comparisons with non-agent baselines are needed before treating this as a scientific-method advance.

A general-purpose LLM is connected to deterministic UrQMD analysis tools and used to help plan and interpret a study of proton azimuthal-partition fluctuations.

Multiplicity Distribution in the Color Glass Condensate Formalism

H. R. Martins-Fontes

Summary. The fits may be serviceable, but a two-component negative-binomial mixture remains flexible and largely phenomenological. Low chi-squared alone does not establish that the shoulder structure has been derived from CGC dynamics.

Critical assessment. The manuscript appears incremental and its language is stronger than the demonstrated predictive content. Independent-energy predictions, uncertainty propagation, and comparisons with modern generators or alternative mixtures would be needed to elevate the claim.
