Selected Papers

Are hybrid hydrodynamic models missing soft-pion physics?

Authors: Osvaldo Ferreira, Thiago S. Domingues, Fernando G. Gardim, et al. (13 authors). Category: Heavy-ion phenomenology. Assessment: strong.

A Bayesian analysis of a hybrid hydrodynamic model finds a low-pT pion excess relative to ALICE data that is not shared by kaons or protons. Two one-parameter enhancement templates are tested; the infrared template improves the calibration and mean-pT agreement.

Why it matters: Soft-pion discrepancies can bias extraction of medium parameters and point toward missing late-stage physics.

Critical assessment: A careful model-data study with a broad parameter analysis and an afterburner. The fitted templates do not identify a unique microscopic cause; conclusions remain conditional on the model, priors, and data treatment.

Abstract, paraphrased: The authors scan the hybrid-model parameter space, confirm a low-pT pion excess even with a hadronic afterburner, examine centrality scaling and test two candidate enhancement mechanisms.

Searching for signatures of criticality in heavy-ion collisions using two-pion correlation functions

Authors: Alejandro Ayala, Santiago Bernal-Langarica, Máté Csanád, et al. (14 authors). Category: Heavy-ion phenomenology. Assessment: mixed.

EPOS simulations with a critical-point equation of state examine Lévy-fitted two-pion sources in Bi+Bi collisions at 5.8–11 GeV. The authors find no unambiguous critical signature; resonance contributions may affect the intercept trend.

Why it matters: It tests a proposed experimental critical-point observable and illustrates an important background.

Critical assessment: A useful cautionary feasibility study, not evidence for or against a critical point. A matched no-critical-point baseline and varied critical strength are needed to establish sensitivity.

Abstract, paraphrased: The study fits simulated pair-distance distributions and momentum correlations to Lévy forms across collision energies using an equation of state containing a critical endpoint.

Learning and Generating Time Trajectories of Multidimensional Stochastic Processes

Authors: Zihan Xu, Changho Kim, Andy J. Nonaka, Yuanran Zhu (4 authors). Category: AI/ML in physics. Assessment: mixed.

A noise-driven recurrent network learns joint statistics using projected distributions and adaptive loss balancing. It is tested on coupled Duffing dynamics, a Fermi–Pasta–Ulam chain and a lattice kinetic Monte Carlo model, with finite-sample-normalized validation.

Why it matters: A possible surrogate for ensembles of coupled Langevin trajectories in heavy-quark transport or fluctuating hydrodynamics, subject to domain-specific validation.

Critical assessment: Methodologically interesting with several benchmarks, but no heavy-ion demonstration. Finite projections can miss joint or rare-event structure; conservation, uncertainty, tails, speed and out-of-distribution robustness require dedicated testing.

Abstract, paraphrased: The paper extends statistics-informed neural networks to multidimensional processes and studies projected distributions, adaptive training, noise dimension, and statistical validation across three stochastic benchmarks.
