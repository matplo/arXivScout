Daily arXiv Physics Scout · 10 November 2026

Heavy-ion physics and transferable AI/ML

System-size dependence of jet quenching, high-baryon-density cumulants, hadronic effects on charm, recombination, domain robustness, and test-beam sensor reconstruction define the selected literature.

Executive takeaways

  • The most consequential new heavy-ion paper uses one DREENA parameter set from oxygen through lead and supplies concrete harmonic, fluctuation, and flavor tests rather than relying only on inclusive suppression.
  • The JAM/CIGAR study is a valuable noncritical reference for 2.4 GeV proton cumulants: centrality construction, acceptance, mean fields, and light-nucleus formation all compete with critical-point interpretations.
  • Hadronic rescattering leaves integrated D-meson ratios nearly unchanged but can reshape differential spectra and v2, reinforcing that late-stage dynamics cannot be ignored in charm transport extraction.
  • The AC-LGAD transformer is the strongest ML result because it is validated on 120 GeV pion test-beam data against calibrated analytic baselines.
  • Domain-adaptive GNNs address a real HEP deployment problem, but their gains are demonstrated only on synthetic perturbations; real simulation-to-data closure remains open.
  • Two papers warrant caution: one quotes CP-sensitivity gains with a parameterized detector response, and one assigns physical weight to nonextensive critical temperatures without adequate lattice-QCD calibration.

Heavy-ion collision papers

Theory

Elliptic flow of phi and Omega via quark recombination mechanism in relativistic heavy-ion collisions

mixed

Zhidong Yang, Lie-Wen Chen · 2 authors · arXiv:2611.11347

quark recombinationphi mesonOmega baryonNCQ scaling

Summary. Tests recombination and NCQ scaling for multi-strange hadrons over an unusually broad energy range and highlights where interpreting scaled hadron flow as partonic flow fails.

Abstract. A quark-recombination model with strange-quark phase space parameterized by a viscous blast wave describes phi and Omega elliptic flow from 7.7 GeV to 5.02 TeV. The analysis finds approximate constituent-quark scaling, but at LHC energies the extracted strange-quark v2 differs appreciably from naively scaled hadron v2.

Why it matters. Phi and Omega have clean strange-quark content and relatively small hadronic cross sections, making them useful hadronization probes across the RHIC-to-LHC energy span.

Critical assessment. Interesting and potentially useful, but the broad claim of recombination validity rests on a parameterized blast-wave input and a narrow observable set. Agreement with v2 alone does not uniquely establish constituent-quark degrees of freedom.

Phenomenology

From oxygen to lead: testing a common QGP description with high-pT probes

strong

Bithika Karmakar, Dusan Zigic, Magdalena Djordjevic · 3 authors · arXiv:2611.12404

system-size scanDREENAjet quenchinghard-soft correlations

Summary. Uses one microscopic energy-loss parameter set across O+O, Ne+Ne, Xe+Xe, and Pb+Pb and turns the common-QGP hypothesis into a suite of falsifiable high-pT predictions, including v2/(1-RAA), v3, cumulant ratios, and D/B mesons.

Abstract. The observation of suppression and finite high-pT elliptic anisotropy in O+O and Ne+Ne raises the question of whether a common QGP description can simultaneously account for both observables across systems from oxygen to lead. The authors couple event-by-event bulk evolution constrained by soft observables to DREENA radiative and collisional energy loss, using the same microscopic parameter set for O+O, Ne+Ne, Xe+Xe, and Pb+Pb. They reproduce the ordering of suppression and predict system-, harmonic-, fluctuation-, and flavor-dependent tests.

Why it matters. A common description across four system sizes is substantially more constraining than retuning each collision system, and the proposed observables can test whether soft-sector geometry fluctuations propagate consistently into hard probes.

Critical assessment. Strong phenomenology with a coherent cross-system design and concrete predictions. The present evidence is still model-conditional: agreement with the ordering of suppression does not uniquely validate DREENA, and several headline observables await measurement.

Proton Number Fluctuations in Au+Au and Ag+Ag Collisions at sqrt(sNN)=2.4 GeV: A JAM Model Study

strong

Yongcong Xu, Zhaohui Wang, Yu Zhang, Xiaofeng Luo · 4 authors · arXiv:2611.12086

proton cumulantsJAMCIGARhigh baryon density

Summary. Builds a noncritical hadronic-transport baseline for high-baryon-density fluctuation measurements and explicitly tests centrality, acceptance, mean-field, and light-nucleus effects.

Abstract. Proton-number cumulants and factorial cumulants through fourth order are studied in Au+Au and Ag+Ag collisions at 2.4 GeV using JAM in cascade and mean-field modes. The CIGAR procedure closely reproduces a participant-number reference and outperforms multiplicity-based centrality treatment; mean fields, rapidity acceptance, and a simplified light-nucleus removal prescription materially affect cumulant ratios.

Why it matters. Critical-point searches require a trustworthy background reference. Demonstrating that centrality construction and cluster formation can move fourth-order observables is directly relevant to HADES/CBM-style analyses.

Critical assessment. Methodologically strong because CIGAR is checked against a participant-number reference and several confounders are separated. The light-nucleus treatment is intentionally schematic and the result remains a JAM-dependent baseline, not a quantitative uncertainty envelope.

Hadronic final-state effects on open-charm production at LHC energies

strong

Taesoo Song, Elena Bratkovskaya, Krzysztof Redlich · 3 authors · arXiv:2611.11569

open charmPHSDhadronic rescatteringelliptic flow

Summary. Separates open-charm observables that are robust against the hadronic stage from those that receive material late-stage modifications.

Abstract. PHSD calculations with and without charm-hadron rescattering are compared with ALICE measurements and statistical-hadronization expectations. Integrated relative D-meson yields are largely insensitive to hadronic interactions, while low- and intermediate-pT spectra and elliptic flow are modified; the model also reproduces the system trend of Ds+/D0.

Why it matters. QGP transport extractions often treat the hadronic phase as a correction. This result identifies differential charm observables for which that correction is not negligible.

Critical assessment. A useful controlled switch-off study tied to ALICE data and an established transport framework. Its reach is limited by the short paper, one transport implementation, and the absence of a broad parameter or model uncertainty analysis.

AI/ML use cases in physics

Test beam results for machine-learning-based reconstruction of resistive silicon sensors

strong

Don C. Wong, Leena Diehl, Spencer Ellis, et al. · 17 authors · arXiv:2611.10194

AC-LGADtest beamtransformer4D tracking

Summary. Validates learned waveform reconstruction on a real SPS beam with calibrated telescope resolution and meaningful analytic baselines.

Abstract. A topology-agnostic transformer reconstructs positions from full AC-LGAD waveforms measured in a 120 GeV pion test beam and is compared with a parameter-free charge-imbalance estimator and fitted matrix inversion. With an in-situ-calibrated 2.5 micrometer telescope pointing resolution, the transformer improves median position error by up to a factor of three over the full active pad; all methods converge in the charge-sharing core.

Why it matters. The method directly addresses nonlinear, below-pitch position reconstruction for candidate 4D tracking sensors and is transferable to high-rate collider and heavy-ion tracking systems.

Critical assessment. The strongest ML paper in this batch because evaluation uses real beam data, calibrated reference tracking, and analytic baselines. The gain is spatially nonuniform, and generalization beyond one 2x2-pad device and its operating conditions remains untested.

Domain Adaptive Graph Neural Networks for Neutrino Experiments

mixed

Hemalata Nayak, Giuseppe Cerati, Aleksandra Ciprijanovic, Tammy Walton · 4 authors · arXiv:2611.10057

domain adaptationGNNLArTPCsimulation-to-data

Summary. Targets the simulation-to-data mismatch in LArTPC reconstruction using adversarial domain adaptation and reports substantial recovery under four controlled shifts.

Abstract. Adversarial domain adaptation is added to GNN reconstruction trained on the MicroBooNE open dataset. Four unlabeled target domains are synthesized using scale, noise, and position-dependent signal perturbations. Target-domain precision improves by as much as 0.19 and recall by 0.37 without a substantial source-domain penalty.

Why it matters. Domain shift is one of the main obstacles to deploying ML in HEP and nuclear detectors; the same strategy could be tested for tracking, calorimetry, and heavy-ion event reconstruction.

Critical assessment. Promising and correctly focused on robustness, but the target domains are engineered perturbations rather than real detector data. Results are workshop-level evidence of stress-test recovery, not yet proof of simulation-to-data closure.

Weak or overclaimed papers worth caution

Machine learning assisted neutrino/antineutrino discrimination in atmospheric neutrino events for CPV sensitivity in a 20-kton liquid scintillator detector

caution

Dek Hao Liew, Mohammad Nizam, Shu Tian Eu, Siew Yan Hoh · 4 authors · arXiv:2611.11474

atmospheric neutrinosXGBoostCP violationparameterized detector

Summary. The proposed statistical separation is plausible, but the claimed sensitivity gain is obtained with a simplified detector response.

Abstract. GENIE events in a JUNO-like geometry are passed through parameterized smearing, and logistic regression, XGBoost, and an MLP use inelasticity, neutron multiplicity, and lepton energy for statistical neutrino/antineutrino separation. XGBoost reaches AUC near 0.83 and improves an approximate peak CP-violation sensitivity, with nuisance parameters marginalized.

Critical assessment. The authors appropriately call the results preliminary. Until neutron response, event reconstruction, backgrounds, and correlated detector/systematic uncertainties are validated in a full simulation, the quoted significance gains should not be treated as an experimental projection.

Critical Temperature in Nonextensive Hadron Models: A Pressure-based Approach

caution

M. A. A. Ahmed, H. Zainuddin · 2 authors · arXiv:2611.10079

Tsallis statisticscritical temperaturebag modelsnonextensive thermodynamics

Summary. The mathematical root structure is clear, but its connection to the QCD crossover in heavy-ion matter is not adequately established.

Abstract. A pressure equation derived from Tsallis entropy is explored for several bag-model variants. The inferred transition temperatures depend strongly on the nonextensive parameter and model term, with quoted values well below the modern lattice-QCD crossover scale for physical quark masses; multiple mathematical roots can appear at finite volume.

Critical assessment. The paper combines phenomenological nonextensivity and model-dependent bag terms without a convincing calibration to lattice thermodynamics or a controlled uncertainty analysis. Calling the resulting weighted value a critical temperature risks giving model algebra more physical authority than the validation supports.
