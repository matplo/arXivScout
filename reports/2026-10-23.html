Heavy-ion collision physics and transferable AI/ML methods, selected for scientific strength and practical relevance.

Executive takeaways

  • The clearest near-term heavy-ion AI result is the sPHENIX TPC foundation model: it improves difficult low-pT and looping-track reconstruction while retaining high purity, although only on simulation from one detector subsystem.
  • The most transferable ML methodology is the neutrino-nucleus study: it turns an ill-posed black-box problem into an identifiable one by imposing Standard-Model structure and fitting flux nuisances jointly.
  • CoMBolt-RTA is a meaningful event-by-event kinetic-theory advance and improves mean-pT behavior, but its small-system conclusions remain calibration hypotheses because the tuning is manual and incomplete.
  • Jet-tagged Lambda_c/D0 offers a clean sign-level discriminator between color reconnection and thermal coalescence, making it the strongest heavy-ion phenomenology proposal in this release.
  • Sequential SBI and Stein-witness anomaly detection are credible HEP methods; the former has stronger validation, while the latter still rests on one synthetic benchmark family.
  • UrQMD-Agent is worth cautioning on: its deterministic physics analysis may be useful, but the paper does not demonstrate that the LLM layer adds scientific value.

Nonconformal kinetic theory with transverse dynamics and event-by-event simulations using CoMBolt-RTA

Seyed Farid Taghavi

Summary. CoMBolt is extended from a massless setup to massive quasiparticles in RTA, yielding a more realistic nonconformal equation of state and better mean-pT behavior in an event-by-event TrENTo plus transport plus UrQMD chain. The tuned model places central OO on the fluid-like side and predicts helium systems nearer the fluid-to-particle transition.

Why it matters. It advances a rare framework that spans free streaming, kinetic evolution, and near-hydrodynamic behavior in realistic transverse geometry, directly addressing the interpretation of collectivity in light systems.

Critical assessment. Substantive but not yet a precision result. The model is compared with several recent OO observables, yet the parameter scan is manual, poor points are not shown, no tested tuning describes the full centrality range, and the full lattice-QCD EOS is not implemented. The helium-system conclusion should be treated as a hypothesis for Bayesian calibration, not a robust prediction.

Abstract

A new version of CoMBolt, called CoMBolt-RTA, is introduced to solve the Boltzmann equation for massive quasiparticles in the relaxation time approximation (RTA). The inclusion of a constant quasiparticle mass allows one to obtain an equation of state (EOS) closer to that of lattice QCD. The nonequilibrium dynamics of a boost-invariant, nonconformal system with transverse spatial extension are studied, with particular emphasis on the effects of quasiparticle mass on the evolution toward equilibrium. Using the hybrid version, which includes TrENTo for the initial state and UrQMD as an afterburner, the model parameters are manually tuned to describe recent anisotropic-flow and charged-multiplicity measurements in OO collisions by the ALICE Collaboration. The resulting predictions for the pT-spectrum and mean pT are also compared with ATLAS measurements. Based on the preferred parameter tuning, the collective response as a function of opacity is investigated. The results suggest that central OO collisions remain in the fluid-like regime, while smaller systems, namely 3He-3He and 4He-4He collisions, could provide a better opportunity to investigate the transition from fluid-like to particle-like collective dynamics.

Anisotropic spin hydrodynamics in a magnetized medium: entropy-current formulation and minimal linear modes

Yiwei Qiu, Duan She, Defu Hou

Summary. A formal Israel-Stewart construction resolves spin susceptibilities and transport into longitudinal, transverse, and mixed channels in a magnetized relativistic medium. Linear-mode analysis exposes failures of the first-order truncation and shows stable sampled behavior after adding relaxation times.

Why it matters. Spin polarization and early magnetic fields remain active heavy-ion questions; a causal anisotropic effective theory is needed before phenomenological extraction is credible.

Critical assessment. Careful and technically useful, but preliminary. The paper is admirably explicit that it does not establish general stability or strong hyperbolicity. The closure is restricted, the equilibrium is unpolarized, explicit spin-magnetization coupling is omitted, and the parameter scan is illustrative rather than QCD-calibrated.

Abstract

We formulate anisotropic relativistic spin hydrodynamics in a parity-even magnetized medium using an Israel-Stewart entropy-current construction. Treating the magnetic field at leading order in the gradient expansion reduces the rotational symmetry to SO(2) and separates spin susceptibilities and dissipative transport into longitudinal, transverse, and mixed channels. A restricted quadratic entropy current yields transient equations for the retained dissipative stresses and spin fluxes. We analyze a minimal linear closure around an unpolarized homogeneous equilibrium, retaining dynamical magnetic-flux perturbations but no explicit spin-magnetization coupling; spin and magnetic fluctuations mix through the fluid variables. Infrared coefficient equations and ultraviolet characteristic expansions organize the magnetosonic and Alfven-like spectra together with spectator spin-flux poles. The first-order truncation exhibits unstable boost-sector poles and superlinear large-wave-number branches. For baseline parameters, finite relaxation times yield asymptotically linear propagation branches and finite relaxation poles, with no growing modes found on the sampled grid. The characteristic speeds provide necessary light-cone diagnostics, without establishing general stability or strong hyperbolicity.

Charmed baryon-to-meson ratios inside jets as a discriminator of hadronization mechanisms: from color reconnection in p+p to coalescence in Pb+Pb

Zi-Xuan Xu, Wei Dai, Ben-Wei Zhang, Enke Wang

Summary. The paper identifies a sign-level discriminator: color reconnection in p+p enhances in-jet Λc/D0 relative to inclusive production, whereas thermal-parton coalescence in Pb+Pb suppresses it because uncorrelated soft partners are removed by the jet requirement. It adds parallel momentum-fraction and radial-profile predictions.

Why it matters. The observable is experimentally concrete and addresses a live ambiguity in charm hadronization across small and large systems.

Critical assessment. Strong phenomenological proposal, not a model-independent conclusion. The opposite-sign mechanism test is unusually clear and the authors discuss experimental caveats. Still, the comparison spans different model frameworks, the current p+p reference at 5.02 TeV is missing, Pb+Pb background subtraction is absent, and existing uncertainties do not yet discriminate the p+p scenarios.

Abstract

Jets separate the products of hard scatterings from the underlying event and thus help to clarify the origin of the enhanced baryon-to-meson ratios observed from small to large collision systems. We investigate the charmed baryon-to-meson ratio Λc+/D0 within jets in p+p and Pb+Pb collisions at sqrt(sNN)=5.02 TeV to discriminate between candidate hadronization mechanisms. In p+p collisions, simulated with PYTHIA8 using color reconnection as a representative beyond-leading-color scenario, the in-jet ratio is enhanced at low-to-intermediate pT relative to the inclusive one. In Pb+Pb collisions, described by the SHELL model with a coalescence plus fragmentation hybrid hadronization, the in-jet ratio is instead suppressed moderately at intermediate pT. The sign of the in-jet modification thus discriminates between the two mechanisms. Both mechanisms shift charmed hadrons toward small parallel jet momentum fraction, with a stronger effect on Λc+, while the radial profile rises with distance from the jet axis.

Track finding and particle identification performance with particle physics foundation models

Shuhang Li, Haiwang Yu, Gabor Galgoczi, et al. (11 authors)

Summary. A frozen pretrained backbone is tested on simulated sPHENIX TPC spacepoints for track finding and particle identification. It substantially improves low-pT and looping-track efficiency over a cellular-automaton seeder while maintaining high purity; a downstream PID adapter is comparable to conventional dE/dx Gaussian-process regression.

Why it matters. This is directly relevant to heavy-ion reconstruction, where high occupancy and low-pT tracks are central rather than peripheral edge cases.

Critical assessment. Strong, with an important caveat. The task-specific baselines and downstream transfer test make this more than a generic foundation-model demonstration. However, the evidence is simulation-only, from one detector subsystem and one operating domain; cross-detector transfer, data mismodeling, throughput, and memory cost remain untested.

Abstract

Modern high energy nuclear and particle physics experiments produce large amounts of sparse data that must be reduced significantly in size before performing physics analysis. Recently, there has been an effort to evaluate Foundation Models as a potential tool for performing data analysis. These models have been shown to learn features in high dimensional datasets based on the multi-modal data they are trained on. To show their effectiveness at performing complex pattern recognition tasks, such as reconstructing charged particles in a magnetic field, we evaluated the performance of a Foundation Model based reconstruction on several downstream tasks with a dataset composed of spacepoints in a Time Projection Chamber. We show that the performance of the Foundation Model based data analysis significantly improves upon that of traditional methods, and furthermore can be adapted to learn additional tasks that are not necessarily related to the initial training objective.

Machine Learning Neutrino-Nucleus Cross Sections II: More Leptons

Daniel C. Hackett, Joshua Isaacson, Shirley Weishi Li, Karla Tame-Narvaez, Michael L. Wagman

Summary. The authors extend a symmetry-constrained, data-driven cross-section model to electron and muon flavors, neutrino and antineutrino modes, and a simultaneous three-parameter oscillation fit. In controlled DUNE-inspired pseudo-data, the learned model recovers the injected parameters without significant bias once flux nuisances are included.

Why it matters. The transferable lesson is excellent: use known tensor and symmetry structure to make a learning problem identifiable, then propagate nuisance parameters jointly. That design principle applies directly to nuclear and heavy-ion emulators and inverse problems.

Critical assessment. Strong within a controlled closure study. It has a clear failure mode for black-box learning, a principled remedy, multi-channel validation, and explicit flux-systematics tests. Real detector response, broader generator mismatch, and genuinely out-of-family nuclear effects still need stress tests.

Abstract

Neutrino-nucleus cross sections are essential inputs for long-baseline neutrino oscillation experiments, yet remain challenging to model with high precision. Recent work demonstrated that fully black-box data-driven cross-section models cannot accurately generalize between near- and far-detector fluxes, but imposing Standard-Model symmetries via structure-function parameterizations provides a well-posed learning problem in a simplified DUNE-inspired muon-neutrino disappearance analysis. Here, we extend this framework to multiple lepton flavors and both neutrino and antineutrino beam modes, incorporating the electron appearance channel and enabling a simultaneous determination of sin²θ23, Δm²31, and the CP-violating phase δCP. We find that the learned cross section model recovers the injected oscillation parameters without significant bias, with constraints comparable to those obtained using the exact underlying cross section. Flux uncertainties have more significant effects in the multi-channel case, and incorporating flux nuisance parameters into the learning problem is required for accurate results. Our results demonstrate that this data-driven approach can be extended to the multi-channel measurements needed for long-baseline neutrino oscillation analyses.

AI/ML usestrong

Sequential SBI for Resonance Searches at the LHC

Henning Bahl, Ranit Das

Summary. Sequential neural likelihood training concentrates simulations in the posterior-relevant region for resonance masses and widths. Against an exact parton-level likelihood and two reconstruction-level studies, it improves accuracy and calibration over one-shot training at fixed simulation budget.

Why it matters. Adaptive simulation allocation is directly transferable to expensive event-generator calibration, detector tuning, and heavy-ion parameter inference.

Critical assessment. Strong methodological evidence: an exact-reference test, two reconstruction-level cases, fixed-budget comparison, and calibration diagnostics. The remaining gap is deployment realism: experimental nuisance dimensions, simulator misspecification, and repeated-data/look-elsewhere workflows are not yet demonstrated.

Abstract

Neural simulation-based inference has been shown to extract significantly more information from LHC data than histogram-based analyses. So far, it has mostly been applied to couplings, for which the polynomial structure of the scattering amplitude can be exploited. Masses and widths instead move and deform the signal in phase space, and learning their likelihood requires many event samples covering the continuous theory parameter space. We show how sequential simulation-based inference can address this by iteratively concentrating the simulations where the observed data points. Using a parton-level example with an exact reference likelihood and reconstruction-level analyses of A to ZH to llbb and Y to HH to (tt)(tt), we demonstrate that sequential training produces significantly more accurate and better calibrated likelihoods than single-round training with the same amount of training events.

RIDDLE: Revealing Irregularities through Distributional Discrepancies in Latent-space via Stein-Witness Estimation

Ali Eren Simsek

Summary. RIDDLE replaces a binary classifier with a learned scalar Stein witness trained against a sideband-derived background model. On LHC Olympics dijets it approaches an idealized benchmark while largely preserving the background mass shape, including tests with deliberately correlated inputs.

Why it matters. Data-driven anomaly tests with explicit control of mass sculpting are relevant to collider searches and could transfer to localized heavy-ion fluctuation or rare-topology searches.

Critical assessment. Promising, but the evidence is narrower than the headline. The construction and stress tests are thoughtful; validation is nevertheless limited to one simulated benchmark family, one author, and a learned sideband reference. Sensitivity to real detector nonstationarity, mismodeled tails, and multiple-testing procedures remains open.

Abstract

RIDDLE is a new method for resonant anomaly detection that uses a scalar Stein-witness function to identify discrepancies between unlabeled signal-region events and a background distribution inferred from dijet-mass sidebands. The witness is optimized in a latent representation of auxiliary jet observables against a differentiable background reference, without requiring an explicit signal model. The method is evaluated using simulated LHC Olympics dijet events and compared with idealized and supervised benchmarks that share the same statistical construction but have access to additional signal or background information. RIDDLE demonstrates strong signal-background discrimination comparable to the idealized benchmark, while largely preserving the background dijet-mass spectrum under anomaly-score selection. Tests incorporating additional angular information and deliberately mass-correlated features further demonstrate robustness under the studied conditions.

UrQMD-Agent: An LLM-Assisted Framework for Microscopic Tracing of Proton Azimuthal Correlations

Fu-Peng Li, Yige Huang

Summary. The deterministic analysis finds negative proton azimuthal-partition correlations in UrQMD at 3-27 GeV and traces them mainly to pairs involving decay-fed protons. An LLM plans and interprets the workflow while deterministic tools execute the numerical work.

Why it matters. The physics decomposition may be useful, and tool-mediated provenance is a sensible architecture for scientific agents.

Critical assessment. Caution on the AI claim, not necessarily the transport result. There is no controlled comparison showing that the LLM improves correctness, efficiency, reproducibility, or scientific discovery over a scripted workflow or expert analyst. Model identity, prompt sensitivity, failure rate, cost, and independent replication are not evaluated; the substantive evidence comes from conventional deterministic UrQMD analysis.

Abstract

We present an LLM-assisted framework for microscopic transport studies that connects a general-purpose large language model to the UrQMD transport model through a model-specific Skill and a deterministic scientific tool layer. The LLM supports literature-guided scientific planning and interpretation, while numerical calculations, event-history reconstruction, and statistical analyses are performed by deterministic tools. As a demonstration, the framework is applied to proton azimuthal-partition fluctuations in Au+Au collisions at sqrt(sNN)=3.0-39 GeV. In the 0-5% centrality class, the fluctuations are statistically resolved below zero from 3 to 27 GeV and consistent with zero at 39 GeV. An exact pair-kernel decomposition shows that the negative signal is carried mainly by proton pairs containing at least one decay-fed proton. A random-label null test further distinguishes the observed pair contributions from expectations based on pair abundance alone.
