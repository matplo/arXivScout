Experimentmixed

Measurements of local polarization of Λ hyperons with event shape engineering in Au+Au collisions at RHIC-STAR

Taiki Kondo (for the STAR Collaboration)

Summary. STAR makes the first direct event-shape-engineered test of whether local Λ polarization tracks elliptic flow at fixed multiplicity. The reported positive slope is about 2.5σ, so this is evidence of a correlation, not a settled separation of shear- and vorticity-driven mechanisms.

Why it matters. The construction is experimentally well targeted to a long-standing ambiguity in spin hydrodynamics and directly links a polarization observable to a controlled flow selection.

Critical assessment. Scientifically useful but not yet strong evidence: it is a five-page proceedings result, the key trend is only about 2.5σ, and the short presentation limits systematic-detail scrutiny. Follow-up with full uncertainties and model discrimination will determine its weight.

Abstract

Anisotropic flow is expected to generate local vorticities along the beam direction, which in turn lead to local polarization of produced particles. Since shear-induced and vorticity-induced contributions may oppose one another, STAR uses event-shape engineering to measure local polarization at fixed multiplicity as elliptic flow changes. In Au+Au collisions at √sNN = 19.6 GeV, v2 rises clearly with q2 while Pz,2 shows a weak positive dependence on q2 and v2.

Jets as differential probes of hot QCD matter

João M. Silva

Summary. A substantial thesis synthesis connects anisotropic broadening and spin information to experimentally legible energy-flow observables, then extends the treatment beyond a single medium-induced splitting. Its value is the coherent observable program rather than one isolated headline result.

Why it matters. Energy correlators and anisotropic jet substructure are promising routes to constrain microscopic medium properties while retaining perturbative control and differential information.

Critical assessment. Strong and technically broad, though it is a thesis consolidating several studies rather than a single new validation. Experimental robustness, hadronization/background sensitivity, and end-to-end extraction of transport parameters remain the practical tests.

Abstract

This thesis develops perturbative-QCD tools for extracting hot-medium information from differential jet substructure. It treats direction-dependent momentum broadening, heavy-flavour and azimuthally differential energy correlators, quantile-based energy-loss interpretations, spin correlations between emissions, and medium response in higher-point correlators.

Parameter dependence of the mapping of the mean-field Ising model onto QCD in the first-order phase transition regime

Xue Pan; Yuepeng Li

Summary. The paper cleanly demonstrates how arbitrary mapping scales reshape the coexistence/spinodal geometry and thermodynamic contributions relevant to low-energy heavy-ion simulations.

Why it matters. It quantifies a real source of model dependence in critical-point equations of state, useful when interpreting hydrodynamic calculations near a putative first-order region.

Critical assessment. A sensitivity study, not a constraint: it fixes the critical point at μBC = 630 MeV, uses mean-field critical behavior, explores selected mapping angles, and does not propagate the variations through dynamical heavy-ion observables. Useful diagnostic work, but modest scientific reach.

Abstract

The authors map a mean-field Ising equation of state onto the QCD T–μB plane and vary the scale parameters w and ρ for conventional and small-quark-mass mapping schemes. Increasing either parameter narrows metastable and unstable regions, while the pressure and density response depends on the mapping geometry.

AI/ML use cases in physics

AI/ML usestrong

A Radiation-Hardened, Fault-Aware Quantized Neural Network That Self-Heals

Peter Xiangyuan Ma; Joshua S. Bloom

Method and result. A concrete hardware-facing ML method for maintaining quantized inference under radiation-induced bit flips, with explicit comparisons to error-correction baselines and unusually clear engineering caveats.

HEP/NP use case. Directly transferable to trigger, detector-control, accelerator, and radiation-exposed edge inference where model memory can accumulate corruption and redundancy is expensive.

Transfer assessment. Strong methodology for the stated regime, but not yet a deployment guarantee: the tests are simulated, runtime arithmetic is roughly 2–3× during healing, clean RMSE pays a median 0.3% and mean ~2% tax with a much worse outlier, and benefit is limited when single-bit upsets dominate.

Abstract

Projection Healing aligns quantized-network weights and activations with a low-rank subspace during fault-aware training, then reconstructs flagged corrupted coordinates at runtime without persistent model replicas. Across six regression benchmarks it roughly doubles the acceptable scrub interval in the stated error regimes and improves over SECDED when multi-bit upsets dominate.

AI/ML usestrong

This is SParTA: A modular, efficient and scalable transformer architecture for jet-tagging

Rajneil Baruah; Subhadeep Mondal; Sunando Kumar Patra; Satyajit Roy

Method and result. The hard-core/soft-radiation factorization is physically motivated and substantially reduces parameter count while preserving competitive jet-tagging performance.

HEP/NP use case. The architecture is immediately relevant to collider classification and potentially to high-multiplicity heavy-ion jets, where constituent count and inference cost are acute.

Transfer assessment. Benchmarking is careful, including ten trainings and uncertainty discussion. Still, it is not universally best: PELICAN wins the quoted quark–gluon metrics, several models beat it on top tagging, and SParTA receives 53 global features unavailable to most comparators, so some headline gains are not an input-matched architectural test.

Abstract

SParTA applies quadratic pairwise attention only to the hardest N jet constituents and aggregates softer constituents with linear cross-attention. A 300k-parameter model matches the quark–gluon AUC of a 2.14M-parameter Particle Transformer and improves IAFormer background rejection at a 30% signal-efficiency working point.

AI/ML usestrong

Pretraining on boosted boson decays for polarization tagging of hadronic W bosons

Saptaparna Bhattacharya

Method and result. A strong example of physics-first ML evaluation: it identifies the information ceiling, tests pretraining against simple baselines, and reports the negative result that specialized pretraining is not uniquely advantageous.

HEP/NP use case. The workflow transfers well to HEP and nuclear analyses with scarce labels: establish sufficient observables and acceptance limits before escalating model complexity, then compress the final model.

Transfer assessment. Convincing and unusually honest. The main limitation is domain scope: several event-level conclusions are at matrix-element level and still need detector/systematic validation in a realistic measurement chain.

Abstract

The study asks where boosted-W polarization information resides and derives an acceptance-imposed upper bound from the decay angle. A point-edge transformer pretrained on 10^7 unpolarized jets improves few-label learning, but generic pretraining and a boosted decision tree do similarly; compact distilled students match the teacher, while event-level production kinematics add more information than jet substructure alone.

AI/ML usemixed

Discovering Hidden Predictive States in Kinetic Transport

Pengfei Zhu

Method and result. A thoughtful framework for diagnosing failed hydrodynamic or transport closure and identifying which unresolved kinetic combinations must survive coarse-graining.

HEP/NP use case. The transfer path to heavy-ion theory is credible: it could test whether reduced pre-equilibrium, kinetic, or hydrodynamic state descriptions omit variables needed for future observables.

Transfer assessment. Promising but not demonstrated on relativistic QCD transport. The strongest tests are controlled synthetic systems, an observability baseline also performs strongly, and the inferred dimension depends on preparations, observables, stencil, and accuracy rather than representing a unique physical state count.

Abstract

The paper casts kinetic state reduction as predictive sufficiency and learns additional latent variables when identical conventional moments lead to different future evolution. Exact and synthetic Boltzmann systems show that learned subspaces can expose predictive directions missed by low-order moments, with deletion and response tests used as controls.
