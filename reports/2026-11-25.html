Softer charged-hadron spectra in Ne+Ne than in O+O collisions at the same multiplicity at √sNN = 5.36 TeV

Experiment · Kishora Nayak · arXiv:2611.32516

An independent reanalysis of published ATLAS data finds softer Ne+Ne spectra than O+O at matched multiplicity; the mean-pT ratio is 0.9822 for the 3–4% Ne+Ne class. This is a useful system-size comparison, not a new ATLAS measurement.

Assessment: Mixed but worthwhile: closure, interpolation and fit-range checks are useful. The spectral-stretch significance changes from 9.5σ to 3.3σ with the correlation assumption, which the published tables cannot test; the geometrical explanation is interpretive, not established.

Abstract summary

Published ATLAS O+O and Ne+Ne spectra are compared at matched multiplicity. A transverse-momentum rescaling describes the spectra in the stated soft range, and the comparison is checked across centrality, pseudorapidity and Xe+Xe/Pb+Pb.

The impact of phase transitions on dilepton production

Phenomenology · Renan Góes-Hirayama, Hannah Elfner · arXiv:2611.32712

SMASH+vHLLE calculations compare first-order and crossover equations of state at energies relevant to CBM and the STAR beam-energy scan. The authors report about 7% more intermediate-mass dielectrons in their more realistic transition scenario, a concrete scale for assessing experimental sensitivity.

Assessment: Mixed: a valuable controlled phenomenological estimate, but a conference proceeding and model-dependent prediction, not evidence for a transition. The small difference requires background, acceptance, rate and systematic-uncertainty studies before experimental discriminating power is established.

Abstract summary

A hybrid transport and hydrodynamic calculation estimates intermediate-mass dielectron yields for a toy equation of state and an equation of state incorporating an Ising-based critical endpoint. A first-order transition increases the yield by approximately 7% in the more realistic scenario.

OmniX: A first jet and event model

AI/ML use · Anna Hallin, Gregor Kasieczka, Henning Rose · arXiv:2611.31403

One model jointly trained on jet-level and event-level samples performs generation and classification; the authors report performance matching or exceeding comparable single-domain models on JetClass, top tagging and Collide-2V. Shared representations could support jet tagging and event simulation in HEP, with heavy-ion transfer an untested possibility.

Assessment: Promising methodological study with multiple datasets and matched model comparisons, not proof of a general-purpose foundation model. Heavy-ion occupancy, underlying event, detector shifts and computational scalability require dedicated evaluation. Submitted 23 November 19:02 UTC, approximately 47 hours before this run, but newly listed on 25 November.

Abstract summary

A joint jet and full-event architecture is evaluated for generation and classification on JetClass, Top-tagging and Collide-2V, with comparisons to equivalent jet-only and event-only training. Code is available.
