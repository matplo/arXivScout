Executive takeaways

  • Leading result: ALICE's identified-hadron v2 in OO shows mass ordering and a 10-sigma baryon-meson split; the phi following K0S rather than the mass-similar Lambda makes a materially stronger case for partonic collectivity than inclusive flow alone.
  • Clean hadronization/nucleosynthesis discriminator: ALICE's first antideuteron-net-Lambda correlation agrees with statistical hadronization while the earlier antideuteron-antiproton anomaly does not, isolating the latter as a composite-formation effect.
  • Promising extraction method, limited evidence: the speed-of-sound framework corrects quantum fluctuation bias in hydrodynamic simulations, but the current proceedings paper does not yet establish robustness to non-Gaussian fluctuations or model mismatch.
  • Small-system flow analyses need explicit stress tests: the proposed v1-based nonflow scaling passes toy and PYTHIA-Angantyr closure tests and is useful for OO, but AMPT predictions are not experimental validation.
  • Best AI/ML contributions: uncertainty-aware generative-model validation and NTK analysis of PDF fits address statistical failure modes that directly matter in HEP; the Lund-plane paper shows why AUC alone can conceal rare-background deficiencies.

Extracting the QCD speed of sound in the presence of quantum fluctuations

Yu-Shan Mu, Li Yan, Xu-Guang Huang

Summary. The authors propose a statistical correction to the mean-pT versus charged-multiplicity correlation used to infer the QCD speed of sound, exploiting an assumed Gaussian structure of quantum-initiated event fluctuations. Event-by-event hydrodynamic tests recover lattice-QCD expectations across large and small systems and suggest an auxiliary thermalization diagnostic.

Why it matters. Bias control is central if this correlation is to become a quantitative equation-of-state observable rather than a qualitative trend.

Critical assessment. Conceptually valuable and directly relevant, but this is a short conference proceeding. The abstract-level evidence is simulation closure; sensitivity to non-Gaussian initial fluctuations, centrality definition, detector response, and hydrodynamic model mismatch remains unclear.

Abstract

A Gaussianity-based correction is proposed for quantum-fluctuation bias in speed-of-sound extraction and validated in event-by-event hydrodynamic simulations.

Heavy-ion - Experimentstrong

Evidence of partonic collectivity in OO collisions through the measurement of phi, K0S, and Lambda elliptic flow

ALICE Collaboration (large collaboration author list)

Summary. ALICE reports the first phi-meson v2 measurement in a small ion system at sqrt(sNN)=5.36 TeV. Low-pT mass ordering is followed above 3 GeV/c by a 10-sigma baryon-meson split: Lambda exceeds the mesons, while the mass-similar phi tracks K0S.

Why it matters. The phi/Lambda comparison weakens a purely hydrodynamic mass explanation and supports quark recombination from a collectively flowing partonic stage in a system much smaller than Pb-Pb.

Critical assessment. The day's strongest result: a targeted species comparison and high-significance pattern provide more discriminating evidence than generic positive v2. The interpretation is still model-dependent; hadronic rescattering and nonflow/systematic treatment in the full paper determine how literally “partonic” should be read.

Abstract

Identified-hadron v2 in OO exhibits mass ordering at low pT and baryon-meson splitting at intermediate pT, with phi behavior favoring recombination over a mass-only account.

Heavy-ion - Experimentstrong

Measurement of antideuteron and net-Lambda number fluctuations at the Large Hadron Collider

ALICE Collaboration (large collaboration author list)

Summary. The first antideuteron-net-Lambda number correlation in Pb-Pb, together with the net-Lambda second cumulant, agrees with Thermal-FIST statistical hadronization. Because Lambda is not an antideuteron constituent, this contrasts cleanly with the previously anomalous antideuteron-antiproton correlation.

Why it matters. The observable separates correlations generated at hadronization from those introduced by late composite-state formation, sharpening the statistical-hadronization versus nucleosynthesis question.

Critical assessment. Strong experimental logic: the nonconstituent control channel is unusually well chosen. The conclusion depends on common acceptance/efficiency corrections and the adequacy of the specific Thermal-FIST parameterization, which warrant full-paper inspection.

Abstract

Antideuteron-net-Lambda correlations agree with statistical hadronization, supporting nucleosynthesis as the source of the antideuteron-antiproton discrepancy.

Heavy-ion - Phenomenologymixed

Improved nonflow control for multiparticle correlators and system-size-dependent evolution from OO to Pb-Pb collisions at the LHC

Neelkamal Mallick, Suraj Prasad

Summary. A v1-estimator-based scaling prescription for nonflow subtraction is tested against a controlled toy model and PYTHIA 8 Angantyr, where collective flow is absent. It outperforms multiplicity reweighting for selected symmetry-plane correlators and symmetric cumulants, then supplies AMPT predictions for OO-to-Pb-Pb comparisons.

Why it matters. Small-system multiparticle observables can be dominated by nonflow precisely where geometry-driven collectivity is most interesting.

Critical assessment. The closure-test design is sound and practically useful. Confidence should remain moderate until the prescription is stress-tested against generators with different jet/resonance structure and, crucially, embedded flow plus nonflow; the AMPT application is a prediction, not validation.

Abstract

A v1-based nonflow scaling method is validated in no-flow simulations and applied to OO multiparticle-correlation predictions.

Heavy-ion - Phenomenologystrong

Higher-order strongly intensive observables for forward-backward multiplicity correlations

Evgeny Andronov

Summary. The second-order strongly intensive construction is generalized to all cumulant orders for independent identical sources, with explicit fourth-order expressions and an all-order recursion. Analytical models and PYTHIA at 0.9 and 13 TeV expose sensitivity to acceptance geometry, selection, and connected correlations.

Why it matters. Higher-order longitudinal correlations could retain dynamical information while suppressing the nuisance contribution from fluctuations in the number of particle-emitting sources.

Critical assessment. Mathematically substantial, with clear normalization limits and nontrivial benchmarks. Its protection is conditional on identical, source-count-independent emission; real nuclear geometry, conservation laws, and centrality selection can violate that premise, so experimental deployment needs dedicated closure studies.

Abstract

An all-order hierarchy of strongly intensive forward-backward observables is derived and benchmarked with analytical models and PYTHIA.

Physics AI/MLstrong

An end-to-end framework for uncertainty-aware validation of generative models

Giada Badaracco, Sean Benevedes, Christina Reissel, Philip Harris, Gaia Grosso

Method and result. SIGMA combines tractable density estimation with uncertainty propagation into a composite-null goodness-of-fit test. On a toy problem and QCD-background benchmark, profiling fit uncertainty extends the usable test-set size by up to an order of magnitude relative to treating the learned model as exact.

Likely HEP/heavy-ion use. This targets a central obstacle for learned background models and fast simulators: distinguishing true mismodeling from finite-training-sample estimation error in downstream statistical tests.

Critical assessment. Statistically principled and unusually aligned with inference requirements. Transfer beyond Gaussian mixtures is the decisive next step; flexible high-dimensional generators may not admit calibrated, computationally manageable parameter uncertainty.

Abstract

Generative-model fit uncertainty is profiled as nuisance parameters in a goodness-of-fit test, avoiding automatic rejection of finite-sample estimators on large test sets.

Physics AI/MLstrong

The proton structure one mode at a time: a Neural Tangent Kernel analysis of a DIS fit

Amedeo Chiefa, Luigi Del Debbio, Richard Kenway

Method and result. Exact gradient-flow analysis is extended to multiflavor neural PDF fits. Training behaves as incremental inclusion of NTK eigenmodes: leading modes reconstruct the target, while late subleading modes preferentially absorb noise; the paper also derives Shapley values for linear PDF observables in the same framework.

Likely HEP/heavy-ion use. The mode picture provides an interpretable route to early stopping, inconsistent-data stress tests, and regularization diagnostics in PDF/nPDF extraction and other ill-posed inverse problems.

Critical assessment. Deep and physics-specific rather than decorative ML, with 70 pages and extensive diagnostics. NTK linearization is most faithful in wide-network, near-initialization regimes, so usefulness for realistic finite-network training must be judged from the detailed experiments.

Abstract

NTK eigenmodes explain regularization and noise learning in multiflavor neural-network fits to deep-inelastic-scattering data.

neural tangent kernelPDF fitsinverse problemsregularization
Physics AI/MLstrong

What the Primary Lund Plane Omits: A Model-Stitching Diagnosis of a Jet-Tagger Representation Gap

Saurabh Rai, Sanmay Ganguly

Method and result. Linear model stitching separates representation loss from downstream-reader limitations in top tagging. AUC misses a rare-background failure, whereas low-efficiency rejection exposes it; rebuilding on the full Cambridge/Aachen tree closes 72% of the ParT gap even though inference remains dominated by the primary branch.

Likely HEP/heavy-ion use. The diagnostic is directly useful for jet taggers, including quenched-jet studies, where rare backgrounds and representation-induced blind spots matter more than aggregate AUC.

Critical assessment. Strong experimental design: a known-incomplete positive control, alternative reader, localization of the failure population, and predicted repair all support the causal claim. Transfer to detector-level or heavy-ion backgrounds is plausible but not established.

Abstract

Model stitching localizes a primary-Lund representation deficit that aggregate AUC conceals; full-tree construction substantially repairs top-tagging rejection.

Energy density in color string clusters and its implications for heavy-quark energy loss

Daria Prokhorova, Shuzhe Shi, Evgeny Andronov

Why caution is warranted. The paper contrasts square-root versus linear source-number scaling when converting overlapping color strings into an effective medium temperature, a choice that can feed heavy-quark loss calculations. But the abstract reports no comparison with heavy-flavor observables, no uncertainty analysis, and no demonstration that either prescription is selected by data. At eight pages and framed as a model-choice discussion, it is useful as a sensitivity warning, not yet a quantitative energy-loss result.

Abstract

Two definitions of energy density in overlapping color-string clusters imply different effective temperatures for subsequent heavy-quark energy-loss calculations.
