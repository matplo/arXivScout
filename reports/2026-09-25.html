Executive takeaways

  • Lead paper: 2609.29886 builds an initial-state-only, multi-system baseline for centrality-dependent nuclear modification and leaves a clear suppression deficit in O+O and Pb+Pb for final-state energy loss to explain.
  • 2609.28394 makes a persuasive case that a single Gaussian is inadequate for peripheral Pb-Pb femtoscopic sources and identifies the physical origin of the long tail.
  • The strongest AI/ML result is experimental: 2609.28604 validates self-supervised detector calibration with independent reference wires and improves inferred position resolution by 33–44%.
  • 2609.29969 is a useful example of honest UQ: strong in-distribution calibration, visible degradation out of distribution, and no claim that uncertainty propagation cures model misspecification.
  • Two HEP ML papers are promising but bounded: hybrid neural density estimation is tested only on an analytic toy, while calorimeter transfer covers one source-target geometry pair.

Measuring short-range correlations using relativistic heavy-ion collisions

Lu-Meng Liu, Jun Xu, Xu-Guang Huang

Summary. The authors propose using the ratio of free spectator-neutron yields at large and small transverse distance as a proxy for the SRC-induced high-momentum-tail fraction; a Zr-96/Ru-96 ratio is intended to probe its isospin dependence.

Why it matters. If experimentally robust, this would provide a collider-based handle on SRCs with different final-state systematics from fixed-target scattering.

Critical assessment. Promising but mixed. The observable is clever and concrete, yet the seven-page study does not establish a detector-level uncertainty budget or robustness against spectator fragmentation, geometry, and transport-model variations.

Abstract

The work proposes measuring nuclear short-range correlations through spectator-neutron yields at large versus small transverse distance in relativistic heavy-ion collisions. The high transverse velocities of nucleons in the SRC-induced high-momentum tail motivate the observable, while a Zr-96/Ru-96 large-radius neutron ratio is proposed to probe isospin dependence.

Centrality-dependent nuclear modification from hard-soft correlations in the glasma

Coleridge Faraday, W. A. Horowitz, Björn Schenke

Summary. IP-Glasma calculates centrality-dependent R_AB from initial-state physics across p+O, p+Pb, O+O, and Pb+Pb. Reproducing centrality biases while underpredicting observed O+O and Pb+Pb suppression creates a useful baseline for isolating final-state energy loss.

Why it matters. It directly addresses the small-system energy-loss problem and the entanglement of hard probes with multiplicity selection.

Critical assessment. Strong. Multi-system validation, HERA-only calibration, Bayesian parameter propagation, and explicit numerical checks are unusually substantial. Interpretation still inherits IP-Glasma’s limited quark treatment and its small-x, intermediate-pT domain.

Abstract

The paper presents IP-Glasma predictions for centrality-dependent nuclear modification in p+O, p+Pb, O+O, and Pb+Pb from initial-state effects. A common color-field framework produces soft and semi-hard particles, describes multiplicity distributions and several R_AB measurements, predicts less suppression than measured in O+O and Pb+Pb, and attributes hard-soft correlations to event-by-event color-field fluctuations.

Phenomenologystrong

Simulating the emission source function in very peripheral relativistic heavy-ion collisions

Angel Reina Ramírez, Volodymyr Magas, Juan M. Torres-Rincon

Summary. A SMASH+vHLLE+SMASH evolution reconstructs emission sources for several pair species in 80–90% Pb-Pb and separates primordial, rescattering, and resonance contributions. A Gaussian-plus-exponential form captures the compact core and long tail better than a single Gaussian.

Why it matters. Source-shape systematics feed directly into femtoscopic interaction and radius extractions, especially in peripheral or small systems.

Critical assessment. Strong and careful model phenomenology. The physical decomposition is valuable, but conclusions remain generator dependent until compared with measured correlations and alternative initial-state or afterburner choices.

Abstract

The study reconstructs space-time emission sources in very peripheral Pb-Pb collisions at 5.02 TeV using a hybrid transport framework. It analyzes meson, baryon, and cross-species pairs, finds a Gaussian-plus-exponential source superior to a Gaussian, and observes approximate mT scaling of core radii.

AI/ML usestrong

Physics-Informed Self-Supervised Learning for Joint Wire Calibration and Interaction Position Reconstruction in Multi-Wire Parallel Plate Avalanche Counters

Antoine Lemasson, Maurycy Rejmund

Summary. Global wire gains and event-level positions are inferred jointly from detector geometry and charge-energy consistency, without labelled positions or dedicated calibration runs, on real VAMOS++ data.

Why it matters. Continuous label-free calibration is directly relevant to long-running HEP and nuclear detectors subject to drift and aging.

Critical assessment. Strong. Independent reference-wire checks show 33% and 44% resolution improvements over the conventional method. Transfer beyond linear gains and detectors with adequate physical redundancy remains unproven.

Abstract

A physics-informed self-supervised framework jointly estimates wire gains and hit positions from detector constraints. Experimental VAMOS++ evaluation shows stable convergence, better spatial homogeneity, and improved position resolution while learning the induction response from data.

Rapid Uncertainty Quantification on a Latent Field using Fisher Information

Karl Daningburg, A. E. Lovell, Arvind T. Mohan, R. O'Shaughnessy

Method and result. Fisher information and a differentiable scattering solver propagate uncertainty from an inferred optical-potential field to observables without full posterior sampling.

Likely use case. Calibrated uncertainties for expensive differentiable inverse models, including surrogate-assisted transport or detector-response inference.

Critical assessment. Strong within domain. Held-out in-distribution cross sections reach 96.0% coverage for nominal 95% intervals with intervals 30% narrower than KDUQ. On a harder out-of-distribution corpus coverage drops to 89.3%, exposing the local-Gaussian/model limit.

Abstract

The method combines Fisher information with a differentiable solver for rapid field and predictive uncertainty. In nuclear optical potentials it distinguishes data-constrained structure from prior-controlled structure and improves held-out predictive calibration relative to an established Bayesian model.

AI/ML usemixed

Simulation-Based Inference and Unbinned Asimov Construction with Hybrid Neural Density Estimation

Rafael Coelho Lopes de Sa, Jay Sandesara

Method and result. A normalizing-flow reference plus classifier density ratios yields an evaluable density, renewable pseudo-experiments, importance sampling, and weighted unbinned Asimov data with finite-sample closure for the learned likelihood.

Likely use case. Unbinned HEP fits, expected-sensitivity scans, and pseudo-experiment generation from implicit simulators.

Critical assessment. Serious but not deployment-grade. It includes analytic truth, likelihood comparisons, 100,000 pseudo-experiments, shape-systematic profiling, and open code; however, exact closure can hold for inaccurate classifiers, and validation is confined to a controlled five-dimensional toy.

Abstract

Hybrid neural density estimation multiplies a tractable parameter-independent flow density by learned target-to-reference ratios. The resulting representation supports normalized density evaluation, sampling, Asimov construction, and lower-variance expected-test-statistic calculations in a HEP-motivated analytic toy.

AI/ML usemixed

Cross-geometry transfer and model collapse in point cloud calorimeter shower generation

Thorsten Buss, Frank Gaede, Gregor Kasieczka, Lorenzo Valente, Duncan Weber

Method and result. CaloClouds II transfers from ILD photons to a different cylindrical electron calorimeter; with 100 target showers it improves geometric-mean Wasserstein distance by about 51% over scratch training, while BitFit stays within 5% of full fine-tuning using 17% of diffusion parameters.

Likely use case. Data-efficient geometry adaptation during detector design and fast-simulation development.

Critical assessment. Useful but compact proceedings evidence. Transfer spans one source-target pair with no direct multi-detector-pretraining baseline; recursive synthetic retraining shows genuine model collapse, and hadronic/mixed-particle tests remain open.

Abstract

The study tests cross-geometry transfer in a point-cloud calorimeter generator and recursive retraining on generated showers. It finds substantial low-data transfer gains, parameter-efficient fine-tuning close to full tuning, and progressive distribution degradation across synthetic generations.
