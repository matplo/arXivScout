Heavy-Ion Collision Papers

Theory

Event-by-event analysis of chiral charge separation in p^up+Au collisions within an improved AMPT model

caution

Chen Gao, Gui-Zhen Wu, Yi Xu, Wei-Tian Deng | arXiv:2608.10496

The paper introduces an improved string-melting AMPT setup for p^up+Au at 200 GeV, adding a formation-time delay and a model CME source scaled with the event magnetic field. It reports that partonic evolution preserves much of the injected signal, while coalescence and hadronic rescattering strongly dilute it.

Why it matters: Small-system CME proposals are experimentally enticing because geometry handles could help separate signal from background.

Critical assessment: Worth reading, but the key conclusion that an inter-scheme difference is essentially background-free is too strong from an injected-source transport study. Background realism and data-facing closure tests are the limiting issues.

Experiment

Anisotropic Flow in Ultra-Central Pb-Pb Collisions at sqrt(sNN)=5.36 TeV with ALICE

strong

Iris Likmeta | arXiv:2608.11199

This contribution reports ALICE Run 3 measurements of v3{2}/v2{2} in ultra-central Pb-Pb collisions, where initial geometric anisotropy is small and fluctuations dominate. The work directly targets the ultra-central-collision puzzle and comparisons to hydrodynamic calculations with possible Pb-208 octupole deformation effects.

Why it matters: Ultra-central flow ratios are unusually sensitive to initial-state fluctuations and nuclear-structure inputs, so even a short proceedings-style result can constrain hydrodynamic interpretations.

Critical assessment: Strong relevance, but likely limited depth as a conference contribution. The value will depend on the quantitative model comparisons and eventual full ALICE publication.

Phenomenology

Extraction of baryon number susceptibilities at finite density from heavy-ion collisions

strong

Gregoire Pihan, Roman Poberezhniuk, Volodymyr A. Kuznietsov, Volodymyr Vovchenko | arXiv:2608.10238

The paper presents a Bayesian extraction of QCD baryon-number susceptibilities from RHIC Beam Energy Scan proton cumulants. It embeds equation-of-state susceptibilities into realistic hydrodynamic particlization hypersurfaces via maximum-entropy freeze-out, maps baryons to protons, applies acceptance, and includes exact baryon-number conservation.

Why it matters: This is the kind of statistically disciplined bridge needed between fluctuation measurements and finite-density QCD thermodynamics.

Critical assessment: Strong and highly relevant. The tight chi2B constraints and the statement that nonmonotonic C3/C1 can arise without irreducible higher-order correlations are important, while the weak constraints on third- and fourth-order ratios are appropriately conservative.

Scaling of soft QGP signatures in relativistic lead, xenon and oxygen collisions in EPOS4

mixed

Salman Khurshid Malik, Fakhar Ul Haider, Pratibha Bhagat, Anju Bhasin, Ramni Gupta | arXiv:2608.10578

This EPOS4 study compares Pb-Pb and Xe-Xe soft observables to ALICE data and projects O-O results, covering multiplicity, identified spectra, pT fluctuations, and v2/v3. It finds UrQMD important for baryon yields and treats O-O as a small-system QGP diagnostic.

Why it matters: O-O baselines will matter for upcoming small-system collectivity discussions, and a single-generator survey can be a useful reference point.

Critical assessment: Useful but not decisive. The paper is strongest as a model baseline; claims about QGP droplets in small systems should be viewed as generator-dependent unless supported by broader model comparison and uncertainty treatment.

Probing vorticity and fluctuations in a rotating hadron resonance gas at LHC energy

caution

S. Ipsita Sahoo, Sanjeebani Biswal, D. Dutta, D. K. Mishra | arXiv:2608.10561

The authors model rotation in an HRG through an effective chemical potential and compare p/pi trends to ALICE Pb-Pb centrality dependence, extracting freeze-out vorticity estimates. They also discuss effects on conserved-charge fluctuations.

Why it matters: Vorticity and polarization remain central heavy-ion topics, and yield-ratio proxies are tempting because they are experimentally accessible.

Critical assessment: Caution. Inferring vorticity from integrated yield ratios risks strong degeneracy with chemical freeze-out, hadronic feed-down, and centrality systematics; it needs more discriminating observables before being treated as a robust extraction.

AI/ML Use

The near-threshold cross section of e+e- to Omega- anti-Omega+: Heavy-flavor rescattering and physics-informed deep learning

caution

Sara Rahmani | arXiv:2608.10301

The paper combines a coupled-channel hadronic model with simulation-based inference via a mixture-density network and a Cauchy-Riemann PINN for analytic continuation into the complex plane. It reports a bound-state pole near the Ds anti-Ds threshold.

Why it matters: SBI and differentiable analytic-continuation tools could become useful in hadron spectroscopy and amplitude analyses.

Critical assessment: Interesting but caution-worthy. A zero-width pole claim from a one-author ML-assisted analysis needs robust conventional cross-checks, posterior predictive validation, and stress tests against model assumptions before it should influence spectroscopy conclusions.

AI/ML Use Cases in Physics

Machine learning methods for spectroscopic information recovery under ultrafast photon pileup

strong

Jayson R. Vavrek, Thomas D. MacDonald, Yue Shi Lai | arXiv:2608.10143

The authors use 3D CNNs, FCNNs, and PointNet++-style models to recover signal fractions and photon multiplicities from spatial energy-deposition patterns under severe pileup. They compare to non-ML baselines and include synthetic pileup built from real Cs-137 data for multiplicity recovery.

Likely transfer: Strong transfer to high-rate calorimetry, active detector readout, and heavy-ion environments where overlapping energy deposits degrade conventional reconstruction.

Critical assessment: Strong for transfer because the task is detector-realistic and baseline-aware. The main caveat is domain shift from representative photon-interaction examples to full experimental detector conditions.

Particle tracking at high luminosities using a novel reconstruction approach

mixed

B. K. Sirasva, S. Dugad, Y. Kumar, P. Suryadevra, M. Yadav | arXiv:2608.10900

This paper proposes an ML-based track-reconstruction algorithm for a Phase-2 CMS-like outer tracker, evaluated on Pythia8 plus Geant4 high-luminosity samples. It reports strong efficiency, fake-rate, and resolution performance versus traditional methods.

Likely transfer: Directly relevant to HEP tracking and potentially to heavy-ion high-occupancy reconstruction if validated under realistic pileup and detector effects.

Critical assessment: Worth tracking, but abstract-level evidence is not enough to call it strong. The key tests are benchmark comparability, occupancy scaling, latency, robustness to misalignment/noise, and reproducibility.

Derivative Computation in PINNs: Automatic Differentiation, Finite Differences and Beyond

strong

Maciej J. Mikulski, Tadeusz Uhl | arXiv:2608.11020

The paper systematically compares finite-difference derivatives with automatic differentiation in PINNs across PDE benchmarks. It argues calibrated finite differences can match AD accuracy, reduce memory, improve runtime, and avoid silently incorrect per-sample derivatives in architectures with inter-sample dependencies such as BatchNorm or attention.

Likely transfer: High value for physics surrogate modeling, hydrodynamic PDE learning, lattice/transport inverse problems, and any PINN workflow operating at large batch sizes.

Critical assessment: Strong transfer value because it addresses a concrete numerical failure mode rather than selling a new architecture. Needs independent replication across stiff and conservation-law-heavy physics PDEs.

The Kuramoto Neural Operator: Learning to Solve PDEs via Coupled Oscillator Dynamics

mixed

Petr Badolia, Leonid Obukhov, Dmitry Bylinkin, Aleksandr Beznosikov | arXiv:2608.10234

KNO represents PDE solution operators through a latent field of coupled spherical oscillators and reports strong benchmark performance with ablations and robustness/resolution-transfer experiments. The synchronization dynamics are used as a partial interpretability handle.

Likely transfer: Possible surrogate-modeling use in repeated hydro, transport, or detector-field solves where local interactions matter.

Critical assessment: Promising but indirect for HEP/NP. It earns inclusion because the PDE/operator-learning connection is concrete, but transfer claims should wait for tests on conservation laws, discontinuities, and uncertainty propagation.

Weak or Overclaimed Papers Worth Caution

arXiv:2608.10301 - ML-assisted pole extraction in Omega-pair production

caution

The method mix is interesting, but the pole claim is much stronger than the abstract-level validation supports. Treat as a methods experiment until conventional analytic continuation and model-systematic tests are shown clearly.

arXiv:2608.10496 - CME signal in p^up+Au from improved AMPT

caution

The proposed observable may be useful, but calling it essentially background-free is premature. A transport model with an injected CME source is not a substitute for data-driven background closure.

arXiv:2608.10561 - vorticity extraction from rotating HRG yield ratios

caution

The topic is relevant, but the inference route appears underconstrained. Yield ratios alone are too degenerate with ordinary freeze-out and hadronic chemistry effects to carry a precise vorticity interpretation.
