Heavy-ion collision papers

Theory

Collective Organization of String Fragmentationstrong, provisional transfer

Romuald A. Janik, Maciej A. Nowak, Marek M. Rams, Ismail Zahed (4 authors) · arXiv:2610.20793

Summary. The authors use real-time tensor-network simulations of the massive Schwinger model to study coherent string breaking. They report that, for intermediate masses, one collective mode captures more than 99% of the time evolution of the electric-field probability distribution, with a survival law matching the Gaussian dependence associated with the Lund world-sheet area law. The same mode is reported to control meson production, an effective equation of state, and entanglement growth.

Why it matters. Hadronization is a major modeling uncertainty in event generators and in the interpretation of soft observables. A microscopic mechanism behind an area-law-like survival pattern could motivate better-controlled tests of string-fragmentation assumptions.

Critical assessment. Strong as a controlled field-theory computation and conceptually interesting. The result is not yet a heavy-ion result: it uses a 1+1-dimensional Abelian model, so universality across QCD color, transverse structure, and realistic multiparticle fragmentation remains untested.

Nuclear PDFsmixed: useful summary, limited novelty

K. Greve, T. Ježo, M. Klasen, A. Kusina, F. I. Olness, J. Wissmann (6 authors) · arXiv:2610.21697 · LHCP2026 contribution

Summary. This contribution reviews model-independent global analyses of nuclear parton distribution functions. It stresses broad data coverage, modern theory input, and uncertainty estimates that do not artificially shrink from parametrization bias, and calls for more serious treatment of the A-dependence as LHC nuclei-collision data accumulate.

Why it matters. nPDF uncertainties remain a limiting theory input for hard-probe baselines and many nuclear-modification observables. The emphasis on A-dependence and fit diagnostics is directly relevant to heavy-ion analyses.

Critical assessment. Scientifically relevant and methodologically sound in emphasis, but this is a short conference proceeding and does not itself present a new fit, quantitative improvement, or full validation suite. Treat it as a useful status signal, not as a standalone result.

AI/ML use cases in physics

Machine-learning-based neutrino-event generation constrained by experimental measurementsstrong

Jose Luis Bonilla, Krzysztof M. Graczyk, Rwik Dharmapal Banerjee, Beata E. Kowal, Hemant Prasad, Jan T. Sobczyk (6 authors) · arXiv:2610.20870

Summary. The paper builds a generative surrogate for charged-current muon-neutrino scattering on nuclei. It starts from a theoretical interaction model, calibrates to MicroBooNE differential cross-section data without requiring event-level experimental samples, and reports improved agreement for observables not used in the fit. The calibrated surrogate is also tested under different neutrino fluxes without retuning while retaining event-level outgoing-muon kinematics.

HEP/NP use case. This is a concrete pattern for replacing expensive or imperfect nuclear event models while keeping detector-facing event generation and data-driven calibration. The same workflow could be relevant to heavy-ion response surrogates, unfolding, or Bayesian extraction, provided correlations, detector effects, and uncertainty propagation are retained.

Critical assessment. This is stronger than a generic ML application because the model is constrained by measured cross sections and tested out of fit. The main unresolved risks are model misspecification, extrapolation beyond MicroBooNE kinematics, and whether calibration uncertainty is propagated into downstream physics inference.

Ergodic topology sampling toward the continuum with Stochastic Normalizing Flowsstrong, early stage

Claudio Bonanno, Andrea Bulgarelli, Elia Cellini, Alessandro Nada, Dario Panfalone, Davide Vadacchino, Lorenzo Verzichelli (7 authors) · arXiv:2610.20747

Summary. The authors combine open boundary conditions with stochastic normalizing flows to mitigate topological freezing in SU(3) Yang-Mills theory. They test lattice spacings as fine as 0.03 fm and report controlled integrated autocorrelation times for the squared topological charge and controlled uncertainty in the topological susceptibility using currently available resources.

HEP/NP use case. Better topology sampling can make continuum-limit lattice calculations more practical and can transfer to finite-temperature QCD observables that inform heavy-ion equation-of-state and topological-physics studies.

Critical assessment. The target is an important, concrete computational pathology and the evaluation uses the right observables rather than only likelihood or training metrics. Evidence is still limited to pure-gauge tests and the explored resource range; claims of broad lattice-QCD impact should wait for larger volumes, dynamical fermions, and independent cost comparisons.
