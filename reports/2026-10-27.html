Executive takeaways

  • Best new measurement: STAR's forward pseudorapidity distributions across eight Au+Au energies provide a precision longitudinal-production baseline for the full RHIC Beam Energy Scan.
  • Most provocative experimental hint: ALICE sees away-side dihadron suppression in OO, but event-selection and cold-nuclear-matter alternatives are not yet controlled well enough to call this unambiguous light-ion jet quenching.
  • Geometry and spin become jointly testable: a UrQMD study predicts near-linear scaling between global Lambda polarization and femtoscopic source tilt, although current experimental points are not fully matched in energy and centrality.
  • AI/ML lesson for nuclear inference: the uranium-radius network is informative at generator level but is not yet connected to observable final states; by contrast, the simulation-based-inference paper supplies a rigorous way to compare lossy event summaries at matched information rate.
  • Benchmark caution: modest tuning overturns neural-operator baselines on 15 of 17 tasks, and the QFT-LLM paper's greater-than-95% accuracy is on 75 closed-form problems, not open-ended research.

A Barnett-Like Effect in Relativistic Heavy-Ion Collisions

Dai-Neng Liu, Kai-Jia Sun, Jan Steinheimer, Jin-Hui Chen, Yu-Gang Ma, Marcus Bleicher

Summary. UrQMD calculations over sqrt(sNN)=2.24-7.7 GeV link the beam-energy evolution of global Lambda polarization to the tilt of the emitting source. A quadrupolar vorticity pattern largely cancels unless the source is tilted, yielding a near-linear polarization-tilt relation that is broadly consistent with existing HADES, E895, and STAR measurements.

Why it matters. It proposes a joint, experimentally accessible geometry-spin test rather than treating polarization as an isolated observable.

Critical assessment. Strong and physically interpretable, with transport and data comparisons. The experimental correlation is not yet a uniform same-energy, same-centrality measurement: one tilt point is interpolated, two high-energy polarization points lie outside the simulated range, and the linear fit neglects tilt uncertainties.

Abstract

Transport calculations connect global Lambda polarization to the source tilt angle extracted from two-pion femtoscopy, finding an approximately linear relation across beam energy.

Measurement of pseudorapidity distributions from Au+Au collisions with the STAR Event Plane Detector

STAR Collaboration

Summary. The EPD measurement spans eight RHIC energies and multiple centralities. Landau-fit hit yields are unfolded with UrQMD+GEANT3 response matrices; agreement with PHOBOS is recovered where overlap exists, with improved precision and much broader energy coverage.

Why it matters. This is a durable reference dataset for longitudinal particle production, energy deposition, and limiting-fragmentation tests across the Beam Energy Scan.

Critical assessment. The day's strongest heavy-ion measurement. The broad energy lever arm and cross-check against PHOBOS are major assets; residual model dependence from response-matrix unfolding and covariance treatment will matter for precision phenomenology.

Abstract

STAR measures charged-particle pseudorapidity densities over 2.14<|eta|<5.09 in Au+Au collisions from 7.7 to 200 GeV using detector-response unfolding.

Jet-like dihadron correlations in OO and Ne-Ne collisions at sqrt(sNN)=5.36 TeV with ALICE

Neelkamal Mallick, Joonsuk Bae (on behalf of the ALICE Collaboration)

Summary. High-pT dihadron correlations in the 2025 light-ion data show a multiplicity-dependent near-side modification and an away-side associated-yield suppression in OO relative to pp. The latter is qualitatively consistent with weaker jet-medium interactions than in central Pb-Pb.

Why it matters. Light ions occupy the crucial gap where collective behavior is established but unambiguous jet quenching remains difficult.

Critical assessment. Scientifically important but not yet decisive evidence for parton energy loss. The paper is a seven-page proceedings contribution, and ALICE explicitly leaves event-activity bias and cold-nuclear-matter effects unresolved.

Abstract

ALICE reports multiplicity-dependent near-side peak widths and the first light-ion per-trigger associated-yield ratios, including away-side IAA in OO collisions.

Levy-stable pion emission sources in Au+Au collisions at sqrt(sNN)=200 GeV from the vHLLE+SMASH hybrid model

Zeinab A. Abdelrazik, Mate Csanad

Summary. For central Au+Au at 200 GeV, a single Levy form describes simulated pair separations to about 100 fm, with alpha about 1.5-1.6 and slowly increasing with transverse mass. Deviations at larger separation are associated with long-lived resonance decays, while rescattering and short-lived resonances may contribute to the non-Gaussian core.

Why it matters. It helps translate measured Levy femtoscopy parameters into specific hadronic-stage mechanisms in a realistic hybrid calculation.

Critical assessment. Useful, focused phenomenology, but currently one system, energy, centrality, model setup, and fit range. The causal attribution among rescattering, resonance feed-down, critical dynamics, and fitting choices is not yet separated quantitatively.

Abstract

A vHLLE+SMASH calculation reconstructs three-dimensional pion pair-separation distributions and fits them with Levy-stable source functions.

Midrapidity dip and limiting fragmentation of charged hadrons from RHIC to LHC energies

Neeraj Chauhan, Md. Kaosar Ali Mondal, Amal Sarkar

Summary. Across central Au+Au and Pb+Pb data, the analysis finds that a midrapidity depression in dN/deta need not imply one in dN/dy. Generator closure exposes a possible false dip from forward-region assumptions, while current LHC coverage is insufficient to establish or exclude limiting fragmentation.

Why it matters. It directly challenges a common visual interpretation of longitudinal distributions using a data-driven rather than generator-imposed Jacobian.

Critical assessment. Methodologically careful and appropriately conservative. Its conclusions are limited by poorly constrained LHC fragmentation regions and assumed covariance/kernel families, but the null-test framing is a strength.

Abstract

Measured identified-hadron spectra are used to construct the y-to-eta transformation kernel and test whether the charged-hadron pseudorapidity dip requires a dip in rapidity density.

Inferring Nuclear Radial Parameters from Initial-State Information in Heavy-Ion Collisions Using Deep Neural Networks

Yang Liu, Yu Sha, Xiang Fan, Yan-Sheng Tu, Shi-Lei Tao, et al. (10 authors total)

Summary. On independent, denser parameter-grid tests, grouping 100 simulated events improves R0 regression to R2=0.84 overall, while diffuseness reaches R2=0.53; sensitivity varies strongly with centrality. The scan uses 8.82 million TRENTo events and a separate test grid.

Why it matters. It quantifies where nuclear radial information lives in the initial geometry and supplies a controlled baseline for later inverse modeling.

Critical assessment. A competent proof of concept, not an experimental inference. Inputs include unobservable initial entropy maps and eccentricities, with no transport, detector response, model discrepancy, nuisance-parameter variation, or comparison against simpler summary-statistic regressors.

Abstract

A multimodal network combines TRENTo entropy-density images with multiplicity and eccentricities to regress the Woods-Saxon radius and diffuseness of uranium nuclei.

Physics AI/MLstrong

Intrinsic Information Rates of Summaries in Simulation-Based Inference

Vansh Bansal, Pradip Sharma Poudel, James G. Scott

Method and result. Theory shows that the required mutual-information budget grows as posteriors concentrate and that, under common support, stochastic summaries strictly outperform deterministic ones below the exact-preservation budget. A finite conjugate example and four simulator benchmarks support the rate-matched comparison.

HEP/nuclear use case. HEP and heavy-ion SBI pipelines often compress high-dimensional events before posterior estimation. Rate-matched evaluation could expose whether an encoder architecture is genuinely better or merely passes more information.

Critical assessment. The strongest transferable ML-method paper today: it combines nontrivial theory with controlled and simulator-based validation. Practical gains on large collider events, systematic uncertainties, and calibration-sensitive downstream observables remain to be shown.

Abstract

The paper treats learned summaries in simulation-based inference as lossy channels and derives rate-distortion bounds for posterior preservation.

Physics AI/MLstrong

Performance of Neural Operator Baselines on 'The Well' Dataset

Yash Pincha, Andy Dimnaku, Andrew Chiang, Adarsh Ganeshram, Valentin Duruisseaux, Anima Anandkumar

Method and result. Using only one input timestep, tuning lowers validation RMSE below the published FNO/TFNO baselines on 15 of 17 tasks, sometimes by orders of magnitude. The central result is evaluative: lightly tuned illustrative baselines should not be treated as architectural limits.

HEP/nuclear use case. Directly relevant to hydrodynamic and transport-surrogate claims in nuclear physics, where weak baselines can manufacture apparent architecture gains.

Critical assessment. A valuable corrective with clear evidence, though it is not a comprehensive architecture study and is produced by authors overlapping the TFNO ecosystem. The lesson is about benchmark hygiene, not proof that TFNO is generally superior.

Abstract

A modest hyperparameter search for a Tucker-factorized Fourier neural operator dramatically improves published The Well baselines under the same wall-clock limit.

LLMs Can Tackle High Energy Theoretical Physics, yet Gaps Remain: Part I, Quantum Field Theory

Tong Liu, Bohan Li, Gongrui Cheng, Zixiong Yu, Yulan He, Volker Tresp

Method and result. Several frontier models exceed 95% final-answer accuracy, while error analysis still finds calculation, convention, and instruction-following failures. The paper also measures whether the displayed derivation actually supports the answer and finds nontrivial unfaithfulness.

HEP/nuclear use case. The benchmark is relevant to assisted analytic work, derivation checking, and training-domain-specific evaluators for HEP theory.

Critical assessment. Broad model coverage and some expert validation make this useful, but the headline should not be read as research autonomy. There are only 75 closed-form tasks; correctness-judge agreement with experts is 98% on a 60-response sample, whereas faithfulness agreement is only 73%, and contamination/memorization is not directly ruled out.

Abstract

QFT-Bench evaluates 39 language models on 75 graduate-to-research-level closed-form QFT problems using symbolic checks and LLM judges.

Weak or overclaimed papers worth caution

Do not treat The Well's illustrative neural-operator baselines as architectural benchmarks

A short TFNO tuning sweep beats the published FNO/TFNO numbers on 15 of 17 tasks under the same runtime cap. Any physics-surrogate paper claiming superiority against those untuned numbers needs a stronger comparison.

Do not equate QFT-Bench accuracy with trustworthy autonomous theory

The benchmark is useful, but 75 closed-form questions are a narrow slice of research. Expert agreement for the faithfulness judge is only 73% on 60 sampled responses, and the study does not directly eliminate training contamination.
