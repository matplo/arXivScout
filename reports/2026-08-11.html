Heavy-Ion Collision Papers

Theory

Improved Regge Kinematics and All-Path-Length Corrections to Momentum Broadening in the Quark-Gluon Plasma

strong

Authors: Dario van den Berg, Isobel Kolbe. Total authors: 2. arXiv: 2608.08894.

Summary: The paper extends first-order opacity GLV momentum broadening by relaxing large-separation/large-formation-time assumptions and by adding improved sub-Regge kinematics. The key claim is that these corrections substantially affect the broadening distribution and qhat, especially for short paths and high exchanged momentum.

Why it matters: Short path lengths are central to small-system and edge-of-medium jet-quenching interpretations. A controlled correction to standard GLV assumptions is more useful than another parameter tune.

Critical assessment: Strong within its scope. It is a compact proceedings-length contribution, so the next value step is implementation in realistic jet-quenching phenomenology, but the physics question is well targeted.

Phenomenology

Prospects for early heavy-quark measurements at the EIC

strong

Authors: Allencris John Rubesh Rajan, Laboni Manna. Total authors: 2. arXiv: 2608.09608.

Summary: This parton-level study estimates inclusive heavy-quark-pair and double-charm-pair electroproduction in early EIC ep and eA running. It maps expected yields and kinematic coverage in Q2, x_Bj, heavy-quark pT, and rapidity, and estimates nuclear modification ratios using nPDFs.

Why it matters: Heavy-quark production at the EIC will constrain nuclear gluon structure, shadowing, anti-shadowing, and EMC-like effects that also enter heavy-ion initial-state baselines.

Critical assessment: Strong as a feasibility baseline. It is not yet a detector-level or higher-order precision study, but the assumptions are appropriately framed and the output is useful for early-science planning.

The role of thermal photons in a magnetized plasma and their significance in heavy ion collisions

mixed

Authors: Zhaolu Ouyang, Xinyang Wang. Total authors: 2. arXiv: 2608.08614.

Summary: The paper studies thermal photon production in magnetized QGP using Landau-level quantization, exploring magnetic-field, chemical-potential, and chiral-chemical-potential effects on yield and polarization.

Why it matters: Electromagnetic probes remain a difficult but important route to early-time QGP information, and magnetic-field-sensitive photon polarization/yield ideas are worth tracking.

Critical assessment: Mixed. The topic is relevant, but this is an 8-page workshop contribution and the abstract's connection to resolving the photon v2 puzzle sounds more prospective than demonstrated.

AI/ML use

Topological analysis of scale-invariant spatial fluctuations in ultrarelativistic heavy-ion collisions

caution

Authors: Salman Khurshid Malik, Ramni Gupta, Fakhar Ul Haider, Balwan Singh. Total authors: 4. arXiv: 2608.08259.

Summary: The paper applies a two-stage topological machine-learning pipeline to simulated critical fluctuations in eta-phi point clouds: Betti-curve features plus TopoPointNet/BDT classification, followed by a particle-level density filter intended to recover intermittency scaling.

Why it matters: If validated on realistic detector-level backgrounds, topological summaries could become useful for QCD critical-point searches and weak-cluster detection.

Critical assessment: Caution. The abstract claims robust recovery of critical scaling, but the evidence appears simulation-defined and the method risks learning generator artifacts or hand-constructed signal structure. Strong claims require mixed-event, detector, centrality, and model-transfer stress tests.

AI/ML Use Cases in Physics

A Gaussian Process framework for constraining the nuclear equation of state from microscopic calculations with correlated uncertainties

strong

Authors: Y. G. Lee, J. Kim, T. Zhao, C. Drischler. Total authors: 4. arXiv: 2608.09678.

Method and result: The paper introduces GPDiff, a JAX-based multivariate Gaussian-process framework with automatic differentiation for EOS predictions and derivatives, trained on microscopic asymmetric-matter calculations with correlated uncertainty.

Likely use case: Directly useful for nuclear-EOS inference and transferable to heavy-ion Bayesian calibration where correlated emulator uncertainties and derivative observables matter.

Critical assessment: Strong. The work addresses a real bottleneck in uncertainty propagation rather than adding ML ornamentation. The main limitation is the initial application range, about up to twice saturation density at zero temperature.

The Living Guide of Machine Learning for Particle Physics

strong

Authors: Claudius Krause, Ramon Winterhalder, Matthew Feickert, Benjamin Nachman. Total authors: 4. arXiv: 2608.09531.

Method and result: This is a community-resource paper: the earlier near-comprehensive HEP-ML Living Review is frozen as an archival record through June 1, 2026, and replaced by a curated, annotated Living Guide.

Likely use case: Highly useful for onboarding and for avoiding reinvention in collider ML, including heavy-ion transfer areas such as simulation, reconstruction, unfolding, inference, and foundation-model workflows.

Critical assessment: Strong as infrastructure, not as a scientific result. Its value depends on continued community curation and clear criteria for representative versus merely popular papers.

Silent coverage failures in rare-event searches and a degeneracy index that predicts them

strong

Author: Davide Pagno. Total authors: 1. arXiv: 2608.09203.

Method and result: The paper studies interval coverage under controlled model misspecification in exact Poisson counting, dark-matter spectra, and neutrinoless-double-beta searches, introducing a Poisson-Fisher degeneracy index to diagnose endpoint vulnerability.

Likely use case: Transfer value is high for HEP/nuclear rare-event limits, low-count resonance searches, and auxiliary-constraint design in analyses where simulator calibration alone can be misleading.

Critical assessment: Strong. It is not an ML paper, but it is directly relevant to AI-assisted inference because it warns that nominal-simulator calibration does not protect against undiagnosed data-generating misspecification.

Weak or Overclaimed Papers Worth Caution

Topological analysis of scale-invariant spatial fluctuations in ultrarelativistic heavy-ion collisions

caution

arXiv: 2608.08259.

The method is interesting, but the abstract's strong language around recovering QCD critical scaling appears to rest on simulated signal extraction. Treat it as a methodological proposal until it survives realistic detector response, mixed backgrounds, centrality variation, and generator-transfer tests.

Alpha Particle Induced Collision Cascade Fusion

caution

Authors: Sandeep Puri, Noah D'Amico, Andrew Gillespie, Ian Jones, Cuikun Lin, Bo Zhao, R. V. Duncan. Total authors: 7. arXiv: 2608.09831.

This is outside heavy-ion-collision physics but worth cautioning about because the claim is unusual: neutron excesses of order 74 and 268 counts after background subtraction from a Po-210/deuterium setup. The abstract reports MCNP agreement, but independent replication, background controls, detector systematics, and activation/neutron-source cross-checks are essential before treating the fusion-rate interpretation as established.
