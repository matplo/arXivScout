Heavy-Ion Collision Papers

Experiment

Three-baryon femtoscopy as an effective 3->3 scattering experiment

strong

Authors: ALICE Collaboration (large collaboration; the arXiv page lists the collaboration rather than a compact author count). arXiv: 2608.05708. Submitted 2026-08-06.

Summary: ALICE measures the three-proton correlation function in pp collisions at sqrt(s)=13.6 TeV and introduces an analysis strategy to isolate the p-p-p contribution after background and detector corrections. The observable is compared to state-of-the-art three-body continuum calculations and is reported to be sensitive to partial-wave structure in the nucleon-nucleon interaction.

Why it matters: Three-body hadronic interactions are hard to constrain directly, especially for unbound and short-lived systems. Femtoscopy provides a realistic path toward precision studies that can later extend into the strangeness sector.

Critical assessment: Strong. The paper combines a real LHC measurement, explicit correction strategy, and theory comparison. The main limitation is interpretive model dependence in femtoscopic source and correlation modeling, so the reported long-range attractive component should be tested against alternative assumptions.

ePIC Early Science Report

mixed/roadmap

Authors: D. Abbott, N. Abdelrahman, S. Abhijit, I. Abualrob, R. B. Achari, and the ePIC Collaboration (large collaboration; compact total not exposed on the arXiv page). arXiv: 2608.05377. Submitted 2026-08-05.

Summary: The ePIC Collaboration lays out early EIC measurements using realistic early-running configurations and detailed Geant4 detector simulation, digitization, and reconstruction. The program includes inclusive, semi-inclusive, exclusive, diffractive, tagging, jet, and heavy-flavor measurements in e+p and e+A collisions.

Why it matters: It is not a heavy-ion result, but it is important for nuclear PDFs, dense gluonic matter, small-x constraints, and cold-nuclear baselines that feed directly into heavy-ion phenomenology.

Critical assessment: Mixed but important. The simulation basis and programmatic scope are serious, but projected early-science reach should be treated as planning evidence rather than established performance.

Phenomenology

QCD Chiral Crossover Line from Lee-Yang Edge Singularities

strong

Authors: Heng-Tong Ding, Swagato Mukherjee, Peter Petreczky, Kai-Fan Ye. Total authors: 4. arXiv: 2608.05752. Submitted 2026-08-06.

Summary: The paper reconstructs the QCD chiral crossover line by mapping lattice-extracted Lee-Yang-zero estimates in the complex baryon chemical potential plane to the universal chiral Lee-Yang edge. As an illustration, it applies the method to recent Wuppertal-Budapest estimates and recovers a curvature consistent with continuum lattice-QCD results at small mu_B.

Why it matters: Beam Energy Scan interpretation depends on reliable finite-mu_B constraints. Lee-Yang singularities offer a principled way to use complex-plane information despite the finite-density sign problem.

Critical assessment: Strong, with a clear scope boundary. The framework is compact and quantitatively coherent, but present conclusions are limited by the available Lee-Yang-zero estimates and temperature window.

Anisotropy effects on heavy quark dynamics in Gribov modified gluon plasma

mixed/useful

Authors: Sumit, Jai Prakash, Santosh Kumar Das, Najmul Haque. Total authors: 4. arXiv: 2506.01922. Revised 2026-08-06; journal reference J. Phys. G 53 (2026) 085101.

Summary: This revised paper studies charm and bottom transport in an anisotropic quark-gluon plasma using a nonperturbative Gribov-resummed framework within a Fokker-Planck treatment. It propagates drag and diffusion coefficients to heavy-quark energy loss and nuclear modification factors with elastic and inelastic interactions.

Why it matters: Heavy-flavor observables remain central QGP transport constraints, and early-time momentum anisotropy is a persistent theory systematic.

Critical assessment: Mixed. The problem is on target and the calculation is useful, but predictive strength depends on weak-anisotropy assumptions, Gribov-medium modeling, and embedding in realistic event-by-event space-time evolution.

AI/ML Use Cases in Physics

Search for resonant production of lepton-enriched semivisible jets in proton-proton collisions at sqrt(s) = 13 TeV

strong

Authors: CMS Collaboration (large collaboration; compact total not exposed on the arXiv page). arXiv: 2608.05323. Submitted 2026-08-05.

Method and result: CMS searches 138 fb^-1 of 13 TeV pp data for lepton-enriched semivisible jets. The analysis uses a graph neural network for jet identification and a fully connected network combining jet- and event-level inputs, excluding mediator masses up to 4.7 TeV in the all-lepton scenario and 1.8-3.5 TeV in the tau-enriched scenario.

Likely HEP/heavy-ion use case: The graph-based jet identification strategy is directly relevant to complex-object tagging, boosted/light-flavor ambiguity, and possibly heavy-ion jet substructure where irregular constituents and background contamination matter.

Critical assessment: Strong as an example of ML embedded in a full collaboration analysis. Transfer to heavy-ion requires careful retraining and systematic treatment under high-occupancy backgrounds, not just reusing the architecture.

NestyNet. I. Physics Functions Are Hard to Fit with Neural Networks: A Framework for Accurate Surrogates and Analytic Derivatives

strong/transferable

Authors: Rodrigo Ibata, Wassim Tenachi, Foivos Diakogiannis, Neil Ibata, Anirudh Shankar. Total authors: 5. arXiv: 2608.05862. Submitted 2026-08-06.

Method and result: NestyNet is a deterministic segmented analytic surrogate with a tailored second-order Levenberg-Marquardt optimizer. It reports large median improvements over standard neural networks on the AI Feynman benchmark for function values and derivatives, and provides analytic gradients, Hessians, Laplacians, and antiderivatives.

Likely HEP/heavy-ion use case: Fast differentiable response surfaces for equations of state, calibration maps, detector corrections, and uncertainty-aware surrogate components where derivatives are scientifically meaningful.

Critical assessment: Strong abstract-level evidence for physics-function fitting. The key test for HEP/NP will be performance on noisy, high-dimensional, discontinuous, or simulator-derived targets, not just clean analytic benchmarks.

Kastor: An efficient fine-tuning strategy for generative emulation of PDE simulations

promising/mixed

Authors: Guillaume Couairon, Alexis Jacq, Yu-Han Wu, Renu Singh, Yana Hasson, Quentin Berthet, Romuald Elie. Total authors: 7. arXiv: 2608.06107. Submitted 2026-08-06.

Method and result: Kastor adapts a deterministic physics foundation model into a generative PDE surrogate using large-stride autoregression, temporal super-resolution, mean-prediction regularization, and spatial-gradient matching. It reports improved forecasting accuracy, spectral consistency, and computational efficiency on The Well benchmark, including a 42.9% average forecasting reduction relative to a Walrus-based reference.

Likely HEP/heavy-ion use case: Long-horizon emulation for hydrodynamics, transport, detector-field simulations, or uncertainty ensembles where stochasticity and spectral fidelity matter.

Critical assessment: Promising but mixed for immediate transfer. The benchmark coverage is broad, but HEP/heavy-ion use would need validation on conservation laws, discontinuities/shocks, domain shifts, and calibrated uncertainty rather than only aggregate forecasting metrics.

Machine Learning is Good for Physics - and Vice Versa

perspective/useful

Authors: Michael Kramer, Tilman Plehn. Total authors: 2. arXiv: 2608.05812. Submitted 2026-08-06.

Method and result: This is a perspective article rather than a new algorithm. It focuses on how scientific AI interacts with fundamental-physics methodology, especially statistical validation and theory-level generalization in searches for new physics.

Likely HEP/heavy-ion use case: Useful framing for analysis validation, ML-assisted model building, simulation-based inference, anomaly searches, and claims discipline in high-dimensional collider data.

Critical assessment: Worth reading as methodological guidance, not as evidence of a new technical advance. Its value is in clarifying standards and failure modes rather than adding a benchmarked tool.
