Daily arXiv Physics Scout

2026-08-31: Heavy-ion and physics AI briefing

Selective Monday scan of arXiv new listings after the weekend release gap. The strongest themes are chemically non-equilibrated QGP modeling, Beam Energy Scan cumulant backgrounds, strangeness system-size systematics, thermal spectral broadening in hadron feed-down, detector-level Bayesian inverse problems, and validation-first AI interfaces for HEP software.

Executive Takeaways

  • The strongest heavy-ion item is Andrew Gordeev's dissertation on light/strange flavor equilibration, because it embeds chemical non-equilibrium into a full multistage Bayesian heavy-ion framework rather than treating it as a qualitative add-on.
  • Net-proton cumulants and the QCD critical-point program get a useful correction study: fRG input propagated through SMASH shows hadronic rescattering can reduce C4/C2 by about 20% near sqrt(sNN)=4.9 GeV while preserving non-monotonic structure.
  • Two strangeness/hadron-production papers are practically useful but should be read with model-dependence in mind: one quantifies the K+/pi+ horn across system size, and one adds thermally broadened rho/omega feed-down to light-hadron spectra.
  • The best AI/ML use case is detector-level Bayesian helicity-amplitude inference with a score-based posterior model; it attacks a real inverse problem with coverage checks and detector-response transfer tests.
  • HEPToolBench and HEPLocalAgent are valuable less for autonomy claims than for their core lesson: typed intermediate representations plus deterministic validators are much more reliable than asking language models to emit raw HEP-tool syntax.
  • Several AI papers are scientifically useful precisely because they show limits: HIBEAM vertexing finds classical methods competitive with GNN chains, and noisy quantum-kernel studies remain far from deployment-level reconstruction claims.

Heavy-Ion Collision Papers

Theory

Constraining Light and Strange Flavor Equilibration in Relativistic Heavy-Ion Collisions

strong

Andrew Gordeev (1 author) | arXiv:2608.27687 | Theory / phenomenology

This dissertation builds a dynamical framework in which light and strange quark fugacities modify both the QGP equation of state and particlization. It is implemented in a multistage model with fluctuating initial conditions, viscous hydrodynamics, particlization, hadronic transport, and Gaussian-process Bayesian inference against RHIC Au+Au data.

Why it matters: Chemical equilibration is often assumed rather than constrained. The reported preference for initially undersaturated strangeness, with slower equilibration than light flavors, is directly relevant to strangeness yields, electromagnetic probes, and hydrodynamic initialization.

Critical assessment: Strong and highly relevant because the physics question is central and the modeling is integrated into realistic inference. The caution is that equilibration times remain weakly constrained, and dissertation-scale results need careful separation between robust posterior features and model-prior structure.

Hadronic rescattering effects on net-proton cumulants from functional renormalization group calculations

strong

Qianru Lin, Shi Yin, Jianing Li, Hannah Elfner, Fabian Rennecke, Long-Gang Pang, Jan M. Pawlowski (7 authors) | arXiv:2608.28299 | Theory

The authors construct event-by-event proton and antiproton distributions from fRG cumulants using maximum entropy, then propagate them through SMASH in a simplified spherical hadronic evolution. They examine BES energies from sqrt(sNN)=3.0 to 7.7 GeV and find rescattering can substantially suppress higher-order cumulant ratios.

Why it matters: Critical-point searches hinge on separating genuine critical fluctuation signals from late-stage hadronic backgrounds. A roughly 20% reduction in C4/C2 near 4.9 GeV is large enough to matter for interpretation.

Critical assessment: Strong as a controlled correction study with a clear target observable. The simplified geometry means it should not be overread as a direct experimental correction, but the direction and scale of the effect are important.

Experiment

The role of strangeness and baryon enhancement in heavy-quark hadronisation from pp to Pb-Pb collisions with ALICE

mixed

Fabrizio Chinu for the ALICE Collaboration | arXiv:2608.28297 | Experiment

This short SQM2026 proceeding summarizes ALICE charm-meson and charm-baryon measurements, including Lambda_c+/D0 in pp at 13.6 TeV, Xi_c0 RAA in Pb-Pb at 5.02 TeV, Ds+/D+ across pp to Pb-Pb, and Ds1(2536)+/Ds+.

Why it matters: Heavy-flavor baryon/strangeness enhancement remains one of the key handles on hadronization in dense and high-multiplicity QCD environments.

Critical assessment: Useful as an experimental status snapshot, but scientifically limited as a four-page proceedings contribution. The underlying ALICE measurements are the real payload; this is a pointer rather than a full analysis paper.

Phenomenology

System-size dependence of strangeness production from p+p to Pb+Pb: quantitative tests of the K+/pi+ horn

strong

Neeraj, Amal Sarkar (2 authors) | arXiv:2608.28189 | Phenomenology

The paper confronts SMASH, PHSD, core-corona, canonical suppression, and SMES descriptions with NA61/SHINE, NA49, and STAR K+/pi+ data across a broad system-size ladder. It reports that no single framework describes the full range, with transport models failing to generate the horn and a 3.7 sigma double-ratio step between light and heavy systems.

Why it matters: The system-size dependence of strangeness production is a sensitive discriminator between geometry, canonical conservation, and genuine thermodynamic equilibration scenarios.

Critical assessment: Strong because it compares multiple model classes using explicit chi-square tests and uses cancellation-friendly double ratios. The main caveat is reliance on preliminary Xe+La inputs for part of the discrimination.

Effect of Thermal Broadening on Light Hadron Production in Heavy-Ion Collisions

mixed

Q'inich Coc, Oscar Garcia-Montero, Aleksas Mazeliauskas (3 authors) | arXiv:2608.28456 | Phenomenology

The authors generalize FastReso to finite-width in-medium spectral functions and test rho/omega thermal broadening in resonance-decay feed-down to pion, kaon, and proton spectra. Simultaneous blast-wave fits to central Pb-Pb and Xe-Xe spectra show extra low-pT pion yield that reduces, but does not solve, the soft-pion discrepancy.

Why it matters: It targets a concrete systematic in thermal-model and particlization-to-hadron-decay pipelines: if resonances are in medium, their spectral widths should not be silently treated as vacuum-like.

Critical assessment: Good, focused phenomenology with practical code-level implications. The result is intentionally modest, and broader resonance content plus dynamical freeze-out effects remain open.

Color screening versus thermal decay as the mechanism of Upsilon suppression in high energy nuclear collisions

mixed / caution

Yida Yang, Baoyi Chen, Jiaxing Zhao, Pengfei Zhuang (4 authors) | arXiv:2608.28277 | Phenomenology

This short paper compares Upsilon suppression at RHIC using a transport equation driven by suppression rates. It argues that sudden color-screening suppression cannot describe ground and excited states simultaneously, whereas continuous thermal decay can reproduce the available measurements.

Why it matters: Separating screening from inelastic thermal parton scattering is central to quarkonium tomography.

Critical assessment: Worth reading, but the phrase "strong evidence" should be treated cautiously. A six-page, three-figure model comparison based on one observable class is suggestive, not a definitive mechanism separation.

AI/ML use

The heavy-ion-adjacent AI/ML papers are listed in the dedicated AI/ML section below because their methods transfer across HEP/nuclear workflows rather than belonging to a single heavy-ion observable.

AI/ML Use Cases in Physics

Direct Bayesian Inference of Helicity Amplitudes from Detector-Level Scattering Data

strong

Bhawani Singh, Harut Avakian, Shohini Bhattacharya, Volker D. Burkert, Latifa Elouadrhiri, Derek I. Glazier, Valery Kubarovsky, Dillon Leahy, Yaohang Li, Richard G. Milner, Yijie Wang (11 authors) | arXiv:2608.27683

A score-based diffusion model, trained on a forward simulator, returns full posteriors for identifiable helicity-amplitude parameters directly from detector-level scattering measurements. The abstract reports empirical coverage, retained correlations, robustness in weakly constrained sectors, and transfer to a realistic detector response absent from training.

Likely use case: Partial-wave analysis, exclusive vector-meson programs, EIC amplitude extraction, and more generally detector-aware inverse problems where unfolding and physics inference should be coupled.

Critical assessment: Strong because it addresses a real statistical bottleneck and evaluates posterior behavior rather than only point-estimate accuracy. The next important test is performance on real data and realistic systematic uncertainty models.

HEPToolBench 1.2: Testing How Reliably Language Models Can Drive Particle Physics Software

strong

Aadarsh Singh, Sudhir K. Vempati (2 authors) | arXiv:2608.28232

This benchmark evaluates 42 model deployments on collider-simulation tasks with deterministic task-specific scorers. Its key result is that schema-mediated typed representations substantially outperform direct generation of native HEP-tool syntax, with pass counts rising from 21/210 to 159/210 in the matched comparison.

Likely use case: Safer LLM interfaces for MadGraph/Pythia/Delphes-style workflows, internal analysis assistants, reproducible workflow builders, and benchmark design for scientific agents.

Critical assessment: Strong and refreshingly careful about scorer asymmetries and runtime caveats. The benchmark measures artifact correctness, not full scientific validity, but that boundary is stated clearly.

HEPLocalAgent 1.0: Running Collider Simulations from Plain-Language Requests on Your Own Computer

mixed

Aadarsh Singh, Sudhir K. Vempati (2 authors) | arXiv:2608.28244

HEPLocalAgent builds bounded collider-simulation workflows from natural language by having an LLM propose a typed workflow that deterministic software validates and serializes. The authors report improved benchmark artifacts after deterministic repair, four of seven approved workflows running to completion, and explicit limitations around MadAnalysis routing and OS-level containment.

Likely use case: Local, approval-gated workflow construction for standard collider simulation chains; useful for lowering friction but not replacing expert review.

Critical assessment: Useful and honest, but not an autonomous science agent. The dropped/defaulted/reinterpreted request cases are exactly where a production HEP assistant would need stronger provenance and user-facing diffing.

Vertex reconstruction for a search for neutron-antineutron conversions with HIBEAM

mixed

Alexander Burgman, Sze Chun Yiu, Yamna Shaikh, David Milstead, Eily Merjy, Lucas Astrand, Kenneth Osterberg, Fredrik Oljemark, Matthias Holl, Valentina Santoro, Andre Nepomuceno, Joshua L. Barrow (12 authors) | arXiv:2608.28314

The paper compares deterministic clustering, trackless projection, and hybrid graph-neural-network reconstruction for foil-plane vertices in a TPC-based neutron-antineutron conversion search. For geometrically simple events, classical reconstruction performs on par with the ML chain for vertex coordinates, while ML can add event-shape information.

Likely use case: A useful benchmark for deciding when GNN reconstruction is scientifically justified in sparse detector topologies, including low-multiplicity nuclear and fixed-target TPC analyses.

Critical assessment: Strong as a cautionary evaluation: ML is not forced into the result when classical methods already solve the main observable. Transfer value is high for detector-method benchmarking.

Localizing Global Discrepancies: Marginal Contributions and Contextual Anomaly Detection

strong

Tommaso Dorigo (1 author) | arXiv:2608.28375

This statistics/ML paper assigns observation-level contributions to global discrepancy tests and connects the construction to influence functions, U-statistics, and MMD witnesses. On the LHC Olympics anomaly benchmark, the efficient pair estimator reproduces the direct MMD witness with very high correlation and essentially identical AUC in the reported setting.

Likely use case: Model-agnostic anomaly localization, data-quality debugging, and event-level explanation of distributional shifts in collider or nuclear event samples.

Critical assessment: Strong and mathematically grounded. The shared-latent toy example is especially useful because it clarifies when context adds real class information beyond single-event features.

How Architecture and Training Affect TPC Representations Across Experiments

strong

Tyler Wheeler, Michelle P. Kuchera, Raghuram Ramanujan, William Sieland, Ryan Krupp, Yassid Ayyad, Daniel Bazin, Connor L. Cross, Hoi Yan Ian Heung, Andrew J. Jones, Ruchi Mahajan, Saiprasad Ravishankar, Pranjal Singh, Benjamin Votaw, Chris Wrede (15 authors) | arXiv:2608.21756 | revised 2026-08-27

This revised paper probes frozen sparse-ResNet and PointNet-style TPC encoders across GADGET II and AT-TPC tasks, including random-weight controls. A notable result is that architecture-induced structure itself can remain informative across detectors, and neither architecture shows a large systematic cross-detector utility loss.

Likely use case: Representation-learning evaluation for TPCs, heavy-ion tracking detectors, and nuclear active-target detectors where reusable embeddings are attractive but must be separated from architecture priors.

Critical assessment: Strong because the random-weight controls directly test a common overclaim in foundation-model work: that downstream performance necessarily proves learned transferable physics structure.

Weak or Overclaimed Papers Worth Caution

Quarkonium mechanism separation claims in arXiv:2608.28277

caution

The Upsilon suppression paper is interesting and relevant, but its abstract-level claim of "strong evidence" that thermal parton scattering dominates over color screening should be read as model-dependent. A short transport comparison to RAA data is valuable, yet mechanism separation in quarkonium generally needs broader observables, uncertainty propagation, feed-down treatment, and cross-energy/system checks.

Autonomy framing around HEPLocalAgent

caution

HEPLocalAgent is worthwhile because it is bounded and approval-gated, but it should not be cited as evidence that LLMs can autonomously run scientifically valid collider simulations. The paper itself says reliable MadAnalysis routing was not demonstrated and OS-level containment was not established.
