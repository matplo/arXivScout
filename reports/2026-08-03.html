Heavy-Ion Collision Papers

Experiment

Recent STAR Measurements from the RHIC Beam Energy Scan II

strong

Ashish Pandav for the STAR Collaboration | arXiv:2607.29616 | Nuclear Experiment

Summary: This SQM 2026 proceedings contribution summarizes recent STAR BES-II results from high-statistics collider and fixed-target data over sqrt(sNN)=3.0--27 GeV, plus 200 GeV isobar results. The covered observables include bulk properties, strangeness production, femtoscopy, collectivity, hyperon polarization, and fluctuations sensitive to QCD phase structure.

Why it matters: Even as a proceedings overview, it is a useful map of where BES-II constraints are maturing: fluctuations, polarization, and lower-energy fixed-target coverage are central to the RHIC phase-diagram program.

Critical assessment: Strong relevance and strong experimental provenance, but this is a compact proceedings synthesis rather than a full analysis paper. Treat it as a high-value pointer to STAR's current BES-II result set, not as a standalone precision interpretation.

Theory

Weak Bose-Einstein condensation in a rigidly rotating magnetized charged Bose gas

mixed

E. Siri, N. Sadooghi | arXiv:2607.29297 | High Energy Physics - Phenomenology; Nuclear Theory

Summary: The authors analyze a noninteracting charged Bose gas in strong magnetic field and rigid rotation using finite-temperature field theory and generalized Fock-Schwinger methods. They find that rotation does not qualitatively alter weak BEC induced by Landau quantization, but can modify magnetic response, with stronger effects under QGP-like conditions than neutron-star-matter-like conditions.

Why it matters: Rotation and magnetic fields are recurring ingredients in heavy-ion spin/polarization and early-time QGP discussions, so a controlled thermodynamic calculation can be useful as a limiting case.

Critical assessment: Scientifically coherent but idealized. The noninteracting gas, lowest-Landau-level, and rigid-rotation assumptions make the connection to real heavy-ion observables indirect; I would not treat the QGP implications as phenomenologically established without dynamical and interacting extensions.

AI/ML Use Cases In Physics

Learning transferable event representations for charmed baryon physics at BESIII

strong

Kaixuan Huang, Yangu Li, Junpeng Zhao, Peilian Li, Peirong Li, Xiaorui Lyu, Yunxuan Song, Shengsen Sun, Yangheng Zheng | arXiv:2607.29088 | Data Analysis, Statistics and Probability; HEP Experiment

Method and result: A Particle Transformer is pretrained on BESIII Monte Carlo for event classification and momentum-direction regression, then fine-tuned across Lambda_c+ benchmark channels. The abstract reports 97.0% background rejection at 90.0% signal efficiency and performance comparable to or better than training from scratch, especially in low-statistics regimes.

Likely use case: This is directly transferable to HEP workflows with many related decay modes or channels. For heavy-ion, the lesson is not the charmed-baryon target itself but the reusable event-representation strategy for low-statistics selections, rare probes, and detector-specific reconstruction tasks.

Critical assessment: Strong by today's standard because it uses a domain-specific architecture, multiple downstream benchmarks, and a realistic low-statistics motivation. Remaining questions are domain shift from simulation to data and whether systematic uncertainties are improved or merely classifier metrics.

Discovery Sensitivity for a Counting Experiment with Background Uncertainty

strong

Enzo Canonero, Glen Cowan | arXiv:2607.29436 | Data Analysis, Statistics and Probability

Method and result: The paper derives approximate median discovery-significance formulae for Poisson counting experiments with known or control-measurement-constrained backgrounds. It combines profile-likelihood Asimov approximations with Barndorff-Nielsen higher-order corrections and compares against Monte Carlo and common significance formulae.

Likely use case: Useful for HEP and nuclear searches where expected counts are small, control regions are finite, and cut optimization depends sensitively on background uncertainty. It is especially relevant to rare probes, exotic-object searches, and trigger/selection studies with sparse yields.

Critical assessment: Strong and practical. It is not AI/ML per se, but it is high-value analysis methodology for ML-assisted cut optimization or search design, where naive asymptotics can mis-rank selections in low-yield regimes.

Freeze, Then Select: Structured Field Adapters and Stability-Validated Weak Selection for PDE Discovery from Sparse Observations

strong

Juncheng Zhong, Chenghuang Shen, Jianfeng Liu, Zhengdong Xiao, Longjiu Luo, Qianrong Wang, Wenjun Xu, Wenlian Lu | arXiv:2607.29665 | Machine Learning; Numerical Analysis

Method and result: The method first reconstructs a continuous field with a structured adapter, freezes it, then performs stability-validated weak-form term selection. It reports the best exact-support recovery across six sparse MDBench regimes, with clear gains on challenging Kuramoto-Sivashinsky dynamics, and extends to genetic-programming-generated nonlinear diffusion expressions.

Likely use case: Transfer value is credible for extracting effective equations or diagnosing surrogate dynamics from sparse hydrodynamic, transport, or detector-field simulations. It could also help test whether learned heavy-ion emulator fields encode physically meaningful differential structure.

Critical assessment: Strong transferable methodology, provided users keep the benchmark-to-physics gap in mind. The key unresolved issue for heavy-ion use is performance on relativistic, noisy, multi-scale fields with conservation laws and realistic uncertainty propagation.

A Neural Operator Closure for Landau Damping in Electrostatic Plasma

mixed

Samuel Burles, Enrico Camporeale, Oreste Pezzi | arXiv:2607.29364 | Plasma Physics

Method and result: A Fourier Neural Operator is trained online inside a differentiable fluid solver to learn a non-Markovian heat-flux closure for one-dimensional linear and nonlinear Landau damping. The closure uses a trailing window of resolved moment history and remains stable in independent fluid simulations.

Likely use case: The transfer path is to kinetic-to-fluid closure problems, including heavy-ion transport reductions, hydrodynamic closure tests, and surrogate modeling of memory effects. The idea of training on closed-loop trajectories rather than isolated snapshots is particularly relevant.

Critical assessment: Promising but mixed for HEP transfer. The validation is careful for 1D plasma damping, but the authors themselves note scheme and training-data dependence of the learned flux; heavy-ion use would need stress tests under relativistic, expanding, non-Abelian, and uncertainty-aware conditions.

The fundamental limit of jet tagging: Beyond top jets

mixed

Sarah Koller, Humberto Reyes-Gonzalez | arXiv:2607.29508 | HEP Phenomenology

Method and result: This ACAT proceedings note extends prior likelihood-ratio comparisons between modern taggers and estimated optimal classifiers from boosted top jets to boosted W, Z, and H->gg jets. It uses transformer-based generative models with known probability densities and finds that the performance gap is strongly jet dependent.

Likely use case: For HEP and heavy-ion jet ML, the useful idea is benchmark discipline: compare taggers to density-ratio or likelihood-ratio estimates rather than only to other taggers. Heavy-ion transfer would be natural for quenched-jet tagging, quark/gluon studies, or medium-response classification, if the generative model remains realistic under background and detector effects.

Critical assessment: Conceptually strong but evidence-limited because this is a proceedings contribution. The result is best read as a benchmark design direction, not a final statement about universal jet-tagger optimality.

Weak Or Overclaimed Papers Worth Caution

MOT-SR: Multi-Objective Tool-Augmented Scientific Equation Discovery with Large Language Models

caution

Boxiao Wang, Runxiang Wang, Kai Li, Chongming Li, Zhiwei Chen, Yifan Zhang, Jian Cheng | arXiv:2607.29561 | Machine Learning; AI

Why caution: The framework is interesting: tool-augmented LLM modules propose symbolic-regression strategies and equations while a Pareto module balances accuracy, complexity, and generalization. The paper reports gains on 40 standard tasks and an EMRI orbital-modeling validation case, but the HEP/heavy-ion transfer path is indirect.

Critical assessment: Worth watching, not yet worth adopting uncritically. For nuclear or heavy-ion workflows, the central tests would be baseline fairness against non-LLM symbolic regression, robustness to prompt/tool choices, reproducibility of the closed-loop search, uncertainty propagation, and behavior on noisy simulation or experimental observables.
