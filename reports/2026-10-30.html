Daily arXiv Physics Scout

Briefing for 30 October 2026

Thermal-QCD fluctuation tests, electromagnetic directed flow, neutron-skin geometry, antimatter femtoscopy, detector reconstruction, amplitude surrogates, and particle-native neural operators.

Executive takeaways

  • Highest-confidence physics contribution: the net-charge fluctuation paper turns a theoretically clean thermal-QCD discriminator into a more experimentally controllable program, although the crucial fourth-order implementation remains future work.
  • The directed-flow study is technically stronger than a simple fit: its electromagnetic hydrodynamics is benchmarked against semi-analytic solutions and yields species-level predictions. Its conductivity inference remains model dependent because effective conductivity, relaxation time, initialization, and freeze-out corrections are entangled.
  • The Ca/Ti comparison identifies second-moment flow-size correlations as a promising neutron-skin observable, but a single AMPT study cannot establish extraction robustness.
  • For direct HEP ML, both selected studies clear the usefulness bar: transformer reconstruction reports accuracy and throughput against a clustering baseline, while the ttH surrogate enables an otherwise difficult two-loop phase-space calculation.
  • NEOPIC is the best neighboring-domain transfer candidate: it combines a particle-native operator, mathematical error control, multi-benchmark validation, and modular replacement of only the field solve.
  • The GDR phase-lock proposal deserves caution. The distinctive dipole comes from an assumed correlated phase and idealized emission-time selection, not yet from a demonstrated dynamical or detector-realistic prediction.

Heavy-ion collision papers

Theory

Mixed / useful observable proposal · Theory

Disentangling the neutron skin of 48Ca from nuclear radius effects through 48Ca+48Ca and 48Ti+48Ti flow observables

Authors: Abhisek Saha (1 author).

Summary: AMPT calculations compare Ca-Ca and Ti-Ti systems with controlled Woods-Saxon variations to separate neutron-skin geometry from overall nuclear size. Second-moment correlations between flow and mean transverse momentum are substantially more sensitive than first moments.

Why it matters: The radius-matched comparison and focus on shape-size correlators address a genuine degeneracy that limits neutron-skin constraints from relativistic collisions.

Assessment: The controlled geometry variations are well motivated, but the inference is currently tied to one transport model and assumed density profiles. Credible extraction will require multi-model response studies, experimental feasibility, and propagation of nuclear-structure and centrality uncertainties.

Abstract-level synopsis

Transport simulations vary the 48Ca neutron skin and nuclear radius independently, comparing Ca-Ca with Ti-Ti at LHC energy. Flow-mean-pT covariance and its normalized correlation emerge as the most sensitive candidate observables.

Caution / speculative benchmark · Theory

Quantum Correlations Between Two Giant Dipole Resonances in Ultra-Peripheral Collisions

Authors: Albandry Alshammri, Ashik Ikbal Sheikh, Declan Keane, Prithwish Tribedy, Gang Wang, Zhangbu Xu, Jie Zhao (7 authors).

Summary: A modified neutron-decay event generator compares recoil, independent polarized GDR decays, and an assumed correlated-phase scenario in mutual Coulomb excitation. The phase-locked benchmark produces a first harmonic for neutron pairs selected to have nearly simultaneous emission.

Why it matters: Forward-neutron azimuthal correlations are experimentally accessible, and distinguishing a dipole from the conventional quadrupole baseline would be interesting nuclear quantum dynamics.

Assessment: The paper usefully exposes a possible observable, but the phase-lock mechanism is posited rather than dynamically derived. The large signal relies on an emission-time selection that is not directly measurable; after time averaging it is strongly reduced. The authors themselves call for time-inclusive matched baselines and detector-response studies, so entanglement language is premature.

Abstract-level synopsis

Three mechanisms for two-neutron azimuthal correlations in mutual GDR excitation are implemented in a Monte Carlo model. An assumed correlated phase yields a pronounced dipole only for small relative emission times, while averaging suppresses it.

Experiment

Tentative / proceedings · Experiment

Search for anti-4Li from antiproton + anti-3He femtoscopy in Pb-Pb collisions at 5.36 TeV with ALICE

Authors: Giorgio Alberto Lucia (on behalf of the ALICE Collaboration) (ALICE collaboration; individual author count not exposed on the arXiv landing page).

Summary: ALICE searches for anti-4Li through its antiproton plus anti-3He decay channel using femtoscopic correlations and a dedicated correlated/uncorrelated combinatorial-background subtraction.

Why it matters: The anti-4Li to anti-4He yield ratio can discriminate high-energy nucleosynthesis models because resonant 4Li states and spin degeneracy should enhance production relative to compact 4He.

Assessment: The observable and background strategy are worthwhile, but this is a proceedings contribution and the abstract does not report a yield, significance, limit, or systematic budget. It is important to watch, not yet a quantitatively assessable result from the public summary.

Abstract-level synopsis

A femtoscopic search reconstructs anti-4Li through antiproton and anti-3He pairs in Pb-Pb collisions. The proposed yield comparison with anti-4He is intended to test nucleosynthesis models.

Phenomenology

Strong / prospective · Phenomenology

Testing thermal equilibrium in heavy-ion collisions with electric-charge fluctuations

Authors: Peter Christiansen (1 author).

Summary: Connects continuum-extrapolated lattice-QCD charge susceptibilities to experimentally controllable net-charge observables. The second cumulant is rewritten using the charge-balance function and charged-particle density so efficiency and acceptance extrapolations can be studied directly.

Why it matters: It lays out a realistic path toward testing whether equilibrium thermal QCD describes central heavy-ion data, especially through the theoretically discriminating fourth-to-second susceptibility ratio.

Assessment: Scientifically sharp and unusually attentive to experimental acceptance. The decisive fourth-order measurement is not delivered here; this is a strategy paper whose payoff depends on extending the demonstrated second-order treatment and controlling global conservation, volume fluctuations, and nonthermal backgrounds.

Abstract-level synopsis

Recent lattice-QCD electric-charge susceptibilities differ strongly from hadron-resonance-gas expectations, motivating measurements of net-charge cumulants. The paper expresses the second cumulant through the balance function and charged-particle density to make acceptance and efficiency effects experimentally tractable.

Strong method / model-dependent inference · Phenomenology

Tracing Electromagnetic Response through Charge Splitting of Directed Flow in the Quark-Gluon Plasma

Authors: Ankit Kumar Panda, Zhu Meng, Long-Gang Pang, Xin-Nian Wang (4 authors).

Summary: A unified 3+1D viscous-hydrodynamic calculation includes electromagnetic source terms and an electromagnetic correction at particlization. It reproduces the opposite midrapidity slopes seen at RHIC and the LHC by using beam-energy-dependent effective conductivity and predicts species-dependent charge splitting.

Why it matters: Charge-odd directed flow is one of the few potential handles on the short-lived electromagnetic response of the QGP, and the identified-hadron predictions are directly testable.

Assessment: The numerical electromagnetic module is benchmarked against semi-analytic resistive-MHD solutions, which is a real strength. Extraction of conductivity is not yet unique: the model uses constant effective conductivity, links it to a relaxation time, varies it across beam energies, and remains sensitive to initialization time and particlization assumptions.

Abstract-level synopsis

The observed beam-energy change in charge splitting of directed flow is modeled with viscous hydrodynamics coupled to electromagnetic fields and an electromagnetic Cooper-Frye correction. Electric and magnetic distortions compete, with their balance governed by effective conductivity; identified-particle predictions are provided.

AI/ML use cases in physics

Strong simulation study · HEP AI/ML

End-to-end Particle Reconstruction with Transformers in High-Granularity Calorimeters

Authors: Qiming Bao, Haotian Bi, Benedikt Maier, Alex Tapper, Lauri Laatu (5 authors).

Method and result: Three transformer-based approaches, including query set prediction, diffusion, and flow matching, reconstruct particles directly from raw high-granularity calorimeter hits. QueryDETR and FlowReco recover about 96.6% of particles versus 87.2% for the CLUE baseline, while reaching roughly 12,000 events per second on the tested A100 setup.

Likely use: This directly targets overlapping showers and throughput constraints expected for HL-LHC calorimetry; the query and flow formulations could also transfer to dense heavy-ion calorimeter environments.

Assessment: The comparison reports five-seed uncertainties, energy/angular resolution, and throughput, making it more convincing than an architecture demo. The simulated data use ideal detector response, local regions rather than full events, 1-10 unusually energetic particles, and a single traditional baseline; pileup, noise, calibration, domain shift, and end-to-end experiment integration remain open.

Abstract-level synopsis

Transformer models based on learned queries, diffusion, and flow matching reconstruct particles from simulated high-granularity calorimeter hits. They outperform the evaluated clustering baseline on recovery and energy resolution, with query and flow models retaining competitive throughput.

Strong physics-enabled ML · HEP AI/ML

Two-loop QCD contribution to quark-initiated top-antitop-Higgs production at the LHC

Authors: Xiang Chen, Massimiliano Grazzini, Stefan Kallweit (3 authors).

Method and result: The first direct generalized-leading-colour calculation of the quark-initiated two-loop ttH virtual contribution uses an ML surrogate trained on about 10,000 phase-space points. Validation at one loop is better than 1% over almost all phase space, typically at the per-mille level, and the two-loop result agrees with existing dynamical approximations within uncertainties.

Likely use: This is substantive ML infrastructure for a genuinely hard perturbative-QCD calculation, not decorative classification. Similar surrogate strategies can make expensive amplitude components usable in precision predictions and event-level integration.

Assessment: Strong because the ML component is embedded in, and validated against, a first-principles calculation. Scope is still limited to the quark-antiquark channel and generalized leading colour; the two-loop leading-colour uncertainty is sizable, so this is an important step toward full NNLO rather than the final prediction.

Abstract-level synopsis

Numerical multiloop methods and a learned phase-space surrogate enable a direct two-loop virtual ttH calculation in a leading-colour approximation. The result is consistent with prior approximate treatments and provides a route to practical cross-section integration.

Strong / highly transferable · Transferable physics AI/ML

NEOPIC: A Neural Operator Framework for Particle-based Kinetic Plasma Simulations

Authors: Sriramkrishnan Muralikrishnan, Chelsea Maria John, Jeffrey Kelling, Michael Bussmann, Robert Speck, Paul Gibbon (6 authors).

Method and result: NEOPIC learns only the particle-to-field operator, acting directly on the empirical particle measure without a mesh or deposition step. The paper proves representation equivalence and separates sampling, learning, and time-stepping errors; multi-application 2D/3D benchmarks include Landau damping, two-stream and bump-on-tail instabilities, cyclotron dynamics, and a Penning trap.

Likely use: The modular field-solver design, conservation diagnostics, explicit error analysis, and linear particle-count scaling are credible ingredients for learned accelerators in kinetic transport workflows. Transfer to HEP or nuclear transport would require adapting the operator and invariants, but the architectural idea is unusually relevant.

Assessment: The strongest transferable ML paper today: extensive theory, diverse benchmarks, code, and stability beyond the training window. It is not a heavy-ion transport result, and extrapolation to relativistic non-Abelian or collision-rich dynamics is speculative; production advantage must also be judged against optimized solvers in the target regime.

Abstract-level synopsis

A nonuniform Fourier neural operator maps particle measures directly to fields and can replace the field solve in several particle-based kinetic methods. The work supplies convergence and complexity analysis plus multi-physics numerical validation in two and three dimensions.

Weak or overclaimed papers worth caution

Quantum Correlations Between Two Giant Dipole Resonances in Ultra-Peripheral Collisions (2610.38324): the proposed neutron-azimuth signal is worth experimental study, but the phase locking is assumed and the strongest effect requires an unobservable emission-time cut. Treat the entanglement interpretation as a hypothesis benchmark until time-inclusive, detector-level predictions and a microscopic coherence mechanism are established.

ALICE anti-4Li proceedings (2610.38549): not weak science, but the public abstract gives no quantitative result. It should not be cited as evidence for or against a nucleosynthesis model until yields, uncertainties, or limits are available.
