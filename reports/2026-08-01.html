Heavy-Ion Collision Papers

Experiment

Probing (Hyper)Nuclei Wave Functions and Production Mechanisms in sqrt(sNN)=200 GeV Isobar Collisions at RHIC

strong

STAR Collaboration | arXiv:2607.28113 | covered in the July 31 report

Summary: STAR reports centrality-dependent hypernuclei and light-nuclei production in 200 GeV Ru+Ru and Zr+Zr collisions. The ratios challenge simple thermal expectations and favor coalescence calculations with more realistic non-Gaussian wave functions.

Why it matters: It uses heavy-ion data as a probe of hypertriton structure and hyperon-nucleon interactions, giving the result unusually direct physics leverage.

Critical assessment: Strong, with the usual coalescence-model caveat. It remains the top heavy-ion experimental item in the latest available arXiv batch.

Phenomenology / AI/ML Use

Unbiased Data-Driven Determination of the Nuclear Dipole Amplitude in the Color Glass Condensate

strong

Si-Wei Dai, Haowu Duan, Long-Gang Pang, Guang-You Qin, Shu-Yi Wei, Han-Zhong Zhang, Wenbin Zhao (7 authors) | arXiv:2607.27603 | covered in the July 31 report

Summary: The paper embeds collinearly improved BK evolution and positivity constraints into a neural extraction of the Pb dipole amplitude from forward-hadron and coherent J/psi photoproduction data.

Why it matters: This is a serious example of ML used as constrained inference for small-x nuclear structure, with possible impact on pA baselines and EIC/heavy-ion phenomenology.

Critical assessment: Strong, but the term "unbiased" should be read as "less parametrized" rather than assumption-free.

AI/ML Use

CLVisc Agent for autonomous relativistic hydrodynamics studies

mixed

Qi Wang, Long-Gang Pang, Shi Pu, Xin-Nian Wang (4 authors) | arXiv:2607.27822 | covered in the July 31 report

Summary: The authors demonstrate an LLM-agent workflow that operates CLVisc, runs hydrodynamic scans, compares results, and generates figures for eta/s and O+O case studies.

Why it matters: It points toward reproducible automation for hydrodynamic simulation campaigns.

Critical assessment: Mixed. It is a useful workflow prototype, but the scientific value depends on reliability, uncertainty handling, and expert validation under less curated conditions.

AI/ML Use Cases in Physics

DoTime: A Synthetic Benchmark Generator for Interventional and Counterfactual Time Series

mixed

Dennis Thumm, Billy Tim Anthony, Ying Chen (3 authors) | arXiv:2607.27263 | covered in the July 31 report

Method and result: DoTime creates temporal structural causal model benchmarks with interventions, counterfactuals, regime switches, nonstationarity, and exact ground truth.

Likely use case: Transfer value is indirect but credible for detector operations, run-quality monitoring, and causal modeling of time-dependent experimental conditions.

Critical assessment: Mixed-positive as a benchmark. Its utility for HEP or nuclear physics will depend on building domain-realistic time-series generators rather than relying on generic synthetic suites.

Learning to Trace Seiberg Dualities

mixed

Jonathan J. Heckman, Shani Meynet, Alessandro Mininno, Gary Shiu (4 authors) | arXiv:2607.28628 | covered in the July 31 report

Method and result: The paper trains transformer and MLP strategies, aided by pathfinder algorithms, to trace dualities in supersymmetric quiver gauge theories.

Likely use case: Most relevant as a theoretical-physics benchmark for graph search and symbolic/structural reasoning, not as a near-term heavy-ion analysis tool.

Critical assessment: Interesting but specialized. Transfer to HEP workflows is plausible for theory search problems, but not yet demonstrated for experimental or phenomenological heavy-ion tasks.
